| `v2_probe.py` | User-run live verification CLI for the V2 order mapping and the transfer unit (~1c exposure) — its closing reduce-only bid is priced at the top of the market's own grid rather than at the rollback builder's $0.62 loss floor, so that floor can no longer cause a FAIL unrelated to the mapping (a book with nothing resting at or below the top of grid still can), and its verdict is judged on one re-read when the read after the close is not exactly 0; the exchange's HTTP 409 kill response to a fill-or-kill (`trader._is_fok_kill`, the live path's one definition) is read as a kill — PASS on the unfillable ask, NEUTRAL on the NO buy, when the account reads flat (a first read that is not exactly 0 is re-read once); a non-object 2xx order body, in EITHER submission-response reader (DR-58), and an object whose fill counts are unreadable in the NO-buy step (`_step_no_mapping`, DR-60), are both a FAIL that still re-reads the position and names what to flatten through the one shared `_recheck_and_report_position`, never a traceback out of the fill readers (`_step_unfillable_ask`'s own unreadable-fill-counts branch is a recorded residual that still FAILs with no post-submission read); `_step_no_mapping` classifies READABLE fill counts into THREE outcomes — complete fill, true kill, fill-or-kill invariant violation (partial / over-fill / stale remainder) — so a partial fill FAILs naming the counts and re-reading the account instead of reporting a clean kill with the account "still flat" (DR-20), which is how `_step_unfillable_ask` always read one, and both steps judge on BOTH counts, deliberately stricter than `trader._v2_fill_status`'s `fill_count`-only contract; `--dest-shard` equal to the source shard is refused with a NEUTRAL at the top of the transfer step, before any transfer I/O, so it can no longer POST a net-zero self-transfer and then report a false in-flight FAIL (DR-22); refuses to start on any `config.ORDER_API_VERSION` but `"v2"` (`config.order_api_version_error`, exit 2 before the banner, logging or any request), the FAIL lines of `--step no-mapping` and `--step yes-close` that doubt the V2 path after a submission on an account it verified flat (a buy that opened the wrong side, an impossible fill-or-kill reply, a close or sale that did not leave the ticker flat) print `v2_probe._REMEDY` (stop trading, and flatten any position on the probed ticker by hand in the Kalshi UI, since there is no other order path), while `main()`'s closing line depends on the outcome — after an order step's FAIL it says to stop trading and to act only on the position warnings printed above, never by itself to flatten (the ticker may carry the bot's own position); after a `--step transfer` FAIL, to stop trading and check each shard's balance; after a NEUTRAL, that nothing needs doing; its informational fee check (`_report_fee`, no verdict) prints the exchange's `average_fee_paid` — the volume-weighted average fee PER CONTRACT by Kalshi's API reference, never to be scaled by the probe's 0.01-contract count — beside the fee model per contract at the order's average fill price (else its limit price), three ways: before rounding (`TAKER_FEE_RATE·p·(1−p)`), `config.fee_leg_exact(1, p)` (one whole contract rounded up to the cent), and rounded as Kalshi rounds an order (`_order_rounded_fee_per_contract`: the order's total fee up to the account's balance precision, `v2_probe.FEE_BALANCE_PRECISIONS` — $0.0001, or $0.01 on some accounts — the excess rebated later). The exchange's figure includes that rounding, so on the probe's tiny order it matches the rounded model, not the unrounded one (the 2026-09-28 probe: $0.0200 per contract on 0.58 and 0.59 fills, $0.0171/$0.0169 before rounding); at a 0.01 count the $0.0001-rounded figure equals `fee_leg_exact(1, p)` at every cent price; `--step yes-close` (`_step_yes_close`) checks the one order shape live selling and live trimming send that no other step does, a `reduce_only` ask that sells a held YES: it buys 0.01 YES (`_yes_buy_body`: the real YES-leg builder with a bid at the top of the grid) and judges that buy as the NO buy is judged, then sells it with the body `trader._build_sale_order_v2` builds (`_yes_close_body`: an immediate-or-cancel ask at `scanner.v2_bottom_of_grid_price`), and PASSes only when the account then reads exactly flat (re-read once if the first read is not 0) AND the sale's reply reports 0.01 filled with nothing remaining (`_sale_reply_problem`: live selling and live trimming take a sale's count from the reply's `fill_count` when they can read one); the closing lines after a PASS, a NEUTRAL and an order step's FAIL (not a `--step transfer` FAIL's) now say `--step yes-close` has to PASS before live selling or rebalancing is turned on (rebalancing sells through Kelly trims, which send the same sale orders); never imported by the pipeline | `main()` |

the very same name twice is refused (`... is given twice`), while a second spelling in another letter case is left out and the first kept, since the live filter ignores case and Kalshi spells a few tags two ways ("Health · COVID" / "Health · Covid") that a page lists both of (`_name_list`); a tag needs no category named, and a tag tied to a category ("Sports · Basketball") is allowed when that category is excluded too, since it excludes nothing more; no source note or one matching `config.LIVE_DEFAULTS_SOURCE_PATTERN` with ASCII digits only, the seed's note only on the seed values, then `LiveSettings`' own rules — `same_title_size_cap`, `rebalance` (`on`/`off`), `min_price_move` (`off` or a plain number of dollars on the 5c grid, `0.1` for 10c), `sell_at` (`off` or a share in (0, 1]) and `sell_min_days` (`off` or a whole number of days, at most six digits) are the five fields that fall back to the saved value — for a file an older version saved, its value in the new form (`_kept_base`), or the seed's with none saved (`_kept`) — so a link that names none of them leaves all five as saved, except that a link carrying the seed's note takes the seed's `rebalance`, `min_price_move`, `sell_at` and `sell_min_days`, so it still proposes exactly the seed; then, before anything is saved, a run's blockers (`_run_blocker`: a server built without a process starter runs nothing; a run started from this checkout's server that is still going blocks both kinds of run — one it started, or one in `LIVE_RUNS_DIR` from before it restarted, with no result yet and its `output.log` still locked by its process (`_own_run`); `run_lock.held()` blocks a real-money run, naming the holder, and calls it possibly hung past `SCHEDULER_JOB_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`), a dry run's need for saved defaults, and, for Confirm and trade, the attention acknowledgement (`_attention`: of the finished real-money runs — each run folder under `LIVE_RUNS_DIR`, read as its run page reads it, and the scheduler's `scheduler_state.json` — the newest that says anything about the account decides; a run that stopped before it could send an order (exit 2, 10, 30 or 50, or an error before its first order) and a dry run neither raise nor clear it; one that exited `EXIT_TRADES_NEED_ATTENTION` (a pair or a sale left for a person) or ended without a clean result (no result, a signal, an error after it began sending orders, a scheduled run with no exit code — stopped at the time limit) makes both trade pages show a red banner and a box to tick, and a trade without it is 409; a clean exit (0 or 40) clears it; and anything unexpected while reading fails closed, with the banner). Only then is the nonce marked used and, for Confirm and save or a Confirm and trade that changes something, `config.save_live_defaults` called — its one call site, in `_App._post_confirm`. A run is started only by `_App._start_run(self, *, settings, dry_run, saved_note)`, the module's one process start, through the `start_process` the `_App` was built with — `subprocess.Popen` from `main()` alone; the default `None` refuses every run, so no test can start `main.py`. It makes `LIVE_RUNS_DIR/<UTC start>-<16 hex id>/`, writes `run.json` before anything starts, locks `output.log` with an flock the run's process inherits through its output (so `_run_alive` tells a run is still going, across a server restart too), and starts `[sys.executable, "-m", "kalshi_betting.main", "--mode", "prod", *config.live_settings_argv(settings), "--result-file", <folder>/result.json]` (plus `--dry-run`) from `config.PROJECT_ROOT` in a new session (`start_new_session=True`: Ctrl-C on the server, or closing its terminal, never reaches a run placing orders); a start that fails is 500, saying whether the defaults were saved; when the process itself could not be started, its `run.json` records why (a folder or first `run.json` that cannot be made leaves nothing behind). The server never stops a run, and at shutdown logs a WARNING for each one it started that is still going. On start (`main()`) it logs a WARNING when the saved file is refused, naming, for one an older version saved, the `/confirm?…` link that saves its values again (ending `do not delete the file`, followed, when the old `categories` listed names, by `They traded only <names>: saved as offered they trade every category, so add an exclude_category= field for each category to leave out`, and when the old `tags` did, by `They limited trading to the tags <names> (a "Category · Tag" limited that category alone): saved as offered they trade every tag, so add an exclude_tag= field for each tag to leave out`), or the index when its other settings are refused too; it opens one page (`_open_start_page`, through `_start_page`): with `--seed` the seed values' confirmation page; otherwise the backtest dashboard when `_dashboard_state` finds the Trade link's id (`_DASHBOARD_MARKER`, `id="flt-trade"`) in its first `DASHBOARD_MARKER_SCAN_BYTES`, else its own index — with an INFO line when there is no dashboard, a WARNING naming the rebuild and `/trade` for a page built before the buttons, and a WARNING with the reason for one it cannot read (the index says the same). When its port is taken (`EADDRINUSE`) it binds nothing and starts nothing — no second server, no `_App`, no run: `_reuse_running_server` asks the listener `GET /checkout` once (`_running_checkout`: no proxy, no redirect followed, `DEFAULTS_SERVER_CHECKOUT_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`, at most `DEFAULTS_SERVER_CHECKOUT_MAX_BYTES` read, never raises), and when the answer names this checkout's resolved `PROJECT_ROOT` and this checkout's code now (`_code_fingerprint()`) it logs (to the terminal only; the running server keeps its log file) "This checkout's defaults server is already running at …/" and opens the page this start asked for, then returns (exit 0); an answer naming another checkout exits 2 naming it (its non-printable characters escaped), one naming this checkout with other code, or none (a server left running across a pull or an edit, whose pages would check and start runs with its older code), exits 2 telling the user to stop it and start it again, and anything else (no answer within the wait, an error or redirect status, not that JSON — one nested too deeply to parse included) exits 2 with the port-in-use error. Every response carries a Content-Security-Policy that allows only the one page script by its hash, forbids framing and lets a form post only to the server; that script arms the page's real buttons (the ids of `_ARMED_BUTTONS` present) only after the page has been visible for `DEFAULTS_SERVER_CONFIRM_ARM_MS` and the mouse moves or a key is pressed (the DoubleClickjacking defence), and disables them for good once the form is sent (`tests/test_defaults_server.py::TestConfirmScript` runs it under node or `jsc`). Every response — a refusal the HTTP layer makes before the application sees the request (501 for another method, 400/414/431/505 for a request it cannot read) included, through `_Handler.send_error`, and all sent as HTTP/1.0, so each has a status line and headers — carries the same six headers (`/checkout`'s type is JSON). It answers one request at a time (`HTTPServer`, so two saves or two starts never interleave), drops a silent connection after `DEFAULTS_SERVER_SOCKET_TIMEOUT_SECONDS`, refuses a request over `DEFAULTS_SERVER_MAX_REQUEST_BYTES` (65,536 bytes, room for hundreds of excluded tags), never serves the dashboard (any script on a page from its own address could press its buttons), and logs to `kalshi_defaults_server.log` (rotating). These checks tell its own pages from other web pages; they do not authenticate local programs. It reads the saved file only through `_current_defaults` and writes it only in `_App._post_confirm` (the AST pin's `_DEFAULTS_READERS`/`_DEFAULTS_WRITERS`, which name a method with its class), and names the file's path only to print it (`_SAVED_FILE_PRINT_ONLY`), reading `config.LIVE_DEFAULTS_FILE`, `config.PROJECT_ROOT`, `config.LIVE_RUNS_DIR` and `config.LIVE_RUN_LOCK_FILE` through the module at call time; imports the standard library, `config` and `run_lock` only, imported by NOTHING (pinned by `tests/test_defaults_server.py::TestIsolation`, which also pins that its one process start is `_App._start_run`'s, that `subprocess.Popen` appears only as `main()`'s `start_process=`, and that it imports only the standard library modules it needs (no `pty`, `multiprocessing`, `asyncio`, `concurrent`, `importlib` or `ctypes`), imports `os` and `subprocess` whole and never renamed, and never names `Popen` bare, so every process start is spelled where the checks read it) | `main()` |
| `live_portfolio.py` | Reporting only, for the live dashboard's Live trading tab: reads the live account with read-only GETs and the bot's trade log, matches the bot's purchases to the Kalshi orders that made them, and replays every fill and payout into a ledger, owner by owner (a bot purchase, or `OTHER_BETS` for everything else, the bot's own sale orders included, which are read as sales by hand). `read_account` reads every fill (live `/portfolio/fills`, then archived `/historical/fills`, the live copy kept when both list a `fill_id`, so a fill Kalshi moves to its archive while they are read is in one of the two), settlement (Kalshi's `revenue` and `value` are whole cents), applied deposit (less its fee) and withdrawal (plus its fee), then the balance and the positions; every request goes through `_get` to `historical._historical_get` at `historical._API_PREFIX`, both looked up when called, and every listing is paged by cursor (`_pages`: a cursor seen twice, or more than `config.SCANNER_MAX_PAGES` pages, raises). It reads again (at most `LIVE_READ_ATTEMPTS` reads, `LIVE_READ_RETRY_PAUSE_SECONDS` apart) while the newest fill or settlement is one the read lacks or the newest fill is under `LIVE_READ_SETTLE_SECONDS` old, and marks the read `changing` when it never settles (deposits and withdrawals are not re-checked: one applied between their read and the balance read would show only in the cash check). A record it cannot read stops the read with an error rather than being left out. A fill's direction is its `book_side` alone ("bid" buys YES or closes NO held; Kalshi's `side`/`action` contradict its docs); cash is every shard's dollars, a shard listed twice counted once by its last entry, as `auth._balance_cents_by_shard` counts it, with a warning on `Account.warnings`; Kalshi's positions value is `auth._positions_value_cents`' reading. `read_markets` looks markets up on `/markets`, then `/historical/markets` for the rest, `LIVE_MARKET_TICKERS_PER_REQUEST` at a time. `trade_log_paths` lists `reporter.PROD_LOG_PATH` (looked up when called) and reporter's own fallback copies beside it (`trade_log_<date>_<time>_<microseconds>.xlsx`, with `-N` or eight hex digits added when that name is taken; a copy you made is never read); `read_trade_logs` reads their rows' local Date and Time (text, or a date and a time) as UTC (in the hour that repeats when clocks go back, the latest reading not after the next row's, since reporter adds rows in time order; a lone row there takes the later one), drops simulated rows and exact repeats in the same log or another, turns a damaged file or row, or a status it does not know, into a warning (those trades count as Other bets), reads a sale row (its Notes start with `_SALE_NOTE`, `[sale: `, as `reporter._sale_to_row` writes them) as a real run's row but never as a purchase or a warning — a run that sells logs its sales and then its purchases at two log times, each with a `RunStart` of its own, the sales' naming the markets their orders may have filled on (`RunStart.sold_markets`, from `_sale_markets`: a held market whose count is blank or above 0, never a paid-out partner, which `reporter.PAID_OUT_MARKET` marks, nor any market of a "not sold" row) — and gives each real run's window: after the previous real run's log time, at most `LIVE_BOT_RUN_WINDOW_SECONDS` before its own. `match_bot_fills` matches each leg to one order of exactly its market, direction and size, reading each log time as the end of its second (`LIVE_TRADE_LOG_TIME_STEP_SECONDS`: the Time cell is rounded down, and a run's last orders can land inside that second): inside the run's window the latest; then up to `LIVE_BOT_RUN_CLOCK_SLACK_SECONDS` further the earliest; then a rolled-back or failed-unwind row, newest first, claims the bids that bought its NO leg back (a manual-review row sent no unwind and claims none). `build_ledger` replays the fills and the payouts (Kalshi's, plus one `all_payouts` rebuilds from the market for each it no longer lists; a market it cannot look up, or finds with no settlement it can read, is a warning and its contracts stay held) oldest first: a fill closes the side held, then opens the rest; an unwind takes its own lots first, and your own sale takes first the bot purchase whose other market you also sold within `LIVE_MANUAL_PAIR_SECONDS` (`manual_partners`: only your sales, fills that close part of what the account holds, are partners), then your own lots, then the oldest. The bot's sale orders are Other bets fills and are read the same way, so a held pair's two sale orders close that pair's purchase first, and a lone held market's sale closes your own contracts in that market first, then the bot's.
