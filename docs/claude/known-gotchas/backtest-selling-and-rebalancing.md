**The Rebalance family: one lazy `RebalanceSweep` for the dashboard's Rebalance and Min. price movement selects (backtest-only).** To rebalance a held pair is to bring it back to its Kelly size, the share of the portfolio value the Kelly rule gives it: at each weekly checkpoint a pair with room below that size is topped up (`_simulate_at_discount(add_to_held=True)`, DR-77) and a pair that has grown past it is sold down (`trim_to_kelly=True`; see the rules paragraph below). The simulator keeps the two steps as separate switches, so the tests of each step alone keep their worked-out figures, but no backtest scenario turns one on without the other: every place a person chooses has only Rebalance, and `RebalanceSweep` is the only family whose simulations rebalance, each with both switches on. That is pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestNoHalfRebalancing` (a spy on every `CapSweep` a full golden run and its cell reads build, and an AST check that every `CapSweep(...)` call in `backtester.py` passes `add_to_held` and `trim_to_kelly` both as `True`, or neither) and by `tests/test_dashboard.py::TestNoHalfRebalancing` over a whole page's walk. `run_backtest_sweep(rebalance_sweep=True)` — the CLI's default, `--no-rebalance-sweep` the opt-out, `False` in the function itself — returns `BacktestSweep.rebalance_sweep`, a frozen `RebalanceSweep` over the same bands, `k`s and caps as the size-cap sweep (the run's own cap alone when the size-cap sweep is off), with the tier floors on and, over the bands a tier binds at, off; `sweep(tier_floors=, min_move=, caps=)` is its `CapSweep` for one Tier floors and price-movement setting. Four things set the family apart from the size-cap sweeps. (1) **It has no eager point to start from**, because no eager run of the backtest rebalances: its `CapSweep`s are built with `eager={}` and no `same_title_eager`, so each cell is simulated when a report reads it — every cap in turn until one reaches its sharing floor (`_sharing_floor`: the larger of its peak Kelly fraction and its `cap_free_from`, which covers every trim decision and, over a walked book, the live search's bound), which every larger cap then shares. (2) **Each cell ends its curves on the day its eager twin's curve ended.** `_sweep_from_candidates` records the last curve day of every eager "all" point as it simulates it — the tier-on loop's in one map and the tier-off loop's in another, since the two share keys (`lazy_end_dates` and `lazy_off_end_dates`, which the sell family reads too) — and hands the family its maps as `end_dates` and `off_end_dates`, keyed by the exact band and `k` objects the cells are read with (pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestRebalanceSweep::test_each_cell_has_the_day_its_eager_twin_ended_on`, across UTC midnight by `::test_a_cell_read_after_utc_midnight_ends_every_cap_on_the_eager_day` and, under a clock that moves one day on every read so that every eager point ends on a day of its own, by `::test_every_cap_of_every_cell_ends_on_its_own_twin_s_day`). (3) **Only the "all" population, and no checks** (`checks=False`, no split date, no same-title population of their own, since "all" already holds the same-title entries): the filter bar reads the "all" point alone, so a cell is one simulation per cap. (4) **The run itself is unchanged.** Nothing is simulated while the family is built, so the run's simulations (their order and keywords), completion lines and every figure it returns are those of a run without the flag (pinned by `::test_on_adds_no_simulation_and_no_run_figure` and `::test_on_adds_no_info_line_but_its_own`). The run logs, right after the `Selling early (backtest)` line (itself right after the `Entry checkpoint (backtest)` line), `Rebalancing (backtest): on — a held pair is added to or trimmed only when at least 7 days have passed since the last trade on it (config.REBALANCE_MIN_DAYS_SINCE_TRADE); the dashboard's Rebalance select is simulated when the dashboard is built` (`_rebalance_wait_text` words the wait; `with no wait after a trade on it` at 0), or `off — the dashboard's Rebalance select stays disabled`; with the family on, a second line, `Rebalancing (backtest): minimum price movements on — the dashboard's Min. price movement select (5c to 95c, config.MIN_PRICE_MOVES) is simulated when the dashboard is built` (the range read off the resolved grid by `_moves_span`) or `off — the dashboard's Min. price movement select stays disabled`; and, once the family exists, `Rebalancing: N size cap(s) x B band(s) (O with the tier floors off) x K k, each simulated when the dashboard reads it`. The family keeps alive the entries of the sweep it mirrors (`entries_by_band`, `tier_off_entries` — the same objects the size-cap and sell families hold, so beside either of them it adds no retention, and on a run with neither it is what keeps them, bounded as `CapSweep`'s retention note says), and `RebalanceSweep.entry_events()` lists the event and fallback category of every qualifying Monday of both Tier floors settings' entries, so a report lists the category and tag of a pair a rebalancing run could trade (a trim frees cash that an add-on or a new pair spends) before any cell is simulated. A run on a Monday-infeasible window has no family (`rebalance_sweep=None`). **Cost.** Every cell is simulated when the page is built, cap by cap (no eager point stands in for any), so the dashboard step takes longer. On `TestFilterPageSize`'s worst case, where every band's rebalancing run trades a different list, the Rebalance view takes the page from 925,829 to 1,396,467 bytes, and from 1,235,347 to 1,943,061 beside the tier-floors-off family (measured 2026-10-17; a real rebalancing run mostly trades the list of the run without it and shares its chunk). **A real 365-day page has not been measured with the family or its minimums.** **Every minimum price movement, without repeating a run (`move_sweep`).** `run_backtest_sweep(rebalance_sweep=True, move_sweep=True)` — the CLI's default, `--no-move-sweep` the opt-out, `False` in the function, and a `ValueError` before anything is logged or fetched without `rebalance_sweep` (in `_sweep_from_candidates` too) — gives the family its minimums: `RebalanceSweep.min_moves`, from `_resolve_min_moves()`, which reads the backtester's own binding of `MIN_PRICE_MOVES` when called (patch `backtester.MIN_PRICE_MOVES` in a test, never `config`'s), checks each value through `_checked_min_move`, refuses an empty grid or a repeated value, returns them ascending, and is called before the fetch only when the flag is on. `RebalanceSweep` refuses `min_moves` that are not strictly ascending amounts from the finest price step to 1 (`__post_init__`, through `_checked_min_move`), since `move_grid` depends on that order; without the flag they are `()`, `sweep` then takes no minimum and `move_grid` yields nothing. Nothing is simulated during the run. `RebalanceSweep.move_grid(band, k, *, tier_floors=, caps=, start=, stats=)` is a generator that yields, for each minimum in ascending order, `(minimum, {cap: SweepPoint | SameMove})`. Per cap it keeps one run in hand — first the rebalancing run with no minimum, then the last run it simulated for that cap — as that run's minimum and its `moves` alone, never a point. `start` is `{cap: moves}` of the no-minimum run for a caller that already has it (then that run is not simulated again); a `start` that lacks a cap covered, or holds `None` for one, is a `ValueError`, raised like its other refusals on the first step. `stats` gains `simulated` and `reused` (`CapSweep`'s counters) and `same` (cells yielded as `SameMove`). **The reuse rule, and why it is exact.** A run simulated at minimum T stands for a larger minimum T′ exactly when every add-on and every trim that run made passes `config.price_move_reached(move, T′)`, the very test the walk's gates ask (a run that made neither always does); it is then yielded as `SameMove(T)` (`SameMove(None)` for the run with no minimum). Follow the two runs side by side: while they have made the same trades they hold the same pairs, each last traded on the same day (`_price_move` and `_lone_leg_move` read only the open trades' entry dates and `last_trim`, both changed only by a trade), so every pair's move reads the same in both. The wait after a trade (`config.rebalance_rested`, asked before the move) reads the same dates, so a pair that waits in one run waits in the other, and a waiting pair is asked for no decision in either. A pair T holds back, T′ holds back too, because the test only gets harder as the minimum grows (`abs(move) >= minimum − PRICE_EPSILON`). A pair the T run trimmed is let through at T′ (the condition) and `_kelly_trim` is handed the same position, day, value, `k` and caps, so it sells the same count. A pair the T run looked at and did not trim is not trimmed at T′ either, looked at or held back. An add-on the T run made is let through at T′ (its move is in the same record, so the condition covers it) and sized the same; one T held back T′ holds back; and one T let through but did not make (no room below its Kelly share, no size that fits) changed nothing, as a hold-back at T′ does not. So the walks make the same trades at every checkpoint and never part. What can differ is only what is not a trade: the held-back counts the log lines report, and `SweepPoint.cap_free_from`, which can be lower in a fresh T′ run (a pair held back there adds no decision to it); neither is read through a `SameMove`, and cap sharing inside each `CapSweep` is decided by that sweep's own runs as before. The other direction holds too: a minimum is simulated only when it holds back an add-on or a trim the run in hand made, so its trades differ from that run's at that checkpoint. A run that traded after a move smaller than a minimum stands for no minimum at that minimum and above; and a move recorded as 0.0 (the walk's stand-in for an add-on or a trim with no move to read) passes no minimum of the grid, which could only cost a simulation. **Unmeasured:** how many minimums a real 365-day page simulates, how long that takes and how much the page grows. The worst case is 19 times the Rebalance step, and a scenario that neither adds nor trims costs nothing more. Each minimum that is a run of its own is packed as a chunk inside the page (not a sidecar file, as a Sell level's is), and the simulations run one after another in the main process, on by default (`--no-move-sweep` skips them). Pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestMoveGrid` (every (minimum, cap) equal to a fresh `_simulate_at_discount(..., add_to_held=True, trim_to_kelly=True, min_move=T)` on trades and curve, a `SameMove` through the run it names, over the short grid and the config grid, and over pairs whose add-ons some minimums hold back; every point differing from the run it replaces; add-on moves in the record deciding which runs are one; a run that trimmed after a 3c fall standing for minimums up to 3c, and a `start` with too small a move never stood in for by the no-minimum run; only the cells yielded as points simulated, by a spy and by `stats`; with `start` the no-minimum run not simulated; the cover asking `price_move_reached` about each add-on and trim of the run in hand; the refusals; `::test_the_parity_check_catches_a_wrong_cover`, which compiles `move_grid` with a cover that always answers the same run, one that never does and one that never lets a fall through, and requires the parity check to fail on each; and `::test_the_parity_check_catches_a_walk_that_does_not_record_add_on_moves`, which compiles the walk without the line that records an add-on's move and requires the parity check to fail on it), `::TestRebalanceSweep` (the setting lines, no simulation or run figure added, the family carrying config's grid, the refusal without `rebalance_sweep` before any fetch or log line, a bad `MIN_PRICE_MOVES` refused before the fetch and not read without the flag, `_resolve_min_moves`), `tests/test_backtest.py::TestRebalanceSweepArgument` and `::TestMoveSweepArgument`, and, by AST, `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_a_run_stands_for_a_larger_minimum_through_the_walk_s_own_test` (`move_grid`'s cover is `all(price_move_reached(move, min_move) for move in moves)`, only the walk (its add-on gate and `planned_trims`) and the grid call that function in the backtester (`seller.plan_trims` calls it too, for the live trim), and no ordering comparison in the module names a move).

**The bar's Rebalance and Min. price movement selects (reporting only).** The filter bar's fifth and sixth selects, after Size cap and before Sell (`_filter_bar_html`, ids `flt-rebalance` and `flt-move`, `SHOWN[6]` and `SHOWN[9]`; the Sell choice is `SHOWN[7]` and Min. days `SHOWN[8]`), are the only held-pair choices the page offers. Rebalance offers "no" — the page as rendered — and "yes": the scenario on screen re-simulated with every held pair topped up to its Kelly size when it has room and sold down to it when it has grown past it (the family above). Its hover text (`_rebalance_select_title`) states that rule and the wait after a trade, that Sell is set to no selling while it is yes, what Save as live defaults saves and that the Scenario Explorer and Interval Discount sections always show no rebalancing. Min. price movement offers "any" and "5c" to "95c" (`_MOVE_TITLE`: used only while Rebalance is yes; a held pair is topped up or sold down only once its total price has moved at least that many cents, up or down, since its last trade; 10c is 10% of a $1 payout). **How the family joins the page.** `dashboard._with_rebalance(source, sweep)` attaches the family to the walked grid (`_GridSource.rebalance_cell` and `rebalance_off_cell`, and, when the family carries minimums, `moves` and `move_cell`) only if its bands, `k`s and caps are exactly the grid's; `_walk_once` reads its cells after the tier-floors-off ones (`_walk_rebalance_cells`) and hands them to the visitors' opt-in `rebalance` method (`_ChunkVisitor`), then, last, reads each of those cells at every minimum (`_walk_move_cells`, through `RebalanceSweep.move_grid`, handed as `start` the moves the Rebalance walk recorded for the cell, so the run with no minimum is not simulated twice; `_ChunkVisitor.move`). `_ExplorerVisitor` and `_KdVisitor` have neither method, so the Scenario Explorer and the Interval Discount section never follow either choice, and the summary line says so (`_REBALANCE_REACH`, or `_MOVE_REACH` in its place when the page shows the minimums, each with a no-explorer twin). The base block carries `grid_rebalance`, with the tier floors off `grid_rebalance_off` (a band no tier binds at reads its tier-on cell, as `grid_off` does) and `rebalance_state` (`shown`; `not simulated` for a run without the family; `unavailable` when the page could not use it); and `moves` (each minimum's `label`, such as `5c`, its `phrase`, which is `backtester._move_label`'s words, " only after a 10c price move", and its `value` in dollars), `grid_move` and `grid_move_off` (`[band][k][cap]` → one chunk id per minimum, in `moves` order, or null where every minimum shows that scenario's Rebalance cell; a grid of nothing but nulls is still the view) and `move_state`. A rebalancing run, and a minimum's run, is packed as a chunk as any scenario is (an equal trade list at an equal `k` shares one, so a cell in which nothing was added or trimmed costs one grid entry, and a minimum that holds every add-on and trim back shows the chunk of the run without rebalancing when its list is that run's); a `SameMove` copies the chunk id of the run it names with nothing packed, and the minimum's index is part of the chunk-key memo's phase (`move<i>`), since one minimum's lists are gone before the next minimum's are made. Every category and tag the family's entries can trade is listed up front, as for the size-cap sweep; with `--no-cap-sweep` that list is every entry's event (the eager grid lists only traded ones), so a voided pair's category can appear as a new Exclude categories or Exclude tags menu box (at 0) and re-index the categories, tags and `k̂` groups, with every figure unchanged. **It fails closed, and costs that choice alone.** A run with no family (`--no-rebalance-sweep`), a family whose bands, `k`s or caps are not the grid's, one the page cannot read, a size-cap sweep the page set aside (so the grid is the eager one, whose caps are the run's own alone) and a Rebalance cell that cannot be simulated each leave the select disabled beside a short note — "(not simulated in this backtest)" or "(not available on this page; see the log)" (`_REBALANCE_NOTES`; `_MOVE_NOTES` for the minimums) — and each has a WARNING of its own, a lost size-cap axis included (the size-cap sweep's WARNING, then `_warn_rebalance_lost_with_the_cap_sweep`'s, so the note's "see the log" is true); a run without the family has none, since nothing failed. A raising Rebalance cell calls `reset_rebalance` on the visitors, which drops the chunks, row heads and grids the Rebalance cells made and, with them, the minimums' (every other choice's chunks are untouched, and the page is the page without a family); a raising minimum calls `reset_move` and costs the Min. price movement select alone, the Rebalance view keeping its chunks and grids; an all-empty grid is never shown as a view. A tier-floors-off twin that does not match or cannot be read costs the tier-off half of the choice alone: with the tier floors off, rebalancing is shown at a band no tier binds at (its tier-on run stands in) and reads as not simulated at a band the tiers bind at. After a tier-floors-off size-cap fallback the two settings can show a different mix of caps (tiers off with Rebalance no, the run's own cap alone at a binding band; tiers off with Rebalance yes, every cap); each figure is real. **The script.** "Yes" at the primary cell is not the page as rendered (`isPrimary` also needs Rebalance no), so it reads as another run; while Rebalance is yes, Sell is set to "no selling" and shut (`refreshSell`: no sell level is built for a run that rebalances) and `chunkAt` answers null for a sell level there. Min. price movement is open only while Rebalance is yes, on a page whose base block carries `grid_move` (`refreshMove`, called from `refreshSell`, so on load, after every draw, when a choice is put back and when Rebalance changes); shut, it keeps its value, so Rebalance switched to yes again shows the minimum it still names, and on load it is reset to "any" with the other selects. A scenario's chunk at a minimum is that minimum's entry in the scenario's cell of `grid_move` (or `grid_move_off` with the tier floors off: `moveGridAt`), and the Rebalance cell's own chunk where that cell is null (`chunkAt`), so a minimum that changes nothing loads nothing. The script writes no word of its own: the option texts, the hover texts, each minimum's summary phrase (`moves[i].phrase`, after `D.text.rebalance`) and the reach sentences are Python's. The bar's call to the explorer's `window.dashScenarioSelect(band, k, cap, tier)` keeps its four arguments and never carries either choice. **The save button** sends `rebalance` (the Rebalance choice) and `min_price_move` (the minimum shown, or `off`) for the scenario on screen only from a page whose base block has the Rebalance view, and stays disabled for a scenario the page never simulated (Rebalance yes with the tier floors off at a band the twin lacks, say); the address read back through `defaults_server._proposal` gives the scenario's own `LiveSettings`. Saving from the bar's default state sends Rebalance no, which would turn off the live rebalancing the saved defaults have on, so on a page with the view whose run recorded saved defaults that rebalance (`_save_target`'s `live_rebalances`), a grey note right after the button says so (`_REBALANCE_SAVE_NOTE`). **What a reader sees for an add-on and a trim.** An add-on is a `BacktestTrade` of its own (`add_on=True`) that books its own count, cost, fees and payout, and the part a trim sells is a trade of its own too (`sold=True`), so the trade counts, the performance figures, the decomposition and the diagnostics count each as one more trade, with no marker in the tables or caption under the chart (either would move a golden digest); in the Risk section's Kelly-vs-actual scatter an add-on's x is the pair's uncapped Kelly fraction at the prices it paid and its y what it spent, fees included, over the portfolio value before it (`balance_at_entry`), so it sits below the 1:1 line and can read as badly undersized, and a trimmed pair shows as several smaller trades. While Rebalance is yes the summary line names the scenario ", rebalancing" (`_REBALANCE_PHRASE`), followed by the minimum's phrase when one is chosen, and says so (`_REBALANCE_SUMMARY_NOTE`, from `D.text.rebalance_note`). Pinned by `tests/test_dashboard.py::TestRebalanceView`, `::TestMoveView`, `::TestRebalanceSelect`, `::TestMoveSelect`, `::TestRebalanceScript`, `::TestMoveScript`, `::TestRebalanceEndToEnd` (a real `RebalanceSweep`, tier floors on and off: at every minimum and cap the chunk the page names equals a fresh `_simulate_at_discount(add_to_held=True, trim_to_kelly=True, min_move=…)`, and the selects draw those chunks), `::TestLiveRebalanceRule`, `::TestNoHalfRebalancing` and the rebalance variants of `::TestFilterPageSize`, whose budgets are their measurements + 20%.

**Selling a position early — the backtest's take-profit rule (2026-10; live selling applies the same rule — see the live-selling paragraph under Key Patterns, which also records where the two differ).** `backtester._simulate_at_discount(..., *, sell_at=None)` sells a whole position before it pays out once it has realized `sell_at` of its potential profit and stayed there for `config.TAKE_PROFIT_HOLD_DAYS` days in a row (see **Days in a row** below); `None`, the default, never sells, and the walk is then unchanged (the commit that added it changed no existing test, the golden fixtures included). Its arithmetic is shared with live selling: `_sells_at` calls `config.take_profit_reached`, `_days_left` calls `config.days_to_maturity`, `_ladder_average` calls `scanner.walk_bids`, `_usable_ask` calls `historical.usable_candle_ask`, `_leg_quotes` records its sale bids with `historical.candle_sale_bids` and `_HOLD_DAYS_MAX` is `config.TAKE_PROFIT_HOLD_DAYS_MAX`, each a one-line wrapper that leaves the backtest's results unchanged (`tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_the_backtest_reads_the_sell_rule_where_live_code_can`). **The definitions** are the operator's — realized profit = current market value − total cost, potential profit = potential total return − total cost — read as follows. A POSITION is an open trade with every open trade joined to it by a shared market (`_positions`, union-find on tickers): without `add_to_held` every open trade is a position of its own; with it, a pair with everything added to it, a lone-leg add-on included. Its total cost is its trades' contract cost plus entry fees; its potential total return its contract pairs × `CONTRACT_PAYOUT_DOLLARS` (what a win pays); its current market value WHAT SELLING IT WOULD RETURN (`_position_sale_value`, the sell rule's one reader of a bid and of a sale ladder; the Kelly trim's `_fresh_bids` reads the fresh bids too): each market's contracts, totalled by ticker and side held across the position's trades (a lone-leg add-on can hold one market as A in one trade and as B in another), walk that market's modeled bid ladder (`LegQuotes.sale_ladder`), anchored at the fresh bid of the side held (`LegQuotes.bid_at_checkpoint`: one minus the other side's ask on the latest candle that ended at or before the checkpoint AND within one candle period of it, that ask usable by `_usable_ask`'s rule — never carried forward, NaN otherwise), and each trade's contracts sell at the average bid reached, less the taker fee on that sale (`config.fee_leg_exact(n, average bid)`, per trade per leg); a position whose ladder at any check holds fewer contracts on a market than the position does is not sold that Monday; with no depth model, no volume data for the market at that check, or a bid below 1c or above 99c (which the model has no ladders for, `depth_model.can_start_at`), the sale is the single fresh bid in any size; a leg whose market has paid out by the checkpoint (`LegQuotes.paid_at_checkpoint`, from the exact settlement time) counts at its payout, with no fee. A position sells when `_sells_at` holds — potential > 0 and realized ≥ `sell_at` × potential − `PRICE_EPSILON` — at the checkpoint and at each daily check before it (next paragraph). A position with a trade that has no quotes, an unpaid leg with no fresh bid, or a ladder too thin for it, is not sold at that checkpoint. Nor is one with an unpaid leg whose market has not traded or moved its quote within `config.BACKTEST_STALE_QUOTE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS` (24 hours) of the checkpoint (`_quote_is_fresh`, at the checkpoint only, not at the earlier daily checks); it may sell at a later checkpoint once its quote moves. Nor, with the volume cap on, is a position holding more contracts on a market still to pay out than that market's cap (`config.BACKTEST_MAX_VOLUME_SHARE` of the contracts it traded in the next day, `LegQuotes.sale_ladder`); it may sell at a later checkpoint the cap allows. The checkpoint's own check and the proceeds are the same number, while the equity curve still values an open trade at the ASK (see the market-valuation paragraph below), so a sale shows on its day as a step down of the spread and the sale's fees. **Days in a row (`config.TAKE_PROFIT_HOLD_DAYS`, 3).** A position is sold at a checkpoint only when it has reached its level there AND at a daily check on each of the `TAKE_PROFIT_HOLD_DAYS` − 1 days before it, so a price that jumps for a moment does not trigger a sale. The checks run 24 hours apart, back from the checkpoint's own instant (`_checkpoint_datetime` less whole days — never the wall clock, so a clock change between two checks moves none of them), and each earlier check values the position as a sale then would (`_position_sale_value(position, day, days_back)`): each leg at the bid from the LAST QUOTE of the 24 hours before that check — the latest candle that ended in (check − 24 h, check], never carried forward (a candle exactly 24 hours old belongs to the check before) — or at its payout once its market had paid out by that check; the checkpoint's own check keeps the fresh-bid rule above, since the sale happens at it. With a depth model, each check walks the ladder built from its own 24-hour volume (`sale_ladder(day, bid, days_back)`). A check that cannot be valued (an unpaid leg with no quote that day, or a ladder too thin for the position there) holds the sale back like a check below the level. `LegQuotes` keeps the checks as a YES and a NO bid, a paid-out marker and a 24-hour volume a day from `first_day` (`yes_bid_daily`, `no_bid_daily`, `paid_daily`, `volume_daily`, in its fingerprint; the check on a checkpoint's own date is that checkpoint), read through `bid_at_checkpoint`/`paid_at_checkpoint`/`sale_ladder`'s `days_back` (1 to 6; 0, the default, is the checkpoint). `_hold_readings` is the one reader of the checks (the sell rule's one caller of `_position_sale_value`, which the Kelly trim's `_kelly_trim` also calls, for part of a position) and `_reached_every_day` the one test (`_sells_at` at every check): the walk's sales, `_position_sells` and `_sale_reach` all decide through them (pinned by AST in `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_open_trades_are_valued_through_one_definition`). With `level=`, `_hold_readings` stops at the first check below that level, so the walk reads no check after a failing one. Every check of a sale falls after the previous checkpoint (`backtester._HOLD_DAYS_MAX` = 7), so every trade the position holds at the sale was already held at each check (trades are bought only at checkpoints), and the rule values those trades at every check (with `add_to_held`, a trade that paid out between two checks is valued at none of them, though it was held at the earlier one). `_resolve_hold_days` reads `backtester.TAKE_PROFIT_HOLD_DAYS` when called (patch the backtester's binding in a test, never `config`'s) and refuses anything but a whole number from 1 to 7 — in `_simulate_at_discount` only when it sells, in `_sale_reach`, and in `run_backtest_sweep` before anything is fetched when the sell family is on, whose setting line names the rule (`Selling early (backtest): on — a position is sold once it has stayed at or above a level for 3 days in a row (config.TAKE_PROFIT_HOLD_DAYS); …`). The dashboard hands it to each Sell worker (`_SellTask.hold_days`) and names it in the Sell select's hover text (`_sell_select_title`). 1 is the checkpoint alone, the rule before the days were added. **Every Sell figure recorded before the days were added sold on the checkpoint's bids alone, so none is comparable.** **A minimum of days before maturity (`sell_min_days`).** `_simulate_at_discount(..., *, sell_min_days=N)` (a whole number of at least 1, set only with `sell_at`; `None`, the default, sets no minimum and changes nothing) sells a position that has reached its level only while at least N days remain before it matures; nearer than that it is not even valued (`_far_enough` is asked before `_hold_readings`), and since its days left only shrink at later checkpoints it is held until it pays out (unless, with `add_to_held`, an add-on brings in a market that closes later). **Maturity is the day the position's LAST market stops trading** (operator decision): `_days_left(position, day)` is the latest `close_date_a`/`close_date_b` among its trades less the checkpoint's date, in whole calendar days — the close date as the market's `close_time` writes it (UTC on every Kalshi timestamp) against the checkpoint's UTC date — and `None` when a trade has no close date (only a hand-built one), which a minimum never sells. Both the walk's sale step and its quick test at a checkpoint with no candidate (`_position_sells(..., min_days)`) ask `_far_enough` first, so the walk applies one rule. A minimum of 1 day is almost, but not exactly, the no-minimum rule: the two differ only where the position's last market closes on or before the checkpoint's own UTC date (0 or fewer days left). **Sales still come before every purchase at a checkpoint** (operator requirement): the walk runs `release(d)` (the day's pay-outs), then `sell(d)`, then the valuation (`checkpoint_value`), then the candidates, new pairs and add-ons alike, so a sale's proceeds are cash for that checkpoint's trades, each is sized on the value after the sales, and a sold pair's markets are neither bought nor added to there (`sold_here`); the days rule sits inside the sale step and the quick test, so it cannot move that order. Every selling run records one frozen `SaleCheck(days_left, profits)` per position it sells, in order, on `SweepPoint.sales` (`()` when it sold nothing, `None` when it never sells; `repr=False`, `compare=False`; a copy `CapSweep` shares at another cap keeps it), and stamps `SweepPoint.sell_min_days`; the completion line's label adds `, at least <N> day(s) before maturity` after the sell level. `_sim_options` forwards `sell_min_days` only when set, `_half_split` and `_ex_top_event` (from `point.sell_min_days`) re-simulate at it, and `CapSweep(sell_min_days=…)` (refused without `sell_at`, checked by `_resolve_sell_min_days` in `__post_init__`) forwards it to every simulation and check; the rule reads only close dates and the checkpoint date, which no cap moves, so cap sharing stays exact. **Residual (look-ahead):** the cache's `close_time` is a settled market's REALIZED close, so for an event decided early the backtest sees maturity as near when a live trader, reading the scheduled close, would not. That holds now only with `config.BACKTEST_SCHEDULED_CLOSE` off, or for a market with no matching rule (no rule for its series, or a fingerprint the rule never saw): otherwise each trade's close dates are its markets' scheduled closes (`backtester._maturity_close`). **A page rebuilt from the saved sweep can mix two versions of the simulator (`backtest.py --dashboard-only`, 2026-10).** Every full run saves what it hands `generate_dashboard` to `config.BACKTEST_SWEEP_FILENAME` right after the sweep, and `--dashboard-only` rebuilds the page from it. The eager points in that file were simulated by the run, but the lazy families (`CapSweep`, `SellSweep`, `RebalanceSweep`) hold only their entries and simulate every cell while the page is built — with the code as it is at the rebuild. **Rebalancing a held pair in the backtest: the trim step, the price movement and the wait (2026-10; the trim's rule is the one a live run with rebalancing on trims by too — see the live-trimming paragraph under Key Patterns).** Until the trim step a run could only grow a held pair (`add_to_held`) or sell it whole at a take-profit level (`sell_at`), so "bring a held pair back to its Kelly size at every run" could not be tested. `backtester._simulate_at_discount(..., *, trim_to_kelly=True)` sells part of a held pair once it has outgrown its Kelly size; `False`, the default, changes nothing, and no eager run sets it. With `add_to_held` as well, as in every run of the Rebalance family, a pair is topped up or sold down each week (never both in one week: a trimmed pair's markets are not bought or added to at that checkpoint). **The trim rule** (operator decision: Kelly at sale prices). At a weekly checkpoint, for each open position that is one exact pair (`_exact_pair`: every open trade the same two markets bought the same way round), the walk reads the fresh best bids of both sides of both markets (`_fresh_bids`, the bids a sale reads) and asks `config.kelly_trim_count`: (1) the chance the pair pays, `config.held_pair_win_prob` — a new trade's own forecast at those quotes, `1 − k × mid spread` for a time-series pair (each ask is 1 minus the other side's bid) and `SAME_TITLE_CO_RESOLVE_PROB` for a same-title one; (2) one contract pair's sale value `v`, the best bids of the two sides held less `fee_per_pair_approx`; (3) the hold share `config.kelly_hold_fraction(p, v) = (p − v) / (1 − v)`, the Kelly fraction of buying the pair at `v`, held to 0–1; (4) the target, `min(config.pair_size_cap, hold share)` of the checkpoint's portfolio value (the cash after the day's pay-outs and take-profit sales plus every open trade at market), which keeps `floor(target ÷ v)` contract pairs; (5) the count to sell is then checked against what selling really returns — `net_sale(pairs)` walks the modeled bid ladder with the exact fees (`_position_sale_value(position, day, sold=...)`) — and the rule sells a count, at most step (4)'s excess, that it still wants at that sale's own average price: the largest such in the usual case, found by halving and then a scan of up to 32 counts above the halving's answer (per-leg fee rounding makes the test uneven for small counts, and beyond that scan the count sold can be a smaller one it still wants); a ladder that holds fewer sells fewer. Buying sizes on the asks and selling on the bids, so between the two sizes nothing trades. **Left alone:** a position that is not one exact pair (a pair with an add-on to a lone leg, which brings in a third market), a trade with no quotes, a missing fresh bid on a side of a market still open, a market whose quote has not traded or moved within `config.BACKTEST_STALE_QUOTE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS` of the checkpoint (`_fresh_bids` asks `_quote_is_fresh`) — and, with the volume cap on, a trim sells at most each market's cap of contract pairs (its sale ladder is cut to it, so the rule sells what fits) — a time-series pair with a crossed quote or a mid spread not above zero, and a position with a market that has paid out, except **a lone leg** (`_lone_leg`): the later market (B) of a time-series pair, still open after the earlier market (A) paid out $0 on the YES held there (read from A's own payout record), which is trimmed by the same rule, as live trims it. A reads as its settlement price (`config.settled_bids`, through `_fresh_bids`: a YES bid of 0 and a NO bid of $1), so it adds $0 to a sale and only B's NO contracts are sold; only B is held to the stale-quote check and the volume cap, and the price move is B's NO ask alone (`_price_move`). A same-title pair with a paid-out market, the earlier market held after the later one paid out, and a lone leg whose YES on A paid $1 are still left alone. **Order at a checkpoint:** pay-outs, take-profit sales (`sell_at`, when set), trims, the valuation, purchases; a trim's cash funds that checkpoint's purchases, and the trimmed pair's markets go into `sold_here`. With trimming on the walk visits every checkpoint up to the last pay-out (`_sale_stream`), as a selling run does. **What a trim records.** The latest trade is sold first (`_last_bought_first`). A trade sold whole becomes its sold copy (`_sold_copy`, as a take-profit sale's does); a trade sold in part is split by `_split_trade`: the part sold is a new `BacktestTrade` with `sold=True`, its own sale prices and exit day, and the kept trade shrinks in place (count, cost, fees and payout in proportion, the two parts adding up to the original), so `profit = actual_payoff − total_cost − fees` holds for both and the equity curve needs nothing new. A pair sold whole may be bought again at a later checkpoint. The completion line's label names a run that trims `, trimming to Kelly` (after the sell level; a run that adds and trims, which every Rebalance run does, reads `, rebalancing` after the cap instead), and one silent-at-zero line reads `Positions trimmed to their Kelly size (<label>): N (contract pairs sold: M)` (DEBUG when quiet). **Two properties the Rebalance family rests on.** (a) A run with the trim step on in which nothing is trimmed makes exactly the trades and the curve of the run with it off: at a checkpoint with no candidate it acts only when something sells or is trimmed. So a dashboard scenario where rebalancing changed no trade shares the chunk of the scenario without it (equal trade lists at an equal `k` share a chunk). (b) The size cap reaches a trim through `min(cap, hold share)`, so each decision reports `KellyTrim.cap_free_from`, a cap such that a decision made at a cap at or above it is the no-cap decision; the walk keeps the largest over every position it looks at, trimmed or not, and the point's `cap_free_from` covers it, so `CapSweep` shares one simulation only across caps at or above every trim's own floor (`_sharing_floor`). **Measured on synthetic test fixtures only (2026-10-10; not real data), the trim step alone.** On `TestTrimCapSweep`'s three pairs (one outgrows its cap; $10,000, band 0–1), hold against trim: at `k` 0.5 and a 10% cap, return +18.42% → +17.82%, max drawdown −9.48% → −8.27%, Sharpe 1.82 → 1.92 (two parts sold; Sharpe at a 0% risk-free rate); at a 20% cap, +38.15% → +37.45%, −17.84% → −16.53%, 1.87 → 1.92; at caps of 50% and above nothing is trimmed and the two runs are one. On the golden band-sweep fixture nothing is trimmed at any cap (band 0–1, `k` 0.4 to 0.75). The hold share of a time-series pair on uncrossed books is at least `1 − k` (its sale value is at most 1 minus the mid spread), above any size it could have been bought at, so a time-series trim comes from the size cap or from a pair that has grown past `1 − k` of the portfolio; a same-title pair's hold share falls to zero as its sale value nears 0.95, so one that has converged is sold whole. **Residuals, recorded.** A trim has no "days in a row" rule, so one fresh quote decides it. A small excess is often not sold: each leg's sale fee is rounded up to the cent, which can make a sale of a few contract pairs worth less per pair than the rule requires. A part sold is a trade record of its own, so trade counts rise and split parts sit below the Kelly chart's 1:1 line. Live leaves the same lone legs alone, and a sell level is never combined with rebalancing on the page. **The price movement.** With `min_move=x` (`None` by default: no minimum, and the run unchanged, trade for trade and curve for curve; refused on a run that neither adds to nor trims held pairs, and when it is not a real number from the finest price step, `MIN_ACTIVE_PRICE_DOLLARS`, 0.0001, to 1 — `_resolve_min_move`, through `_checked_min_move`: a smaller minimum would sit inside the float slack `price_move_reached` allows, where a flat price would pass as a move) a held pair is looked at for trimming, and a held pair (or a lone leg) added to, only once its total price is at least `x` dollars above or below where it stood at the last trade on it. A pair's total price is the price of the side held on each of its two markets, added (`_pair_price`): YES on the earlier market plus NO on the later one for a time-series pair, NO on the first plus YES on the second for a same-title pair; a lone leg's is its open market's ask alone, as a live trim reads it. The last trade is the pair's latest purchase or add-on (the latest `entry_date` among its open trades) or, when it is later, the last checkpoint a trim sold part of the pair at (`last_trim`, keyed by the pair's two tickers and written by the walk's `trim`). `_price_move` hands the two prices to `config.pair_price_move`, and `planned_trims` asks `config.price_move_reached` before `_kelly_trim`. A rise and a fall of the minimum both open a trim, and a checkpoint a pair is held back at does not move the price it is measured from. An add-on is gated the same way in Pass 2, after the ladder checks and before `held_pair_fraction`: first the wait (`config.rebalance_rested` of the day of its last trade), then, with a minimum, the move (`_held_pair_move` for an exact pair, the trim's own day and move; `_lone_leg_move` for a lone leg, its one market's ask through `_leg_mark` at that market's last purchase or a later trim of any pair it was in), a move that cannot be read (no quotes) held back by any minimum; each hold-back is counted (`Adds skipped because the held pair traded fewer than N days before (<label>): M`, `Adds skipped because the held pair's price had not moved <cents>c since its last trade (<label>): M`, silent at zero, DEBUG when quiet). A pair held back from a trim at a checkpoint is not in `sold_here`, so it may still be added to there, if its add-on passes the same gates, where a trimmed one may not. Buying a new pair follows the same rules as without the minimum; what a run goes on to buy can still differ, since a trade held back leaves the cash and the holding as they were. **The price is the ask-based mark.** Both prices are read through `_leg_mark`, the quote an open trade is valued at: the latest usable ask of the side held at that checkpoint, the payout once a market has paid out, the price paid before a side has had a usable ask. It is read at the checkpoint of the last trade and at this one, the same quote at both moments, so what a trade paid (its fills) and the gap between a market's bid and ask never count as a move. The trim itself still sizes on the bids; a bid is recorded only when a market traded in the hour before a checkpoint, so many purchases have none to measure from, which is why the asks are read. **Cap sharing.** A pair the minimum holds back is not asked for a decision (`continue` before `_kelly_trim`), so it adds nothing to the point's `cap_free_from`, and an add-on held back is never sized. The tests read prices and trade dates, never the cap, so `CapSweep`'s sharing stays exact: `CapSweep.min_move` (refused unless the sweep adds to or trims held pairs) reaches every simulation through `_sim_options`, which forwards it only when set, as `_half_split` and `_ex_top_event` (from `point.min_move`) do. **What a run records.** `SweepPoint.min_move`, and `SweepPoint.moves`: the move behind each trim and each add-on made, in order (an add-on whose move could not be read records 0.0, which no minimum lets through) — `()` for a run that could add or trim and did neither, `None` for a run that does neither, a negative figure after a fall. (2) the sale walks the real order book's bids within `KELLY_TRIM_MAX_BID_DROP` (20c, where the backtest's modeled ladder ends; `config.kelly_trim_bids`) of each held side's best bid, so a gap in a book is never sold into; (3) the fee is charged once per market (the backtest charges it per trade per leg); (4) the pair's kind and which market is A are read from the two markets' wording, series labels and event competitions (`scanner.held_pair_kind`, which also refuses a same-title pair from two sports or two competitions, DR-79 — a check the backtest's trim, which knows its pair's type, does not make — and which fails closed and, across two events, refuses pairs the finder can buy: closes at the same moment, stated days that are one day or contradict the close order, and unreadable stated days with closes within the same-title bound), where the backtest knows the pair's type, so a pair whose kind cannot be read is never trimmed, nor is one with a best YES bid of 1c or less (`historical.candle_reads_bid`: the backtest's candles cannot show such a bid, so it never trims on one); (5) the wait counts every fill on either market, a fill by hand included (the backtest knows only its own trades), from Kalshi's list of the account's fills, which must reach back the whole wait (Kalshi moves its oldest fills to an archive that read skips), and it counts calendar days on the bot's own clock (Pacific), so a trade late one evening can be trimmed early in the morning seven dates later, a little under 7 × 24 hours (the backtest's checkpoints are whole weeks apart and never see this); a live add-on waits too (`seller.plan_add_ons`), from the latest fill on its markets, so it cannot come the day after a trim, as in the backtest; (6) the price move is read differently (below); (7) after a trim, `_value_after_sales` can refuse Kalshi's value read back, and the run then sizes its buys on cash alone and adds to nothing (the backtest always reinvests a trim's proceeds), which a dry run has no read-back to show; (8) Kalshi's `fees_paid` for a market is cumulative, so after a trim the kept contracts' cost keeps the sold ones' fees, and a later take-profit check and an add-on's stake read a higher cost than the backtest does (both lean toward holding and buying less); The pair's total price is the same sum, the ask of the side held on each market, but the two prices do not come from the backtest's weekly candles: the price now comes from the order books the run has just read (a side's ask is 1 minus the other side's best bid), and the price then from each market's hourly candles at the pair's last fill, the latest fill on either market in Kalshi's list of the account's fills (`scanner.get_fill_times`; a fill by hand counts, and it is the very fill the 7-day wait counts from). `_Reads.ask_then` reads the candles of the `PRICE_THEN_MAX_AGE_DAYS` (7) days before that moment (`historical.recent_candles`, never cached) and `historical.ask_before` returns the side's latest usable ask on a candle that ended at or before it. **It fails closed:** with a minimum set, a pair is not trimmed, and its line says why, when no fill is listed on either market (a pair whose last fill is older than the list reaches, which Kalshi moves to an archive that read skips, has no readable price then, and waits until it trades again), when the candles cannot be read, or when a side has no usable quote in the 7 days before the fill. The step can only hold a trim back: it either stops the pair or lets it go on to the sizing it would reach with no minimum. With no minimum set, no candle is read at all.
