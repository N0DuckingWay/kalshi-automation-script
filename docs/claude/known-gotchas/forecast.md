- The time-series forecast reads the midpoints (DR-78, 2026-10).

**The time-series forecast reads the midpoints (DR-78, 2026-10).** **What.** A time-series trade's chance of profit is `p = 1 − k·spread` (`config.time_series_profit_prob(spread, k)`), and `spread` is the MID spread: the later market's midpoint minus the earlier one's, a market's midpoint being halfway between its best YES ask and its best YES bid (its YES bid is 1 minus its best NO ask). `config.time_series_mid_spread(yes_ask_a, no_ask_a, yes_ask_b, no_ask_b)` is the one definition, written as the YES-ask gap less half the difference in the two books' widths (a book's width is its YES ask plus its NO ask, less 1) — the same number as mid B − mid A, and the ask gap itself, bit for bit, whenever the two books' YES ask plus NO ask come to the same float (as every whole-cent book with no width written as decimals does, 0.30 and 0.70 say, so every fixture with such quotes keeps its pinned figures); on asks built as the book reader builds them (1 minus the other side's bid) it can differ from the ask gap in the last bit (on 648 of the 4,851 whole-cent books with no width, by at most 1.1e-16). Before DR-78 the forecast read the YES-ask gap `pB − pA`, re-priced at each size's YES fill. **Why.** The ask gap carries half the later book's width less half the earlier book's: a cost of trading, not the market's view of the odds, and on the backtest data the later book is usually the wider one, so the gap overstated the in-between chance. On the strictly tradeable backtest points of that analysis (154 ladders, each counted once) a constant `k` on the mid spread beat a constant `k` on the ask gap in 93% of whole-ladder resamples (97% with a smoothed variant), in every draw of a stratified 75/25 test in both halves of the year, with a Brier skill of about +5%. **Where it is read.** Live: `scanner._enrich_pair` writes it on each time-series pair it prices at its fills whose later book has a YES ask (`CandidatePair.mid_spread`, from the tops of the two books it already fetched, at no extra request: the best level of all four sides, two of them sides neither leg buys from — the earlier book's YES bids and the later book's NO bids, whose complement is the reference YES ask), and `strategy._kelly_p_at` reads it through `scanner.pair_mid_spread` (by type: a real, finite number above `PRICE_EPSILON`, else `None`). Backtest: `_candidate_pair` sets it from that Monday's four candle quotes (never the market record's end-of-life quotes; a walked book's enrichment writes it again from the book's top, which reproduces them), and Pass 1b's Kelly gate computes it from the same four quotes. Dashboard: the Kelly-vs-actual scatter forecasts each trade at its entry quotes' mid spread (`_entry_mid_spread`) while pricing its legs at what it paid (`_quotes_at_fills`), as the live sizer forecasts at the books' tops and prices at the fills, and the spread calibration scores the same entry mid spread (`_spread_observations`). Calibration: the backtest's empirical `k̂` (`_interval_calibration`) divides the realised in-between rate by the mean mid spread, each pair's read at its first qualifying Monday's four quotes (`CalibrationObservation.implied`), so `k̂` and the forecast's `k` are fractions of one quantity and the `k̂ − k` cards and heatmap compare them directly; its log header reads `Interval-discount calibration (k_hat = realised in-between rate / market-implied gap at the midpoints)`. **What follows from it.** `p` is the same at every order size; the prices an order pays set only the cost, the fees and Kelly's `b` (DR-62, unchanged). A time-series pair with no usable mid spread — one never priced off its books — has no forecast: `_kelly_p_at` returns `None` and `compute_trade` refuses it with one INFO line (DEBUG when quiet), `No forecast for '<title>': no usable mid spread — skipping`, or for an add-on its add-on line alone, `Not adding to held pair '<title>': no forecast (no usable mid spread)`, so the forecast's zero clamp, which would read such a pair as riskless, is never reached on the live path. Every `Trade computed` line of a time-series pair names its spread after `p` (`| p=0.66 (mid spread 0.490) kelly=…`). **A missing YES bid** on the earlier market counts as a bid of 0 (a NO ask of 1.0, so its midpoint is half its YES ask), which can only lower that midpoint and so raise the spread and lower `p` — the cautious direction, by operator decision. **The `1 − k` bound needs BOTH books uncrossed, and has two guards.** `f* = 1 − k·s/(1 − c)` with `c = pA + nB + fee` at the fills; on books neither of which is crossed by more than `PRICE_EPSILON`, the later midpoint is at least its YES bid (`≥ 1 − nB`) and the earlier at most its YES ask (`≤ pA`), so `s ≥ 1 − c + fee − PRICE_EPSILON > 1 − c` and `f* < 1 − k` (`config.max_kelly_fraction`'s docstring gives the argument; enrichment's depth bound relies on it; the backtest's `cap_free_from` does not, since it is never below the peak Kelly fraction, so no size depends on the cap above it either way). A crossed EARLIER book puts its midpoint above its YES ask and can break the bound, so enrichment now refuses it as well as a crossed later book (`ENRICH_CROSSED`, WARNING `Pair '<title>' dropped: the earlier contract's YES ask 0.3000 sits below its own YES bid 0.3500 — a crossed book`), and the backtest's Kelly gate skips a time-series Monday whose quote is crossed on either market (`pA + nA` or `pB + nB` under `1 − PRICE_EPSILON`), counted once per pair per Monday on its own silent-at-zero line, `Time-series Mondays skipped because a market's YES ask sat below its own YES bid (a crossed book) (<label>): N` (DEBUG when quiet). On uncrossed quotes that pass the fee check the mid spread is above the fee less `PRICE_EPSILON`, so above zero, and the clamp never decides a Monday there either. **What does not move.** The entry rules — positivity, the band, the tier floors, the price-sum ceiling, the fee check, `_find_entry`, and the live finder's one-best-pair contest by the largest `pB − pA` — all still read the YES-ask gap (operator decision: forecast only). Costs, fees, Kelly's `b`, the caps, budgets and the cash reserve; every `k` value (config.py's 0.80, the seed's 0.80, whatever is saved); same-title (its fixed 0.95 prior); the order path (`trader`, and `validate_pair_price`, which never re-checks `p`); the trade log's columns and the run result's fields; the cache tags (no `SETTLED_PREFILTER_CACHE_TAG` bump); the candidate pair list; and the `k̂` calibration's population (every time-series entry with a binary settlement, premise violations excluded — no crossed-quote skip there either). **What moves.** Every time-series `p`, so sizes and which pairs trade, live and in the backtest; the new crossed-book refusals (none fired on the two recorded books below); the `Trade computed` line; the empirical `k̂` (log and dashboard) and the dashboard's spread calibration, wherever a pair's two books differ in width (on books with no width the mid spread is the YES-ask gap, so the dashboard's golden fixture kept every value, and only words moved: the calibration section's caption and x-axis title, and a caption under the interval-discount section's calibration table saying its "Mean implied gap" is the mid spread); and the backtest's `SweepPoint.peak_kelly_fraction` wherever a crossed quote had set it (on the golden band sweep, 156 tier-on points, the time-series and cross-event populations whose peak was the TA/TB pair's crossed later quote — every trade, curve and check was unchanged). **Measured** (2026-10-06, offline replays of the live pipeline over the recorded books of 2026-09-27 and 2026-10-07 at the saved defaults' settings — tier floors off, band 0–0.8, `k` 0.70, a 15% per-trade cap, a 20% same-title cap — but with adding to held pairs off (held markets dropped; the saved defaults have it on), at $1,000 and $211, main and this change back to back): identical candidate pairs, enriched leg prices and depth in all four; main's code with only its forecast swapped for the mid spread reproduces this change's specs, portfolio and validations exactly; the portfolio moved from 12 to 14 trades ($954.91 → $953.74) and 10 to 11 ($200.85 → $200.50) on the September books, and from 16 to 14 ($764.96 → $693.91) and 12 to 11 ($200.91 → $200.74) on the October ones; of the tradeable time-series pairs, the mid spread sat below the top-of-book ask gap on 20 and above it on 21 of 54 (September) and below on 26 and above on 18 of 60 (October). The `k̂` calibration (2026-10-07, offline, the cumulative-wording subset of the corpus starting 2025-10-01, candles from the disk cache only, main and this change's code over the same entries): pooled `k̂` 0.852 → 0.873 at band 0–1 with the tier floors off (1,028 entries), 0.850 → 0.858 at band 0–0.8 with them off (975) and 0.891 → 0.904 at band 0–1 with them on (592), each equal to within 1e-15 to `k̂` recomputed outside the package on its own basis (in-between count over the summed YES-ask gaps, then over the summed mid spreads). **Residuals, recorded.** (1) The candle bid floor: the candle fetch clamps every NO ask into 0.01–0.99, so a backtest YES bid never reads below 0.01 where live reads a missing bid as 0; where an earlier market's YES bid is missing or under a cent, the backtest's mid spread can sit up to 0.005 below the live one, and its `p` up to `k × 0.005` above. (2) `k` is now relative to the mid spread and no `k` value changed (operator decision); on the 365-day data the empirical `k̂` reads about 0.04 higher on mids than on the ask gap (0.008 to 0.022 higher on the 2025-10-01 cumulative subset measured above), and at one `k` a pair whose later book is wider than its earlier one now forecasts a higher `p` than before at the top of the book, and so can size larger. Deeper it can be the other way round: the old forecast re-priced `p` at each size's YES fill, so once the YES leg's average fill sits more than `(wB − wA)/2` above its top (`wA` and `wB` the earlier and later books' widths), the new `p` is the lower one, and a size that deep can come out smaller than before. (3) The mid spread is read at the top of both books while the legs fill deeper: a size that walks past the top pays more, and `b` falls with it, but the forecast does not move. (4) The backtest's entry (`_find_entry`) still records a crossed Monday as a qualifying Monday; the gate never trades it, but the `k̂` calibration, which reads each pair's first qualifying Monday, still counts it, at that Monday's mid spread — and a crossed earlier quote is the one way an entry's mid spread can be zero or negative, since on any other entry the fee check keeps the later book's width under the YES-ask gap, which keeps the mid spread above half that gap. On the 2025-10-01 cumulative subset no entry's first Monday is crossed on either market and no mid spread is at or below zero (the smallest is 0.015 with the tier floors off and 0.16 with them on), so the `k̂` measured there does not depend on this. (5) The forecast reads two quotes neither leg buys from, each at the top of its book at whatever quantity rests there: the earlier market's best YES bid and the later market's best NO bid (whose complement is the reference YES ask). `scanner._bids_to_ask_levels` keeps any level above 0 contracts, fractional included, so a quote worth under a cent can set the forecast; and `validate_pair_price`, which re-reads both books just before submission, re-checks the price-sum ceiling, the edge after the fee, the later market's YES ask (that it is there, and the band's ceiling on the YES-ask gap between it and the earlier market's best YES ask) and the reachable depth, but never the earlier market's YES bid, the mid spread or `p`. A higher earlier YES bid or a lower later YES ask narrows the mid spread and so raises `p`. Measured as a change in `p` the effect looks small (under 0.09 on the recorded books below), but it can move the stake from nothing to near the cap. The final review's probes (`.git/mid-forecast/final-review/`), at the saved defaults' settings (tier floors off, band 0–0.8, `k` 0.70, a 15% cap) on $10,000, with 5,000 contracts on each leg's side, an earlier YES ask of 0.45 and a later YES ask of 0.75 beside a later YES bid of 0.72: earlier YES bids of 5,000 at 0.21 alone give a mid spread of 0.405 and no trade; with 4 contracts at 0.42 above them, 0.300, a Kelly fraction of 11.97% and a $1,196.99 stake; with 0.01 contract at 0.44 above them instead, 0.290, 14.91% and $1,490.14. Before DR-78 the forecast read one such quote, the later market's YES ask, at full weight: on the same books but with a later YES ask of 0.85 (NO bids of 5,000 at 0.15), a 0.01-contract later NO bid at 0.28 above them moved main from no trade to the full 15% cap ($1,499.28). DR-78 reads that quote at half weight and the earlier market's YES bid at half weight: the same total weight on quotes neither leg buys from, now split over two, but how far a stray quote can move the forecast depends on both books' widths. A quote can sit no further than its own book's other side, since each crossed-book check stops it there, so main's spread could move by up to the later book's width and DR-78's by up to half of each book's width: further than main's wherever the earlier book is the wider. On the probe's books with the later YES ask of 0.75 (widths 0.24 earlier, 0.03 later), one 0.01-contract quote moves main from $1,196.99 to $1,499.28 (a later NO bid at 0.28) and DR-78 from no trade to $1,490.14 (the earlier YES bid at 0.44). With the later YES ask of 0.85 (width 0.13) one later NO bid moves main to the cap, while DR-78 needs a quote on each side to reach it (an earlier YES bid alone gives $22.85, a later NO bid alone no trade). On the recorded books the earlier book is the wider at the top on 21 of the 54 tradeable time-series pairs of 2026-09-27 and 18 of the 60 of 2026-10-07, so there quotes on both sides could move DR-78's mid spread further than any quote could move main's spread, and on 6 and 7 of them a single quote could.
