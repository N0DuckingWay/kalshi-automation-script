**At most one open time-series trade per ladder (DR-76, 2026-09 — the live path here, the backtest mirror in the next paragraph).** The weekly live run dropped every market it HOLDS before pairing, but nothing stopped it opening a DIFFERENT pair on the SAME ladder in a later week while the first trade was still open. V0's week-by-week replay of the live rules (`.git/live-toggles/v0/report.md`, the decision record's item 2) found 18 trades entered while their group was still open (`v0_results.json`'s `entered_while_group_open`, summed over the 16 groups traded more than once), and $3,990 lost when stacked rungs of `KXMLBRETURN-26DETTSKUBAL29` and `KXSENATEREC-26MAY` lost together — in each, the second trade's in-between window sat inside the first's, so one outcome sank both. Stacking multiplies one question's exposure past the per-trade size bound and concentrates the book. **The definition.** A ladder is one question asked at several deadlines. Two markets are on one ladder when they share an EVENT ticker, or the time-series QUESTION key — `scanner.time_series_group_key(pair_key(m), subtitle)`, the finder's own group key (the date-stripped event title + market title + outcome label), so the same question at any deadline, across events. `scanner.ladder_keys(event_ticker, question_key)` is the one definition: a frozenset of up to two TAGGED labels, `("event", …)` and `("question", …)`, so an event ticker can never match a question, and a blank or non-string value is skipped (a `MagicMock`'s auto-attribute names no event). The question label is the group key as `scanner._ladder_question` compares it — letter case, the words "the"/"a"/"an", quote marks and `?!:;()` ignored, numbers, `$`, `%`, commas, hyphens and full stops kept — because one question listed as two events can be worded a little differently, and the group key alone kept such listings apart (PR #77 review, 2026-09-28): on the 2026-09-27 03:45 live snapshot it merges 23 of 114,375 question keys, every one the same question (22 "Who will Trump pardon?" names whose two events' keys differ only by a space before "?", and one Alaska Senate question left with an empty "( )"), and in the 365-day backtest a "the"-less relisting of "When will the Senate vote on the SAVE America Act?" (`KXVOTESAVEAMERICA-26` beside `-26MAR`). It changed none of the 1,404 simulations of the 365-day run's entries (every band x `k` x 20%/100% cap x tier setting). It can only block more, never less: it is not the finders' grouping key, which is unchanged, so two such listings still never pair with each other. `market_ladder_keys(market)` builds them for a live market and `pair_ladder_keys(pair)` is the union over a pair's two markets. Consequence of the event label, by design: two OPTIONS of one multi-choice event ("Trump" / "Vance" under one election) are one ladder, so a held option blocks a time-series pair touching any other option of that event. **Exposure, live** = the account's open positions: `get_held_positions` (whose tickers `get_held_tickers` returns) reads `count_filter="position"`, so a market leaves the held set — and its labels stop blocking — once its position is gone (assumed to be when it pays out, as the backtester already assumes — not yet observed live; if a paid-out position ever stayed in that listing, its ladder would stay blocked, and once its market leaves `/markets/{ticker}` (a 404, which `api_call_with_retry` does not retry) `resolve_held_ladders` would return None on every run, stopping all time-series trading, every run exiting `EXIT_TIME_SERIES_SKIPPED` (40) — check this on the first live positions). **The resolver.** `scanner.resolve_held_ladders(client, markets, held_tickers)` is called by `main._run_prod` right after the `_blind_run_reason` early return and BEFORE `markets = [m for m in markets if m.ticker not in blocked_tickers]` (`blocked_tickers` is every held ticker, less the two markets of each exact held pair the run adds to — DR-77, below; with `rebalance` off it is every held ticker, as before) — it reads each held market's labels from THIS run's full market list, and looks up a held market the list lacks (closed but not yet paid out, or on a trading-inactive shard) through one raw `/markets/{ticker}` read and one raw `/events/{event_ticker}` read for the event title (memoised per event), both `api_call_with_retry(fetch_json_page, …)` like `get_held_positions`, parsed by the same `_market_from_dict` so a looked-up rung's labels (outcome label included) match its listed siblings'. It logs `Open ladder exposure: N held market(s) in E event(s), asking Q question(s) (L looked up because this run's market list did not have them)` on every production run that reaches it (after the balance check and the blind-run exit) and identifies every held market, zero included (DR-66); a run that cannot identify one logs the ERROR below instead. **It fails CLOSED:** a failed read (HTTP or transport, after the retries), a non-object body, a reply with no market or event object, a market with no event ticker, or a held position with a blank ticker returns `None` with one ERROR naming the ticker (`Could not look up held market …, so no time-series trade will be made this run …`), and it stops at the FIRST such market, since the answer is already `None` and each further read could wait out ~62 s of backoff. `_run_prod` then makes NO time-series pair that run — `find_time_series_pairs` is not called at all (so its always-logged lines, `Time-series entry rule: …` included, are absent: the ERROR is the signal; and if no same-title pair survives either, the closing `No qualifying pairs found` line reads `time-series: not searched this run, because a held market could not be identified (see the ERROR above)` instead of naming a rule the run never applied — `main._no_pairs_msg(..., time_series_searched=held_ladders is not None)`) — while same-title pairing, sizing and execution go ahead, and `select_portfolio` is handed `held_ladders or frozenset()`. Fail closed because a position that cannot be placed on a ladder could be on ANY ladder, and a failed EVENT-title read in particular would build a question key that matches none of its listed siblings — a silent fail-open. Such a run exits `EXIT_TIME_SERIES_SKIPPED` (40) instead of `EXIT_OK` (unless a trade needs a human, which wins with 20), so the scheduler's own log says time-series trading was skipped; like any run that scanned, it satisfies the weekly slot and is not retried — see the BS-14 paragraph. **That lookup's type check on a 2xx body is a FAILED READ, not DR-05's money-in-flight guard**: nothing has been submitted when it parses, so it neither logs MONEY IS IN FLIGHT nor joins DR-05's three guarded sites (`trader._execute_transfer` and `v2_probe`'s two submission readers), whose rule "do not generalise the guard" is unchanged; it simply refuses to identify the market (`_http.py`'s notes say some read-only lookups check the type this way). Per-lookup WARNINGs describe the failure in one line of at most 160 characters (`scanner._error_text`: type + HTTP status and reason, or type + the first line of a status-less error's message), never an SDK exception whole (TS-02). **The finder refuses first.** `find_time_series_pairs(..., *, held_ladders=frozenset())` builds, once per group, the set of member tickers whose `ladder_keys(m.event_ticker, norm_title)` meet `held_ladders` (the group key IS each member's question, so a held QUESTION blocks the whole group, a held EVENT only that event's members), skipping the work entirely when `held_ladders` is empty, and refuses any candidate touching one FIRST in the candidate loop — except the exact held pair the run adds to (`add_on_pairs`, DR-77), which passes here and has its sides checked once its legs are ordered; any other candidate touching one of that pair's markets is refused on this same line — before the same-event branch, every DR-72 wording check, the gap cap, the spread rule and the `$1` guard, and before the one-best-pair-per-group contest. Counted on one silent-at-zero INFO line, `Time-series candidates refused because one of their markets is on a ladder we already hold (counted before every other check): N`. Three consequences: (a) a refused group's RUNNER-UP that touches no held ladder is promoted, so a diff of two runs' pairs tables is not a pure deletion (the DR-02 promotion again); (b) a candidate it refuses is counted on no other line, so on a run that holds positions `ladder_disabled_skips` and the DR-72 counts can read lower than they would otherwise; (c) with nothing held, its output and every other count are exactly what they were. **`select_portfolio` refuses within the run too, and backstops the finder.** `select_portfolio(specs, cash_cents, *, held_ladders=frozenset())` seeds `used_ladders` from `held_ladders`, refuses a TIME-SERIES spec (`pair.pair_type == "time_series"` exactly, `leg_sides`' rule) whose `pair_ladder_keys(spec.pair)` meet `used_ladders` — after the ticker check, before the cash check and any shrink — and adds EVERY selected spec's labels, same-title and shrunk specs included; a spec skipped because not one contract pair of it fits the cash left claims nothing. An add-on to a held pair (DR-77) is the one exception: it is blocked only by the ladders of specs picked earlier in this run (`picked_ladders`), because `scanner.held_pairs` adds only to a pair no other held market shares a ladder with, so the held ladders it meets are its own; a shrunk add-on is still an add-on and claims its ladders like any pick. So a same-title pick blocks a later time-series spec on its ladder while a time-series pick never blocks a same-title one (see "Pair dedup prefers same-title"), and of two time-series specs on one ladder only the higher-ranked is taken. A refused spec keeps its cash, so the greedy walk can then take a lower-ranked spec of either kind that it would otherwise have skipped, or at a larger size than it would otherwise have shrunk it to — the portfolio can lose a same-title trade, or gain a time-series one, beside the specs the rule refused (worked example in "Pair dedup prefers same-title"). One silent-at-zero INFO line, `Time-series trades skipped because the account already holds, or this run already picked, a trade on the same ladder: N`. The within-run rule applies in `_run_dev` too, since it lives in `select_portfolio`; `_run_dev` itself is unchanged — it holds nothing (see the dev-mode gotcha) and passes no held ladders. **`held_ladders` is not a `LiveSettings` field**, so `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_live_path_reads_toggles_only_through_live_settings` and `tests/test_main.py::TestLiveSettingsReachEverySite` are untouched. **Not changed:** same-title pairing (a same-title spec is never refused by this rule, though its markets' ladders count and the portfolio around it can change, as above), Kelly, fees, the spread rule, the trader, `_run_dev` (the scheduler and the exit codes gained only `EXIT_TIME_SERIES_SKIPPED`, above). **The backtest applies the same rule** — see the next paragraph. Pinned by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestLadderKeys`, `::TestResolveHeldLadders`, `::TestErrorText`, `::TestFinderRefusesHeldLadders`, `tests/test_strategy.py::TestSelectPortfolioLadders`, `tests/test_main.py::TestRunProdHeldLadders` (a run holding nothing still trades time-series; a held rung in the market list, one missing from it and looked up, and a failed lookup that stops every time-series pair while the same-title trade still goes through, or, with no same-title pair either, ends on a no-pairs line saying time-series was not searched), `::TestNoPairsMsg::test_a_run_that_did_not_search_time_series_says_so` and the AST pin `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_the_live_run_refuses_pairs_on_held_ladders` (`_run_prod` calls `resolve_held_ladders` and passes `held_ladders` to both the finder and `select_portfolio`; the finder calls `ladder_keys`, `select_portfolio` calls `pair_ladder_keys`, and `market_ladder_keys` calls `ladder_keys` and `time_series_group_key` — a dropped keyword would otherwise refuse nothing, silently, since both default to an empty set). **Known residuals.** (1) Live keeps one pair per question per run by the widest `pB − pA` BEFORE Kelly, so a group whose winner fails Kelly trades nothing that week (pre-existing; the backtest picks its best Kelly-passing candidate instead). (2) A spec dropped after `select_portfolio` refused its time-series ladder-mate — by `pre_execution_check`, `ensure_shard_collateral` (an unfunded shard), or a failed or rolled-back execution — leaves that ladder untraded that run. (3) The held set is read once, at the top of the run; a trade this run opens is covered by `select_portfolio`'s within-run rule, not by the resolver. (4) Same-title pairs can still stack on one question across weeks (out of scope by operator decision). (5) A dry run holds nothing new, so consecutive dry runs never see each other's simulated trades. (6) The within-run rule acts after the finder's one-pair-per-group choice, so a group whose best pair `select_portfolio` refuses (a higher-ranked pick shares its ladder) offers no runner-up that run, even one that shares no label with that pick — a missed trade, never a stacked one.

**The backtest mirror of DR-76: at most one open time-series trade per ladder, Monday by Monday, with retries (2026-09).** Before it the backtester could not show stacking at all: `_simulate_at_discount` kept ONE time-series pair per group for the whole window (`best_by_group`, chosen with hindsight — the look-ahead the DR-75 paragraph records), so it never stacked a second rung, but it also never re-traded a ladder after its trade paid out and never retried a pair Pass 2 could not take, both of which live does. The operator's rule for it (2026-09-27): the backtester never sells; on each Monday it may enter a pair only if nothing it still holds touches that pair's tickers or ladders; a ladder is tradeable again once its positions pay out; a pair refused one Monday can be taken on a later qualifying Monday. How it is built. (1) **One reader.** `backtester._ladder_keys_dict(m, question_key=None)` returns `scanner.ladder_keys(m.get("event_ticker"), _ts_group_key(m) if question_key is None else question_key)` — the live rule's own labels. (2) **Pass 1b keeps every Kelly-passing Monday of a time-series pair**, one candidate each, still read only through `_entry_mondays` (`time_series_profit_prob` and `pair_size_cap` still called inside `_simulate_at_discount`, so the existing AST pins hold); a same-title pair still stops at its first passing Monday. The outcome, premise and exit-date checks run ONCE per pair, on its first passing Monday's markets (a time-series pair's markets never swap between Mondays), so `premise_violations` still counts pairs, never Mondays. Each candidate carries its own `kelly_f_capped`, `entry_monthly_ratio` and `holding_days`, plus `pair_id` (the record's index in `raw_entries`) and `ladder_keys_a`/`ladder_keys_b`. (3) **`best_by_group` is same-title only**; time-series candidates pass straight through, and `_drop_cross_type_duplicates` and the sort are unchanged — so at an EXACT tie (entry date and ratio) time-series candidates now keep their raw order rather than their group's first-seen order. (4) **Pass 2** skips a candidate whose `pair_id` already traded (FIRST, so a traded pair's later Mondays are never counted as ladder refusals), then applies the existing ticker check, then — time-series only — skips a candidate while any label of either of its markets is held by an open trade of EITHER type; every trade adds both markets' labels, each released at THAT market's `settled_date` with the same `<= d` rule as tickers and cash (so a cross-event pair's earlier-paying market frees its event while the shared question stays held until the later one pays out). Cash, checkpoint sizing, ticker release and the shrink loop are unchanged by this rule. One silent-at-zero line per simulation, at the simulation's own level (DEBUG when `quiet`, so reading every lazy cap cell adds nothing to the INFO log): `Time-series pairs skipped on a Monday because we still held a trade on the same ladder (<the completion line's label>): N` — N counts pair-Mondays, so a pair refused on three Mondays counts 3; the completion line is built from the same label and is byte-identical. (5) **`SweepPoint.peak_kelly_fraction` is now the largest uncapped `f*` over every Monday a pair may be traded on — every Monday a time-series pair passes the Kelly gate, a same-title pair's first, and, with `add_to_held`, a same-title pair's later same-legs Mondays (without it, a same-title pair's later Mondays are never scored)** — still taken before the outcome and premise checks, still cap-independent — **and that is REQUIRED for `CapSweep`'s reuse rule to stay exact.** A time-series pair can now be traded on any of its passing Mondays, and WHICH one depends on the walk (the cash left, the ladders in use), which depends on the cap: at `k` 0.40 with no cap, a pair that does not fit beside another on 2026-01-05 enters on 01-12, while at caps of 20% and 45% both fit on 01-05 (`TestOpenLadderExposure`'s cash-retry fixture, checked 2026-09-27 on a $10,000 balance, when Pass 2 still skipped a trade its cash could not afford whole; since 2026-09-29 it shrinks the trade to the cash left, so a pair waits for cash only when its budget buys no contract pair that still pays in a win after the exact fees — which still depends on the cap — and that fixture now runs on $1.20). **Adding to a held pair in the backtest (DR-77) — `_simulate_at_discount(add_to_held=True)`, off unless a caller asks; the dashboard's Rebalance family is the caller, always together with `trim_to_kelly`.** With `add_to_held` the cash walk may add to a pair it still holds, as the live sizer does for a held pair. **What adds:** a candidate whose pair has an open trade, with neither market paid out that Monday, trades again as a new `BacktestTrade` with `add_on=True` (the same two markets bought the same way round, its own count, cost, fees and payout, the same exit date; the open trade is never changed). A time-series pair's later passing Mondays are candidates already (DR-76); a same-title pair also keeps its later passing Mondays whose legs are its first one's, the same way round, as add-on-only candidates, which never contest the one-pair-per-group choice (the winner's are kept, a loser's dropped) and never open a pair. **Sizing:** Pass 2 first asks `config.held_pair_fraction(kelly_f_capped, the pair's stake, the checkpoint's portfolio value)` and skips a pair already at its share; `_size_trade` then hands `compute_trade` a `HeldPair` carrying the stake, which sizes the add-on as live does — `held_pair_fraction` on its own capped fraction, then the one `config.kelly_budget(portfolio value, fraction, cash)` every trade uses: Kelly sizes the whole position as a share of the portfolio value (the checkpoint's cash plus every open trade at market, fees left out, as the equity curve carries them), the pair's stake being its open trades at market plus the fees paid for them (`held["trades"]` keeps each of the pair's trades with what it spent, fees included, and the stake is the sum of what each spent plus its change in value since: `paid + (_open_value(t, d) − t.total_cost)`, which without quotes is what it spent), and an add-on spends at most the cash left and never more than a new pair would; a pair already at its share is skipped. **Refusals:** another open trade holding one of its ladder labels, or two markets sharing no label (live, `scanner.held_pairs` adds only to two held markets that share a label and no other held market shares); a new time-series pair on a held ladder is still refused as DR-76 has it. **The rebalance gate:** an add-on is a rebalance, so after the ladder checks and before its size it waits as a Kelly trim does — `REBALANCE_MIN_DAYS_SINCE_TRADE` calendar days since the last trade on what it adds to (`config.rebalance_rested`), and, with `min_move` set, a move of the price at least that far, up or down, since then (`config.price_move_reached`; the pair's total price through `_held_pair_move`, a lone leg's own market through `_lone_leg_move`); at weekly checkpoints the shipped 7 days hold none back (see the paragraph on the Rebalance rules). Five outcomes each have a silent-at-zero line, DEBUG when `quiet`: `Trades that added to a held pair (…)`, `Adds skipped because the held pair is already at its target size (…)`, `Adds skipped because another open trade is on the same ladder, or the two markets share no ladder (…)`, `Adds skipped because the held pair traded fewer than N days before (…)` and `Adds skipped because the held pair's price had not moved Nc since its last trade (…)`; an add-on skipped for any other reason — one of its markets already paid out, a budget (the cash left binding it) that buys not one contract pair, fees eating the profit — is not counted (except that, over a walked book, a refusal by the walk, or no size fitting, is counted on the `Trades refused when their book was walked` line, and one the cash left cannot buy one contract pair of on the `Trades skipped with no cash left for one contract pair` line, as for any trade). The completion line's label ends `, adding to held pairs`, and the point is stamped `SweepPoint.add_to_held`. **Why the size-cap sweep stays exact:** the cap reaches an add-on only through `min(pair cap, f*)`, as it reaches any trade, and `peak_kelly_fraction` covers every Monday an add-on may be made on (a same-title pair's later same-legs Mondays included), so at any cap at or above the peak (over a walked book, `max(peak, cap_free_from)`) the whole walk is the same. `CapSweep.add_to_held` forwards the setting to every simulation and check, and `CapSweep.end_dates` gives a cell with no eager point its end day, so the Rebalance family, built with `eager={}`, simulates each cap until one reaches its peak and shares that one above. Such a sweep refuses what would make it silently wrong: built with eager points (a non-empty `eager`, or a `same_title_eager`) it raises `ValueError`, since those points never added and would be handed back as its own; and a cell `end_dates` has no day for raises `ValueError` naming the (band, `k`, population) — the keys must be the exact band and `k` objects the cells are read with — rather than ending that cell's curves on whatever day it is read. **Off unless asked for:** the default is `False` (`_sim_options` forwards it only when `True`), and no eager run sets it, so every backtest result and log line is what it was; only the Rebalance family (see its paragraph), always with `trim_to_kelly` on too, simulates with it on. **Why the stake counts the fees (operator decision, 2026-09-30):** fees leave the portfolio value the day they are paid (they buy nothing that can be sold on), so a stake that left them out would find a held pair short of its share by about (1 − its fraction) × those fees on every later Monday it passes, and top it up by about a contract pair each time, each top-up paying a rounded-up fee of its own. Counted in the stake, they tip the check the other way: at the quotes it was bought at, a pair bought at its full Kelly share adds nothing, and an add-on comes only from a larger Kelly share (better quotes, or a larger portfolio value after another trade paid out) or from a first trade the cash left had shrunk — and can still be small. Live sizing counts the fees the same way (`HeldPair.stake_dollars`). **Measured on the golden fixture** (2026-09-30, synthetic data, `apply_pre_toggle_defaults`, band 0–1, $10,000, 2026-01-01 to 2026-04-01): 31 add-on trades over its 3 `k` × 20 caps (11 at `k` 0.5, 20 at 0.75, 0 at 1.0), with open trades valued at market (71 at cost). **Adding to a held pair (DR-77) — switched on by the live toggle `rebalance`, together with the Kelly trim.** A production run with `LiveSettings.rebalance` on (the saved live defaults', or `main.py --rebalance` for one run; config.py's `REBALANCE` and the seed ship it on, but a saved file that leaves the key out reads it as off — see the saved-live-defaults paragraph) may add to a pair the account already holds: exactly the same two markets, buying the same side on each, judged by every rule any other trade is judged by, and sized so that Kelly covers the whole position. A live add-on waits as a trim does (`seller.plan_add_ons`, called by `main._run_prod` after the held pairs are found and before both finders): at least `REBALANCE_MIN_DAYS_SINCE_TRADE` calendar days since the last fill on its markets, a fill by hand included, and, with `min_price_move`, a move of at least that much, up or down, in its price since that fill (an exact pair's two held asks added, a lone leg's one market's ask; the price then from hourly candles, the price now from this run's market list); with `rebalance_min_days_to_maturity` ("Rebalance until") set, a pair whose later market (a lone leg's own) closes in fewer than that many days from today (UTC dates) is held back first, before any read; a pair held back, or one whose fills or prices cannot be read, is left out of `add_on_pairs`, so its markets stay blocked. With it off, a held market is never traded again, as DR-76 describes. `scanner.get_held_positions` is the one reader of the positions listing: each held market's signed count, `market_exposure_dollars` and `fees_paid_dollars` (`HeldPosition`; a count or cost it cannot read is `None`, and a ticker listed on two rows stays held with its count and costs `None`, so it is never paired; a flat row whose ticker is a JSON list or object is skipped, as before), with the held set exactly the one `get_held_tickers` returned before — `get_held_tickers` is now `set(get_held_positions(...))`, with no caller on the live path, pinned against a reference copy of that per-row rule by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestGetHeldPositions::test_the_held_set_matches_the_reference_rule` — and its keyword-only `complete_out` set `False` on entry and then to whether the walk read the listing to its end (a cursor guard or `SCANNER_MAX_PAGES` stopping it early reads `False`). `resolve_held_ladders(..., labels_out=)` hands back each identified held market's ladder labels, and `scanner.held_pairs(positions, labels_by_ticker, markets_by_ticker)` joins held markets that share a label: a group of exactly two with opposite, equal counts (compared exactly) and readable costs, each exposure at least `MIN_ACTIVE_PRICE_DOLLARS` × its count, is a `HeldPair` (its `sides`, `count`, `cost_dollars` — both exposures plus fees paid, named in the logs — `value_dollars` — the pair's worth at today's prices, fees left out, also named in the logs: each market's contracts times the ask of the side held there, from the run's full market list (`markets_by_ticker`, built before held markets are dropped), or the market's exposure when the list lacks it or its ask is not a number strictly between 0 and 1 — and `fees_dollars` — both legs' fees paid — with the property `stake_dollars`, that worth plus those fees, what sizing and the at-cap check read); every other shape (one leg, three held markets on one ladder, unequal counts, one side twice, an unreadable count or cost) is never added to, and it returns no pair at all when any held market is missing from the labels or has none (a market whose ladder is unknown could share one with a pair). It logs either `Held pairs to add to: none (a held market's ladder is unknown)` or one line per pair, such as `Held pair to add to: YES A / NO B, 30 contracts each, cost $18.90 (fees $0.90), worth $22.50 at today's prices` (the cost includes the fees), and then `Held pairs to add to: N (other held markets, never added to: M)`. Both finders take a keyword-only `add_on_pairs` (`{frozenset of two tickers: HeldPair}`): the time-series finder lets the exact pair past its held-ladder refusal, refuses on that same line any other candidate touching one of the pair's markets, and checks the pair's sides with `HeldPair.matches` after a ladder may have swapped its legs; the same-title finder refuses every other candidate touching one of the pair's markets before any other check (so an unheld pair of the group can still win the group's slot), and refuses the pair itself when its prices have crossed so it would buy the other way round. With no held pair to add to, neither finder looks one up. Each refusal has its own silent-at-zero line, the time-series finder logs `Time-series pairs that add to a held pair: N` whenever it is handed any, and a kept pair carries the `HeldPair` on `CandidatePair.held` (copied through enrichment and `compute_trade` by `dc_replace`), read only through `scanner.pair_held`, by type. `strategy._evaluate_size` sizes an add-on on its whole position through `config.held_pair_fraction`, the one definition, before the `config.kelly_budget` every trade uses: it stakes what the held pair's stake — its worth today plus the fees paid for it — is missing of `min(f*, cap)` of the portfolio value, and like every trade never more than the cash, so its budget is `min(fraction × portfolio value − the pair's stake, cash)`, never more than a new pair's, and `config.max_kelly_fraction` still bounds every size; a held pair that has gained value gets a smaller add-on, one that has lost value a larger one; `compute_trade` logs one INFO line when it sizes an add-on to nothing, once per pair — `Not adding to held pair '…': it already holds its Kelly share (30 contracts each, worth $30.00 at today's prices, fees paid $0.90, portfolio value $211.00)` when `held_pair_fraction` refused it at the smallest size, `Not adding to held pair '…': no size fits this run` for any other refusal — and its `Trade computed` line ends ` | adds to N held` for an add-on (every other line is unchanged). `select_portfolio` blocks an add-on only by the ladders of this run's earlier picks (`picked_ladders`), because `held_pairs` adds only to a pair no other held market shares a ladder with — and only a spec whose `HeldPair` `matches` its own two markets and sides counts as one; every ordinary time-series spec on a held ladder is refused as before, and a spec shrunk to the cash left keeps its `HeldPair` and stays an add-on. **How `main._run_prod` switches adding to held pairs on (DR-77).** It reads `get_held_positions(client, complete_out=held_listing)` (its keys are the held tickers `get_held_tickers` would return; only a run past the $50 minimum reads it), checks Kalshi's value of the open positions against it (`_checked_positions_value`, see risk (4) of the portfolio-value paragraph), reads `resolve_held_ladders(..., labels_out=held_labels)` and builds `markets_by_ticker` (every market of the run by ticker, before held markets are dropped). With `settings.rebalance` on, and only when `held_ladders` is not `None` (every held market identified), `held_listing["complete"] is True` (the listing read to its end) and, when anything is held, Kalshi's value of the open positions was read and kept, it calls `held_pairs(held_positions, held_labels, markets_by_ticker)`; otherwise it logs one WARNING, `Not adding to held pairs this run: a market the account holds could not be looked up`, `… the list of the account's positions was cut short, so a held market may be missing from it`, `… Kalshi's value of the open positions could not be read, so the portfolio value counts only the cash` or `… Kalshi's value of the open positions was refused, so the portfolio value counts only the cash`, and adds to nothing: an unknown or unlisted held market could share a pair's ladder, and being alone on its ladder is what makes an add-on safe, while a portfolio value that counts only the cash leaves out the very positions an add-on tops up. Holding nothing, the last two never fire. It then leaves out every pair with no room even at the run's per-trade cap (`held_pair_fraction(settings.size_cap, pair.stake_dollars, portfolio_value_cents / 100) <= 0`: the pair's worth at today's prices plus the fees paid for it already fill its cap's share of the portfolio value), logging `Held pairs already at their size cap, not added to this run: N`, so such a pair's markets stay blocked like any other held market's and cannot take their same-title group's one slot only to size to nothing; it then drops every pair picked this run for a take-profit sale or a Kelly trim (`Held pairs picked for sale this run, not added to: N`, `Held pairs picked for a Kelly trim this run, not added to: N`), and last hands the rest to `seller.plan_add_ons(client, add_on_pairs, markets_by_ticker, settings=settings, now=…)`, which keeps only the pairs whose later market closes at least `settings.rebalance_min_days_to_maturity` days from today ("Rebalance until", asked first, before anything is read; a pair held back logs `Not adding to …: its later market closes in N day(s), and rebalancing stops M day(s) before maturity`), then those that have waited `REBALANCE_MIN_DAYS_SINCE_TRADE` calendar days since the last fill on their markets and, with `settings.min_price_move`, whose price has moved that far, up or down, since that fill; a pair held back, or whose fills or prices cannot be read, is left out, so its markets stay blocked; `add_on_tickers` is built from that same, final `add_on_pairs` — the dict both finders then get as `add_on_pairs=add_on_pairs` — and `blocked_tickers` is the held tickers less those markets. `blocked_tickers` is the only thing that keeps every other held market out of both finders (the same-title finder has no ladder refusal of its own), so a set built from `held_pairs`' result, or before the at-cap drop or `plan_add_ons`, would let a full or held-back pair's markets into an ordinary pair. With rebalancing off, `add_on_pairs` is `{}` and `held_pairs` is never called, so every pair-finding, sizing and order line is that of a run that never adds to a held pair, and the settings text (the `Live settings:` line, the trade-log run note, the run result, the defaults server's tables, `live_settings_argv`) reads `rebalance no` / `--no-rebalance`. A dev run reads no positions (see the dev-mode gotcha), so the setting changes nothing there, on or off. The defaults server's confirmation page keeps the saved value (the seed's, with none saved) when a link leaves the field out; the dashboard's save button sends the choice only from a page that shows it. A link carrying the seed's note that leaves the field out takes the seed's value instead, so an out-of-date seed link still proposes exactly the seed, with the change shown.

**Why adding to a held pair is safe (DR-77).** Both finders' exemption and `select_portfolio`'s rest on one guarantee: a `HeldPair` is built only by `held_pairs`, which returns one only when its markets (two for an exact pair, one for a lone leg) are alone on their ladder and every held market's ladder is known; `pair_held` reads it by type, and `HeldPair.matches` checks both tickers and the side on each after a ladder may have swapped its legs (a same-title pair whose prices have crossed, so it would buy the other way round, is refused). Just before the NO leg is sent the trader checks again that the legs and the positions still match the held pair exactly (check 2, the next paragraph). **Kelly on the whole position.** `config.held_pair_fraction` makes an add-on stake what the held pair's stake — its worth today plus the fees paid for it — is missing of `min(f*, cap)` of the portfolio value (the cash plus Kalshi's value of the open positions), and like every trade it spends at most the cash, so it never stakes more than a new pair would, `config.max_kelly_fraction` still bounds every size and `live_rule_warnings` needs no new sentence. **The value it sizes on is checked.** Kalshi's value of the open positions counts only when the contracts held can back it (`main._checked_positions_value`), and when it is unread or refused the run adds to no held pair, so an add-on is never sized on a value the run could not read or one that overstates what the account holds. A value of 0 is always kept: it counts the positions as nothing, which can only make an add-on smaller. **Rollbacks touch only the new trade** (by count and by confirmed fill), and every alert names the older holding (next paragraph). **Residuals.** A same-title held pair with room at the per-trade cap but not at the lower same-title cap, or whose stake (its worth at today's prices plus its fees) already holds its Kelly share, can still take its group's one slot and size to nothing (`compute_trade` logs it), so a run never trades two pairs of one same-title group, as before. `market_exposure_dollars` is documented only as "Cost of the aggregate market position in dollars"; that it excludes fees, is positive for a NO position and falls with a partial close is inferred, and the code fails closed on a missing, unreadable, negative or implausibly small value (below `MIN_ACTIVE_PRICE_DOLLARS` × the count). The portfolio value counts the positions at Kalshi's own valuation while a held pair counts at the ask of the side held, so the two need not agree; a held market whose ask is missing, 0 or 1 counts at its exposure, not at a settled price, and its fees still count in the stake (`tests/test_scanner.py::TestHeldPairs::test_a_leg_with_no_usable_ask_still_counts_its_fees_in_the_stake`). The stake counts the fees paid for the pair while the portfolio value leaves them out (they are spent), so the check leans toward adding less: at the prices it was bought at, a pair bought at its full Kelly share adds nothing (a stake without the fees would top it up by about a contract pair on every later run, each paying a rounded-up fee of its own; see the backtest add-on paragraph); an add-on comes from a larger Kelly share, a pair that lost value, or a first trade the cash had shrunk. **An add-on is sent only onto exactly the pair it adds to, and its rollback touches only the new contracts (DR-77, order time).** Kalshi nets every trade on a market into one position, with no lots, so "only the new trade" is kept by count and by confirmation, never by a position read: the unwind is always `no_leg.count` (this pair's), reduce-only and immediate-or-cancel, and it is sent only after THIS order's NO fill is confirmed (a 2xx full fill, or a position delta of exactly −`no_leg.count`), so a held −30 that this pair took to −40 goes back to −30 and never below. With an older position on the NO leg's market, `reduce_only` no longer makes a mistaken unwind harmless — it would close the older contracts — so those confirmation gates are load-bearing: do not loosen them, and never size an unwind from a position read. **Check 2:** in `trader._execute_legs`, right after both baselines are read and before the NO leg's POST (so no pacer place is taken and nothing is sent), `trader._add_on_mismatch(spec, no_leg, yes_leg, before_no, before_yes)` refuses an add-on (`scanner.pair_held(spec.pair)` not `None`) whose NO leg is not on the ticker held NO, whose YES leg is not on the ticker held YES, either of whose positions could not be read or did not read as a finite number, or whose positions no longer read the held count (−count on the NO market, +count on the YES market): one WARNING (`Not sending '…' (A=… B=…): it adds to a pair the account holds, but <reason>. Nothing was sent.`, the reason e.g. `the account does not hold NO on T` or `T now holds 20 NO contracts; the held pair had 30 NO contracts`) and `status="failed"`, error `not sent: this does not match the held pair it adds to (<reason>); nothing submitted`. The held count it compares with is the one read from the positions listing at the start of the run, so a trade by anyone else on either market since then (by hand, or another client) makes the add-on "failed" with nothing sent, never a resized one. Nothing in the same run can change it: `select_portfolio` takes a spec only if neither of its tickers was used by an earlier pick, so no other pair of the run trades either market before the add-on's NO leg goes out. A spec `select_portfolio` shrank to the cash left is checked the same way: the check compares the held counts, never the new count, and the unwind is the shrunk count. It returns `None` for every ordinary pair, and it runs after the disproven-latch stop, which stays first. **Adding to a lone held leg (DR-77, PR #88 review, 2026-09-30).** When one market of a held pair pays out (its position leaves the positions listing) while the other is still open, the open one is still a held market — it stays in the held set and keeps blocking its ladder (DR-76) — and, with `rebalance` on, it is a **lone leg** a run may add to (operator decision). `scanner.held_pairs` returns it as a `HeldPair` with ONE side (`HeldPair.lone`, keyed by a one-ticker frozenset in `add_on_pairs`): a held market alone on its ladder (no other held market shares a label, every held market's ladder known), a readable count, exposure and fees, and an exposure of at least the finest price times the count. It is valued like a pair's leg (`_held_leg_worth`: the ask of the side held, or its exposure with no usable ask), and its stake is that worth plus that market's own fees — the partner's payout is already cash, so it counts nothing. A candidate adds to it when it buys the held market on its held side beside a market the account does not hold (`HeldPair.matches`; the finders' lookup is `scanner._add_on_for`, which refuses a candidate touching two held markets that are not one exact pair), and, on the time-series finder and in `select_portfolio`, only when that new market meets no held ladder but the lone leg's own (`HeldPair.labels`): otherwise a new market on another held market's event would stack a trade beside it. Sizing, the at-cap drop and the R5/R6 fail-closed rules are the pair's (`held_pair_fraction` on `stake_dollars`). At order time `trader._add_on_mismatch` requires the held market to still read its held count and the new market to read nothing (`"<ticker> now holds …; it was not held"`). Log lines: `Held market to add to (its partner has paid out): NO <ticker>, N contracts, cost $C (fees $F), worth $W at today's prices`, and, silent at zero, `Held markets to add to whose partner has paid out: N`; the `Held pairs to add to: N (other held markets, never added to: M)` line counts exact pairs only and leaves lone legs out of M. Its add-on waits as a pair's does (`seller.plan_add_ons`), counted from the latest fill on its own market and on any market of its ladder in the run's list (`seller._last_fill`, the day a Kelly trim of the lone leg counts its wait and price move from too): a partner sold by hand leaves the positions listing just as a paid-out one does, so the open market reads as a lone leg, and the finders could pair it with that partner again; counting the partner's fill keeps the bot from buying it back within the wait. A lone leg whose partner paid out $0 is also trimmed when it outgrows its Kelly share (the live-trimming paragraph), by the pair's own rule. **Shapes it does not cover:** a partner that has CLOSED but not yet paid out is still in the positions listing, so the two still form an exact pair the finders cannot trade (the closed market is not in the run's list) and the open market stays blocked until the payout; a lone held market that was never one leg of a pair (an orphan left by a `rollback_failed`, or a position bought by hand) cannot be told apart from one whose partner paid out, and is added to the same way; and a lone leg that has been added to is no longer lone (its new partner is held), so it is added to again only as an exact pair — never, since the counts differ — until that partner pays out too. **Backtest mirror:** `_simulate_at_discount(add_to_held=True)` keeps each open leg (`open_legs`: its trade, side, ladder labels and the days its market and its partner pay out), and a candidate whose one held market has only legs whose partners have paid out, all on the side the candidate buys there, and whose other market no open trade holds, adds to it (`_lone_leg_records`), refused on the add-on ladder line when another open trade holds a ladder label of either market; its stake is that leg alone in each of its open trades, at market plus its exact fee (`_open_leg_stake`, through `_leg_mark`, now the one reader of a checkpoint quote, which `_open_value` also reads through). It is sized through `_size_trade` like any trade, so the cap reaches it as it reaches any trade and `CapSweep`'s reuse rule (the sharing floor) is unchanged.
