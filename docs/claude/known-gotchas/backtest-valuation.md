**The equity curve is a PORTFOLIO VALUE, not a cash balance — open positions are carried at COST (DR-61, fixed 2026-09-16).** `backtester._build_equity_curve` used to accumulate cash alone (`-= total_cost + fees` on the entry date, `+= actual_payoff` on the exit date), so an open position was carried at **ZERO** for its whole holding period and the curve dived on entry and recovered at settlement *whether the trade won or lost*. It now tracks cash PLUS the cost basis of everything still open, which leaves a trade exactly two moves: **`-fees` on the entry date** (cash falls by `total_cost + fees`, the contracts enter at `total_cost`) and **`+(actual_payoff − total_cost)` on the exit date** (the position is written off at cost, the gross receipt credited). The two sum to the trade's own `profit`, so **`total_return`, the final balance and the trade list are identical to within float noise — only the PATH between entry and settlement moves.** ("Identical to the last cent", not literally byte-identical: the position accumulator adds `+cost` on entry days and `−cost` on exit days, so when entries and exits interleave the pair need not cancel to exactly `0.0` in IEEE754. Fuzzed worst case ~4e-12 on a $10,000 balance — under a ULP of the rendered `%.2f`, which is why the parity test uses `pytest.approx(abs=1e-9)` rather than `==`.) That endpoint invariance has one unstated precondition: every trade must settle inside `[start_date, today]`. It does in production — `exit_date` comes from the `settlement_ts` of two already-settled markets — but a trade exiting past the curve's last row would stay carried at cost forever, ending at `initial − fees` where the cash-only curve ended at `initial − cost − fees`. Fees are deliberately NOT capitalised into the carrying value: they buy nothing that can be sold on, so they land on the day they are charged. **Why it mattered:** that series is the sole input to the "Max Drawdown" KPI, the "Drawdown (%)" chart, `_sharpe`/`_sortino` (via the derived `daily_return` column), the per-`k` sweep table's drawdown and Sharpe columns, and the benchmark row that sits in the SAME COLUMN as `^GSPC`'s genuine mark-to-market drawdown — so every one of them was reporting capital DEPLOYMENT as loss. The real 2026-05-01 run is the proof: its **`k = 1.00` point had 3 trades, all 3 profitable and a +4.8% return, and rendered "Max Drawdown −60.0%"**, while its `k = 0.40` point rendered **−100.0% (total ruin) against a final balance of $4,655.87**; the decoded `k = 1.00` trace was flat at $10,000 through 2026-05-24, ONE declining step to $4,001.03 on 2026-05-25 (a pure deployment day), then monotonically non-decreasing. The primary `k = 0.750` card meanwhile showed Sharpe 0.73 and Sortino 2.09 beside a Total Return of −27.0% — the same inversion from the other end. **The accepted cost** is that carrying at cost is not a true daily mark-to-market: an unrealized swing inside the holding period is invisible, so the reported drawdown is REALIZED drawdown and a lower bound on the intraperiod one. Mark-to-market was considered and rejected as materially larger, but **not because the data is missing**: `backtester._fetch_candles_parallel` already fetches each leg's WHOLE hourly series (from the later of `start_date` midnight UTC and the market's own `open_time`, floored to the hour, through one day past its close) and disk-caches it per ticker (a window longer than Kalshi's 5,000-candle-per-request cap is paged — see the scenario-explorer paragraph above). What mark-to-market actually costs is PLUMBING plus a policy — `_find_entry` returns entry-checkpoint prices only, so `candles_by_ticker` lives on the `_Candidates` object only until the last entry pass (it dies with `_prepare_entries` on `run_backtest`'s path, and `_sweep_from_candidates` deletes it before its first simulation), and a per-day series would have to be threaded through `raw_entries` and `_simulate_at_discount` into `_build_equity_curve`, with a rule for the days a leg has no candle. Price it as a data-threading change, never as a new multi-hour fetch. Cost-basis carry is the minimal change that makes each metric mean what its label says; if it is ever revisited, the direction is a strict refinement of this, not a return to cash-only. **DR-03 is untouched and is the constraint any future change here must keep**: the curve still opens one day BEFORE `start_date` at the untouched initial balance, and that row is still what keeps the `cummax` peak at the initial balance rather than at the already-charged day-0 value (see the DR-03 sentences above for the measured before/after). **Every backtest drawdown, Sharpe and Sortino figure ever recorded before this change measured peak deployment and does not reproduce** — including the DR-03/DR-56 fixture log in the paragraph above, which is now marked historical. `_simulate_at_discount`'s own Pass-2 accounting (`cash`, `open_trades`, `checkpoint_value`) is SIZING and is kept apart from the curve: the equity curve is reporting-only, and the two must not be unified. Since 2026-09-29 Pass 2 values its open trades the way the curve does — at cost until 2026-09-30, at market since — when it sizes each Monday on the portfolio value (see the portfolio-value paragraph under Key Patterns); it keeps its own running figures rather than reading the curve. With `add_to_held`, an add-on is sized on that same portfolio value, while its pair's stake is the pair's open trades at that same valuation plus the fees paid for them, which the value has already lost; `tests/test_backtester.py::TestAddToHeldPairs::test_the_account_value_is_the_equity_curve_s` pins the portfolio value an add-on is sized on to the curve's value at the close before its Monday (its trades have no quotes, so both are at cost). **This paragraph is the history: since 2026-09-30 an open trade with quotes is valued at market (next paragraph), the strict refinement it anticipated.**

**Open trades are valued at market, on the curve and in sizing (user decision, 2026-09-30).** The backtest values an open trade the way live values a held pair: each leg at the ask of the side it holds (a YES leg at the YES ask, a NO leg at the NO ask), the latest usable one on an hourly candle at or before the moment — the end of each UTC day for the curve, the entry checkpoint for sizing; at its payout (1 or 0 a contract) once its market has paid out; and at the price it paid (`_paid_prices`) only before its side has had a usable ask. A usable ask is a number strictly between 0 and 1 (`backtester._usable_ask`, the rule live's `scanner._held_leg_worth` applies to a held pair's ask), and for a NO ask also below 0.99 (`backtester._CANDLE_NO_ASK_CEILING`): the fetch stores a candle's NO ask as 1 − its YES bid clamped into 0.01–0.99, so 0.99 is also what an empty YES-bid book reads as. A side keeps its last usable ask through anything else — an empty YES ask book (1.00), an unreadable candle, a NO ask at the clamp, the time between its last candle and its payout — so a nearly won leg is not marked back down to the price it paid, and a sub-cent ask on a fine grid counts as the price it is. The prices come from the candles the backtest already fetches, so nothing new is fetched: `backtester._attach_leg_quotes`, the one writer, samples them into one `LegQuotes` per market (its day-end and checkpoint asks and its payouts, with the bids and paid-out markers the sell rule reads and the 24-hour volumes a modeled book reads; no candle list is kept, about 47 bytes per market per day of its life) and stores the pair's two on each entry record as `rec["leg_quotes"]`, in `_prepare_entries` after its entry pass and in `_sweep_from_candidates` just before the candles are released; `_simulate_at_discount`'s Pass 1b, the one reader, hands each trade its two markets' quotes as `BacktestTrade.marks` (not compared and not shown, but read by the dashboard's `_list_key` through each `LegQuotes.fingerprint`, so two trade lists equal but for their quotes, which draw different curves, never share a chunk). `_open_value(trade, day)` is the one valuation at a checkpoint (Pass 2's portfolio value, and an add-on's stake), and `_open_value_path(trade)` the one day-end path, from which `_carry_steps` (what the curve carries in the trade) and `_value_steps` (what it adds to the portfolio value each day) are built; `_build_equity_curve`, `dashboard._return_by_trade_type` and `dashboard._carried_on_days` (what the risk-free hurdle charges) all read them, pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_open_trades_are_valued_through_one_definition` (one writer and one reader of `leg_quotes`, and every reader of the quotes through those helpers). A trade with no quotes (`marks` is `None`: every hand-built trade, and a pair whose markets have no candles) is valued exactly as before, at cost: its steps are the two at-cost steps, bit for bit, and the equivalence check run for this change (the code with `_attach_leg_quotes` switched off against the code before it, on the golden fixture with every family and cap cell, the ladder and cap-bound fixtures, four seeded corpora and a corpus whose prices move, comparing trades, curves, checks, logs and the dashboard page's bytes) found no difference. What does not change: a trade's steps still sum to its profit, so every curve still ends at the start plus every trade's profit, and DR-03's leading row stays. What changes: a swing while a trade is open now shows in the drawdown, Sharpe and Sortino, the trades themselves move through sizing (a portfolio value that moves with the market), and the curves change on most days a trade is open, so the page carries more points. Every curve stays daily, by the user's choice (2026-09-30; the explorer still turns weekly only above 400 rows, `dashboard._EQUITY_DAILY_MAX_ROWS`, as at cost), so on the test fixtures the filter page grows 823,634 → 1,397,952 bytes (+70%; 897,445 → 1,342,624, +50%, re-measured 2026-10-10 with the check-box menus, where a category · tag view ships its curve once, as `m`) and a full 20-cap explorer grid's blocks 249,188 → 7,608,372 bytes (`tests/test_dashboard.py::TestFilterPageSize`, `::TestExplorerFullGrid`); the quoted page-size budgets are their measurements + 20%, with no ceiling. A real 365-day page has not been measured at market. Pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestLegQuotes`, `::TestSizesAtMarket`, `::TestSameTitleFlipAtMarket`, `::TestAddOnStakeAtMarket`, `::TestMarketCapSweepParity` (every cap of the size-cap, tier-off and Rebalance sweeps equals a fresh simulation on a corpus whose prices move), `::TestUnquotedExactness`, and `tests/test_dashboard.py::TestOpenTradesAtMarket`. Known limits, recorded: a quote has no age limit, so a leg that stops trading keeps its last usable ask until it pays out (a flat line, then a jump; a DEBUG count, silent at zero, names the legs valued on a quote more than 7 days old), and that covers a nearly won leg whose book has emptied, which stays at its last usable ask (a YES leg below 1.00, a NO leg below 0.99) rather than its payout until it pays out; the ask overstates what a leg would sell for, by the spread; a real NO ask of 0.99 cannot be told from the clamp, so it is not used either; candles end one day after a market's close, so a leg that pays out later is valued on its last usable ask until then; and a swing inside one day is not seen. **Every backtest result recorded before 2026-09-30 valued open trades at cost**, in sizing and on the curve, so its sizes, drawdowns, Sharpe and Sortino are not comparable.

**The backtest walks a modeled order book (2026-10, backtest-only).** Kalshi keeps no historical order books, only hourly candles, so the backtest used to fill every trade at one candle price, in any size. It now sizes each trade over a modeled book, with the live bot's own code. **The depth table.** `python3 -m kalshi_betting.depth_model snapshot` saves the live books of up to `config.DEPTH_SNAPSHOT_MARKETS` open non-combo markets, each with its 24-hour volume read from its recent candles (read-only GETs, under `backtest_cache/depth_snapshots/`). `depth_model.fit` builds a table from every saved snapshot: for each 24-hour volume bucket (`config.DEPTH_MODEL_VOLUME_EDGES`) and best-bid price band (`DEPTH_MODEL_PRICE_EDGES`), the median contracts resting within each distance of the best bid (`DEPTH_MODEL_DISTANCES`, 0 to 20c). One table serves buys and sales, since buying YES walks the NO bids and selling YES walks the YES bids. A cell with fewer than `DEPTH_MODEL_MIN_LADDERS` ladders reads its volume row, then the whole table. `backtest.py` fits it before the fetch. **The book at a checkpoint.** `LegQuotes.book_at` builds each market's book from the table, the market's 24-hour volume at that checkpoint (`depth_model.volume_24h` on the cached candles, the function the snapshot reads volume with) and the candle's own quotes: the YES bids start at 1 minus the NO ask and the NO bids at 1 minus the YES ask, so the top of the book is the quote `_find_entry` priced, to within float noise. **Sizing.** `_size_trade` passes the two books to `scanner._enrich_pair` and then `strategy.compute_trade`, the live code, under one `config.LiveSettings` per simulation, with the portfolio value and the cash in whole cents (`_cents`). So the live caps, the edge-after-fee cut, the price-sum ceiling, the crossed-book guards, the FoK reachability check and Kelly at each size (with the chance of profit read from the mid spread at the book's top, as live sizing reads it) all apply, and a trade pays the average price over the levels it walks. `BacktestTrade.fill_price_a`/`_b` record that average, and `_paid_prices` is the one reader of what a trade paid: the open-trade valuations, the equity curve, the dashboard's trade rows and its Kelly scatter read it there. The entry scan (`_find_entry`), Pass 1b's Kelly gate (at the top of the book: the prices of one contract pair, the smallest size the live sizer can return), the `k̂` calibration and the dashboard's entry-price buckets stay on the candle quotes. A candidate whose walked book `_enrich_pair` refuses, or for which no size fits, is skipped and counted by reason (`Trades refused when their book was walked (<reason>)`), except one the cash left cannot buy one contract pair of: `_enrich_pair` calls it unaffordable and the cash, not the Kelly share of the portfolio value, sets its budget (`scanner._cash_binds`, the test behind enrichment's "the $C of cash binds" note). The book did not cause that skip, and a run with no depth model skips the same candidate uncounted, so it has its own line, `Trades skipped with no cash left for one contract pair`. Both lines are silent at zero and DEBUG when `quiet`. **Sales.** A sale walks the modeled bid ladder from the bid of the side held (`LegQuotes.sale_ladder`), at the checkpoint and at each daily check before it (`config.TAKE_PROFIT_HOLD_DAYS`), each ladder built from that check's own 24-hour volume (`LegQuotes.volume_checkpoints` at the checkpoint, `volume_daily` at an earlier check); a position larger than its ladder at any of them is not sold that Monday. With no depth model, no volume data at that check, or a bid below 1c or above 99c (`depth_model.can_start_at`), it sells at that one bid in any size (see the selling paragraph). With the volume cap on (`config.BACKTEST_MAX_VOLUME_SHARE`, 10%), a buy takes at most that share of the contracts each market traded in the next day: where that is tighter than the modeled book, the book's sides are cut to it, and with no book the trade is a one-level book at the quotes holding it; the cap reads hours a live run cannot see and only ever makes a trade smaller. **No depth data.** With no depth model, or no volume data for either market that Monday, `compute_trade` sizes at the candle prices (the top of the book, in any size). The trade has `book_walked=False` and is counted: in the log (`Trades sized at the top of the book (no depth data)`, silent at zero) and in the dashboard's Fills line, which is red when the run had no depth model at all. **The size-cap sweep.** Over a walked book the cap also bounds how deep the live enrichment averages (`config.max_kelly_fraction`), so a point records `cap_free_from`: the larger of its peak Kelly fraction and, for each pair type with a candidate whose Monday has a book, the cap above which that bound stops moving (`round(1 − k, 12)` for time-series; the lower of the same-title cap and `SAME_TITLE_CO_RESOLVE_PROB` for same-title). It is decided from the candidates, never from the trades reached, because which trades are reached depends on the cap. `CapSweep` shares one simulation only at caps at or above `max(peak_kelly_fraction, cap_free_from)` (`_sharing_floor`, in both its sharing branches), so a cap below `1 − k` is simulated even where it is above the peak. The Rebalance and Sell families build `CapSweep`s and inherit the rule (pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestCapSweepSharesFromCapFreeFrom`, below). So a higher cap can give a smaller walked trade, or none: the cap sets how deep enrichment averages the book, and that bounds the live sizer's search. **What the live sizer demands.** The backtest's `k` must be above 0 and at most 1, and its caps on the 5% grid (`config.SIZE_CAP_STEP`), exactly as `LiveSettings` requires; `backtest.py --interval-discount 0` exits 2. **Recorded limits.** The table does not vary with the time of day or the time to close. A snapshot is a thin sample: more of them, taken near Monday 09:00 PT, fit it better. A candle dropped for a missing price loses its hour's volume. When `--start-date` is a Monday, that first Monday sees only the hours since its midnight UTC (about 16 hours of volume), because candle requests open there. The book is typical depth for a market like it, not that day's real book. **Every backtest result recorded before this change filled every trade at one candle price, in any size, so none is comparable:** trade sizes, which trades enter, returns, drawdowns and every figure that follows from them move. **Sharpe and Sortino subtract the 8-week T-bill yield in force each day (2026-09, backtest dashboard only).** (The Live trading tab calls the same `_sharpe` and `_sortino` from `live_portfolio.period_stats`, over whole days, with a hurdle of its own — the yield on the share of the account in positions — passing `periods_per_year` explicitly; see "The Live trading tab" under Key Patterns. Everything below is about the backtest page.) `generate_dashboard(risk_free=)` takes a `treasury.RiskFreeRates` (see the `treasury.py` Module Map row), and every ratio on the page subtracts, row by row, the yield of the latest 8-week bill auction on or before that row's date (`RiskFreeRates.annual_on`) — per period, `rf / 365` on a strategy curve and `rf / 252` on the `^GSPC` row's own trading days, so the `sqrt(365/252)` identity above now holds only at `rf = 0` — charged on a strategy curve's capital in open trades only (rule 3). Four rules. (1) **`rf` is POSITIONAL.** `annual_on` returns an `np.ndarray` on purpose and `_sharpe`/`_sortino` subtract `np.asarray(rf)`: a date-indexed Series subtracted from a RangeIndex returns Series aligns on the index and turns every ratio into NaN, and `_view_payload` cuts a curve to the page's axis without resetting its index — each caller takes the hurdle from the SAME frame whose `daily_return` it passes, and a wrong-length array raises. (2) **A curve that never moves reads 0.0** (`dashboard._varies`, checked first in both): with a nonzero rate every flat day is the same negative excess return, which read `_sharpe` −9.6e16 (a float std of ~1e-20, not 0) and `_sortino` exactly `−sqrt(365)` on any zero-trade scenario — both at a constant rate; at a rate that changes inside the window each is a large finite negative instead, and the guard returns 0.0 either way. "Never moves" means its largest and smallest returns differ by at most `config.FLAT_RETURN_TOLERANCE` (1e-12) — a tolerance, not an exact comparison or rounding, so float noise alone never counts as movement. (On a strategy curve the deployed-capital hurdle of rule 3 already charges a curve with no trade nothing; the guard still matters for a direct call and the fully invested `^GSPC` row.) A wrong-length `rf` array raises `ValueError` only on a series that varies (a flat one returns 0.0 first), and a length-1 array broadcasts like a scalar. At `rf = 0` the arithmetic is bit-identical to before on every series that varies — and an equity curve's first return is always 0.0, so a curve either varies or is flat. (3) **The yield is charged on deployed capital only; idle cash is taken to earn it** (operator decision, 2026-09-27). The backtester books idle cash at 0%, so the first version, which charged the whole balance, charged every idle day the full rate: under the tests' two-auction `_rates()` (3% then 5%) the `_ex_sweep` fixture's card read Sharpe 1.10 → −3.33 and Sortino 3.70 → −8.57, and one $3.50 trade on a $10,000 curve (`make_trade()`) Sharpe 1.03 → −234.59, both over 267 rows. A strategy curve's row `t` is now charged `annual_t × f_t` (`dashboard._rf_hurdle(risk_free, equity_df, trades)`), where `f_t = open[t−1] / portfolio_value[t−1]` (`f_0 = 0`) and `open` is what the curve carries in every trade with entry ≤ row date < exit (`dashboard._carried_on_days`: its value at market for a trade with quotes, from the backtester's one day-end path, and its cost WITHOUT fees for one without, matched on exact dates through `_deployed_on_days(..., include_fees=False)`; with nothing quoted it is exactly that cost) — so a trade is charged exactly `exit − entry` days and a same-day trade nothing; the same two fixtures now read 1.10 / 3.70 (Sharpe 1.0961 → 1.0954, Sortino 3.7000 → 3.6968) and 1.03 / 9.13 (Sharpe 1.0297 → 1.0277, Sortino 9.1475 → 9.1269), 267 rows. A curve with no trade held overnight equals `rf = 0` exactly. **Why what the curve carries, without fees:** with idle cash earning the yield `y`, a row's excess return is `r + y·cash/V − y = r − y·open/V`, where `V = cash + open` is the portfolio value — which carries an open position at market (at its cost when it has no quotes), its fees already spent — so `open` must be the very value `V` holds. Then `f ≤ 1` wherever cash ≥ 0, which the backtester's sizing guarantees on every curve it simulates (`_simulate_at_discount` never spends more than its running cash); a category or tag slice's curve (the starting balance plus that slice's own trades — a contribution, not a simulation) can exceed 1 only where the slice's trades were sized on another slice's winnings, its own cash negative, which the same identity then charges the yield as borrowing. (An earlier cut of this rule divided the fee-inclusive capital deployed by `V`, which overstated `f` by the open trades' fees and let it top 1 on a fully deployed row.) A row whose previous value is not positive (a ruined curve) is charged nothing, without dividing. Every strategy site hands the hurdle the curve's OWN trades — `_performance_kpis`' `trades`, `_kd_cells`' and `_point_kpis`' `point.trades`, `_strategy_row(trades=)` (`_view_payload` passes the view's `sel`, so a category or tag slice is charged on its own trades; `generate_dashboard` passes the run's through `_section_benchmark(trades=)`) — and rates without trades raise `TypeError`, never a silent full-balance or zero hurdle. The `^GSPC` row is fully invested and keeps the whole yield (`_rf_hurdle_invested`). **One definition of capital deployed, read two ways:** `_deployed_on_days(trades, days, *, include_fees)` (per-day sums with `np.add.at` in trade order, exact-match lookup, a sequential `cumsum`, a `> 0.0` floor), whose required keyword makes each caller say which it reads — the Risk section's "Capital Deployed Over Time" chart stays FEE-INCLUSIVE (`_capital_deployed`, `include_fees=True`; TS-12 made that deliberate, and its golden digest did not move), the hurdle reads the cost alone (`include_fees=False`) for every trade with no quotes, through `_carried_on_days`, and a quoted trade's value at market instead. With fees it is bit-identical to the per-row loop it replaced for `datetime.date` trade dates (`BacktestTrade`'s type) — pinned by `tests/test_dashboard.py::TestCapitalDeployedParity` (the loop kept verbatim as its oracle, over off-axis entries and exits, same-day, overlapping, Timestamp and tz-aware date columns, NaN and `−0.0` costs and 400 random fixtures) and by the Risk section's golden digest; a trade dated with a datetime or Timestamp now matches its calendar day, where the loop's dict lookup matched nothing; a `None` date matches nothing, as before (`_trade_day`), so a hand-built trade never raises in the Risk section. A trade whose ENTRY falls before the curve's first row is never added but its exit is still subtracted, so the running sum goes negative and masks later trades (floored at 0 on output) — unreachable in production, since a curve opens on `start_date − 1` and is only ever cut at its end, and the loop's behaviour too. A curve's dates are converted to day numbers once and read for both the yield (`RiskFreeRates.annual_on_days`) and the open capital. (4) **`None` subtracts 0%** and reads no date or trade — every direct section call and `tests/dashboard_golden.py` pass none, so no golden digest moved — and a grey header line under the run-settings and live-rule lines says so (`_risk_free_html`: grey when downloaded, with the window's average yield and the latest auction; amber when the download failed and an earlier download stands in; red when neither exists and every ratio subtracts 0% — worded "no usable earlier download is saved", like the loader's WARNING, since a copy saved for another term or field, a corrupt one, one stamped naive or out of range, or an unreadable directory is no copy either; with rates it says the yield is charged on the capital in open trades at each previous close (so a same-day trade is charged nothing), idle cash is taken to earn the same yield, and the S&P 500 row, when shown, is fully invested and is charged the whole yield). The pin checks how each pass-through is spelled, not what the name is bound to: a local rebinding, a walrus or a helper taking `risk_free` positionally passes it — the by-value page tests are what catch those. The largest single piece (about 40% of `_rf_hurdle`'s ~0.45 ms on a 2,460-row curve with 88 trades, that page's count; a third of ~0.52 ms with 300) is converting each curve's dates to day numbers (`treasury.day_numbers`, ~0.17 ms per 2,460 `datetime.date` rows through `toordinal`, against ~0.27 ms through `pd.to_datetime`); a curve with no trade skips it. **`treasury.load_risk_free_rates` never raises** — a cache read that fails in a way `_load_json_cache` does not catch (an unsearchable directory's `PermissionError`, a deeply nested file's `RecursionError`), a cached yield no float can hold (`OverflowError`, skipped as an unreadable record) and any failed save each degrade with a WARNING, and a JSON `true`/`false` yield is skipped as unreadable rather than read as 1%/0% (`bool` is an `int` subclass) — and its worst case is time: a host that swallows packets costs each of `api_call_with_retry`'s 6 attempts at least the 30 s `TREASURY_API_TIMEOUT_SECONDS` (30 s per resolved address — the timeout bounds each socket operation, not the whole request) plus 62 s of backoff: about 4 minutes for a single-address host, before the fallback.
