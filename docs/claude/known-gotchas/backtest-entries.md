**The backtest's entry checkpoint IS the scheduler's run instant (2026-09-27).** The live bot runs at `config.SCHEDULED_RUN` — Monday 09:00 America/Los_Angeles, i.e. 16:00 UTC under daylight time and 17:00 UTC under standard time — and the backtest used to open every trade at Monday 09:00 UTC, 7–8 hours before the live bot sees the book. Two read-only replays on 2026-09-27 over the cached 365-day corpus (start 2025-09-24, ladders on, band 0–0.5, tier floors off, k 0.80, cap 0.20) put the cost at +78.4% return / −9.3% max drawdown at 09:00 UTC against +26.3% / −24.2% at 09:00 Los Angeles (+36.3% / −23.8% at a flat 16:00 UTC) — approximate, because they re-scanned a corpus the v2 prefilter had already cut for the 09:00 UTC checkpoint, so pairs built on markets opened on a Monday between 09:00 UTC and the later checkpoint were missing at the later anchors; an exact figure needs a replay over a corpus assembled under the v3 prefilter. **That replay was run the same day (the A/B measurement, 2026-09-27):** the 365-day window from 2025-09-24, ladders on, DR-75 in place, over ONE v3 corpus the branch's production run assembled (2026-09-28 01:56 UTC, archive cutoff 2026-07-29), both arms through the full pipeline — the old arm with `backtester.SCHEDULED_RUN` patched to Monday 09:00 UTC, so its prefilter re-check cut the corpus back to the v2 set. 09:00 UTC → 09:00 Los Angeles: 9,505,434 → 11,070,031 eligible markets, 3,122 → 5,898 time-series pairs, 400 → 442 no-band tier-on entries (706 → 778 with the tiers off). The old checkpoint's scan over the new arm's candidates entered exactly the old arm's set (406 of 406 tiers on, 712 of 712 off), so v2 was result-neutral and the difference is the checkpoint alone. Band 0–0.5, tier floors off, k 0.80, cap 20%: +58.9% / −13.2% max DD → +1.9% / −26.4% (−2.2% without its top event, H1 −30.6% / H2 +28.9%); the same band at k 0.75 and a 5% cap: +26.9% → −1.8%; the primary cell (0–1, tiers on, k 0.75, cap 20%): +116.4% / −83.4% → +136.3% / −63.6%; pooled empirical k̂ 0.891 → 0.958 (tiers off 0.884 → 0.927). At a 20% cap the share of time-series cells with a positive return rose from 20.3% to 37.0% (tiers on) and 38.5% to 53.0% (tiers off), but the Spearman rank correlation of those cells' returns between the two checkpoints is only 0.68 (tiers on) and 0.58 (tiers off) — 0.48–0.68 across the 5%, 20% and uncapped grids: which configuration looks best moves with the checkpoint. Re-run from the same entries on the simulator after DR-76 (time-series retries, one open trade per ladder), the live-toggles cell reads +67.3% / −29.8% max DD at 09:00 UTC against **−11.9%** / −25.5% at 09:00 Los Angeles (−22.6% without its top event). The new best cells (tiers off, band 0.25–1, k 0.65: +311.3%) still rest on one event (KXFISAEXTEND-26MAY; −18.4% without it, second half −14.1%). Harness and outputs: `.git/checkpoint-tz/` (`ckpt_measure_ab_build.py`, `ckpt_measure_ab_grid.py`, `ckpt_measure_ab_robust.py`, `ab_grid.out`, `ab_robust.out`, `r1/`). `backtester._checkpoint_datetime(d)` is now `SCHEDULED_RUN.instant(d)` — the one definition, shared with `scheduler.py`'s host-clock check — and `_monday_timestamps` and `_can_ever_enter` step to `SCHEDULED_RUN.weekday` (the function names keep "monday"). `_find_entry` and `_simulate_at_discount` are untouched. Six things are load-bearing. (1) **The backtest's dates stay UTC dates** — `start_date`, entry dates, `scan_end`, the horizon, the feasibility window and the equity curve — which is sound only while every checkpoint lies on its own date in UTC and its wall time occurs exactly once (no daylight-saving gap or fold). Monday 09:00 Los Angeles is sound on every Monday from 0001 to 9999. `_prepare_candidates` checks it with `ScheduledRun.date_problems(start_date, today + _SCHEDULE_CHECK_DAYS_AHEAD)` (ten years out, since a listed market can close years ahead; a backtester-private constant beside `_DAY_SECONDS`, the scheduler's `_HOST_CLOCK_CHECK_WEEKS` precedent; about 2 ms from 2020) right after it reads today and BEFORE the feasibility short-circuit and any fetch, and raises `ValueError` naming the schedule and its first three problem dates — or that its zone cannot be resolved — rather than returning the validation `None`: a schedule that shifts dates (Los Angeles 20:00, Tokyo 08:00) or sits on a clock change (Sunday 02:30 / 01:30 Los Angeles) is a configuration error. Pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestScheduleProblemsAreRefused`. (2) **The prefilter floors the checkpoint onto the candle grid.** A market can have a candle at or before checkpoint `t` only if it opened before `floor_P(t)`, the start of the candle period (`CANDLESTICK_PERIOD_INTERVAL_MINUTES`, the epoch/UTC-day grid `_candle_window_open` floors onto) that holds `t` — from the measured rule that no candle ends at or before the start of the period a market opened in (see the prefilter gotcha). On the shipped schedule, on the hour, that is the old `open_time < checkpoint`; `backtester._checkpoint_floor(run, d)` computes it, `lru_cache`d on the pair (schedule, date) so a patched schedule never reads a stale entry, and returns `None` when the instant falls outside `datetime`'s range — that checkpoint then counts as reachable on the opening date and the close-date bound alone decides, so `_can_ever_enter` still never raises. The candle period is an input to the predicate that the tag does not name, so changing `CANDLESTICK_PERIOD_INTERVAL_MINUTES` needs a `SETTLED_PREFILTER_CACHE_TAG` bump (the constants rule). The predicate stays exactly the scan's reachable set for any sound schedule (`TestCanEverEnterMatchesTheScan`, parametrized over five schedules including 03:30 UTC and 00:00 UTC ones, on a grid spanning both clock changes), and costs 0.75 µs a call over 300,000 synthetic cache-shaped records (0.97–0.98 µs on Monday openings, 0.71–0.72 µs on the rest) against 0.76 µs for the Monday 09:00 UTC predicate in the same run. (3) **The cache tag is built when it is used** (`backtester._prefilter_cache_tag()`: `checkpoint-v3` plus the schedule's slug — see the cache-semantics gotcha, which also lists the v2 caches this orphans). On the shipped schedule v3 admits a SUPERSET of v2: the checkpoint is later on the same UTC date, so markets opened on a Monday between 09:00 UTC and 16:00/17:00 UTC are now kept (daily contracts opened after 09:00 UTC and closing before the next Monday among them). (4) **Never move the scheduler to `schedule`'s `.at(…, tz)`** to "make it match": that path goes through pytz, whose America/Los_Angeles table has no daylight time after 2037, so from 2038 it would fire at 10:00 PDT all summer. The scheduler stays on the host's clock and checks at startup that the host's clock lands its fires on `SCHEDULED_RUN.instant(d)` (`scheduler._host_clock_realises_run`); on a host that fails that check the CRITICAL says the backtest no longer replays its runs. (5) **Same-day cash release, pre-existing and unchanged.** `_simulate_at_discount` releases a trade's receipts on its exit DATE, whatever the hour, and sizes a checkpoint's entries on that date's opening portfolio value, spending only its cash (since 2026-09-29; before, on its opening cash) — so a receipt settling later on the entry date funds that morning's entries. At 09:00 UTC that window was up to 15 hours after the checkpoint; at 16:00/17:00 UTC it is 7–8 hours. (6) **Not comparable:** every backtest result, dashboard and assembled cache from before this change was entered at 09:00 UTC; entries, trades, returns, drawdowns and every eligible-market count move. Patch `backtester.SCHEDULED_RUN` in a test, never `config.*` (the module binds it by value); the tag and the floor cache follow the patch. The test fixtures' 09:00 UTC candle stamps (`tests/test_backtester.py`'s `_MONDAY_TS`, `_MONDAY2_TS` and `TestActiveTickerRelease._M2`) are read at or before the later checkpoint and were not moved: they are candle stamps, not checkpoints, so a fixture meaning "at the checkpoint" must build it from `backtester._checkpoint_datetime`. **The run records which checkpoint it entered at.** `run_backtest_sweep` logs `Entry checkpoint (backtest): Monday 09:00 America/Los_Angeles (config.SCHEDULED_RUN, the live scheduler's run time): 16:00/17:00 UTC in this window` right after the size-cap line, before any fetch: the distinct UTC times of `_monday_timestamps(start_date, today)` (`backtester._checkpoint_utc_times`), `no checkpoint in this window` when there is none, and `UTC times not computable (<exception class name>)` (e.g. `(ZoneInfoNotFoundError)`) when they cannot be computed — the line never raises, so `_prepare_candidates` still refuses an unresolvable zone or an unsound schedule with its `ValueError`, and a `start_date` whose next run weekday lies past `date.max` still raises `OverflowError` there (pre-existing, from its own `_monday_timestamps` call). `BacktestSweep.entry_checkpoint` carries `SCHEDULED_RUN.label()` from both production constructions (the infeasible branch included), and `dashboard._entry_checkpoint_html` prints `Entry checkpoint: <label> — the live scheduler's run time` (`not recorded` for anything but a non-empty string) on its own header line directly under the run-settings line; `_run_settings_html` is untouched and the header is outside `TestGoldenSections`' digests. Pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestEntryCheckpointLogging`, `::TestRunBacktestSweep::test_feasibility_short_circuit_records_the_entry_checkpoint` and `::test_the_sweep_records_the_schedule_it_entered_at`, `::TestBandSweep::test_the_sweep_records_its_entry_checkpoint`, and `tests/test_dashboard.py::TestEntryCheckpointHeader`.

**Time-series pair extraction is close-time-windowed, not naive O(n²).** For string-keyed (time-series) groups, `_extract_pairs()` sorts members by `close_time` and sweeps a nested loop with an early `break` once the window closes, bounded by `MAX_DEADLINE_GAP_DAYS + 1` days of margin — the outcome is O(n·window), not the naive O(n²), but it isn't a classic two-pointer sweep since the inner loop restarts from each outer index rather than advancing a shared second pointer. The window exists because `_find_entry()` unconditionally rejects any time-series pair whose close dates differ by more than `MAX_DEADLINE_GAP_DAYS`, so pairs outside it are skipped without ever being materialized as a candidate (counted since M10, never enumerated). The `+1` day is slack only (it can only add harmless boundary candidates that `_find_entry`'s exact cutoff then rejects, never drop a legitimate one). Same-title (3-tuple-keyed) groups have no deadline-gap concept and stay naive — the eligibility prefilter above is what keeps those groups small. Both branches also drop two events of one series (`_same_series_dicts`, plus `_identical_wording_dicts` on the time-series branch — see the one-series gotcha); grouping is deliberately left alone, so the rule lives in exactly one place on this path, as it does on the live one. The same-title branch additionally refuses, per visited candidate, a pair whose closes cannot be compared or sit more than `SAME_TITLE_MAX_CLOSE_GAP_SECONDS` apart (DR-74 — `_comparable_closes_dicts`, then `scanner.closes_apart`; see the close-gate gotcha). That is a pairing rule, not a window: the branch stays naive, and its groups are not close-filtered, so a member whose `close_time` cannot be parsed stays in its group and is refused per candidate on its own line. Since M10 each of those drops, and each same-event skip, is counted on its own silent-at-zero INFO line, and so are the two things the sweep sets aside before visiting anything — members without a readable `close_time`, and the pairs its window excludes (counted from the index at the window's `break`, once per outer member, so the sweep's cost is unchanged) — see the DR-67 observability paragraph. `config.LARGE_GROUP_WARN_THRESHOLD` (1000) logs a warning (not a cap) whenever a post-filter group is still large, as a canary that the prefilter/windowing are working as expected.

**A pair enters on its EARLIEST qualifying Monday that passes the Kelly gate (DR-75, 2026-09).** **The defect.** The backtest entered each pair on its FIRST qualifying Monday or never: `backtester._find_entry` — which applies only the price, deadline and band filters and holds no probability model, which is what lets one entry pass per band serve every `k` and every size cap — returned at the first Monday that passed them, and `_simulate_at_discount`'s Kelly gate, which runs later and per `k`, DROPPED a pair whose first Monday failed it and never looked at it again, even on a later Monday where it would have passed, while the weekly live run re-scans and re-sizes every Monday. That is what made "tier floors off" produce FEWER trades than "on": at band 0–1 and `k` = 0.75 in the 2026-09-26 20:45 run (365 days from 2025-09-24), 61 trades with the tiers on and 56 with them off — with the floors off, pairs first qualify weeks earlier at a thin spread on a wide book, Kelly refuses that price, and 13 groups lost their only trade. **Every qualifying Monday is recorded.** `_find_entry` now scans to `scan_end` and returns the first qualifying Monday's dict — the same eight keys, with the same values — plus `"later"`: a tuple of same-shaped dicts, one per LATER qualifying Monday, earliest first, `()` when the pair qualified on one Monday only. Each later dict carries that Monday's own date and four quotes, the pair's `gap_days`, and its own `mA`/`mB`: a same-title pair's canonical A is re-decided every Monday (the pricier side that week), so a later Monday can hold the legs the other way round — `tests/test_backtester.py::TestActiveTickerRelease`'s TX/TY pair has A = TX on 2026-01-05 and A = TY from 2026-01-12 — while a time-series pair's order never changes. They reference the SAME market dicts the first Monday does, never copies. `backtester._entry_mondays(entry)` — `(entry, *entry.get("later", ()))` — is the one reader, and its `.get` default is load-bearing: an entry built without the key (several tests build eight-key entries by hand) reads as a pair that qualified on one Monday, which is how it was simulated before the key existed. `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_later_mondays_have_one_reader` lets the key be spelled — as a string, or as a keyword argument such as `dict(entry, later=...)` — only in `_find_entry`, `_entry_mondays` and the truncating writer `_split_halves`, anywhere in the package (so an unrelated `"later"` in any module fails it too; widen its allowed homes deliberately if one is ever needed — a spelling built at run time is out of its scope), and pins that `_log_qualifying_mondays`, `_simulate_at_discount`, `_ex_top_event` and `CapSweep.entry_events` read the Mondays through `_entry_mondays` while `_interval_calibration` and `_split_date` never call it. `tests/test_backtester.py::TestFindEntryRecordsEveryQualifyingMonday` checks the recorded Mondays against `_find_entry` itself restarted the day after each hit — on the golden pairs, TX/TY and 300 seeded random pairs whose Mondays are refused between qualifying ones by every per-Monday filter — which is independent of how one pass COLLECTS the Mondays, but not of the per-Monday filters and the candle lookup both sides share; and it refuses Monday 2 of three by each filter in turn (the band ceiling, the tier, the leg-price-sum ceiling, a dead YES quote, an unparseable quote, the fee, a non-positive spread, a dead NO leg), each a case a scan that ENDED at the first refusal after a hit would fail. The independent anchors are `TestPrepareEntriesGolden`'s rows, which now carry each pair's later dates, computed by restarting the PRE-change code, and the critics' cross-version checks: the pre-change code, restarted, agreed with the new `"later"` on all 7,940 later Mondays of four seeded random corpora and on 54,132 real calls (each entered pair, both ways round) across the 365-day window's 54 band passes (measured 2026-09-27). `_find_entry` still returns checkpoint prices only — every qualifying Monday's now, never a daily series; the day-end prices that value an open trade at market are sampled separately, from the same candles, by `_attach_leg_quotes` (see the market-valuation paragraph after DR-61's). **Each leg's candles are read in ONE forward pass** (`backtester._candles_at_or_before`): scanning to `scan_end` made a per-Monday `_candle_at_or_before` — still the reference rule, unchanged — rescan each leg's list from its first candle at every Monday, O(Mondays × candles); the pass returns exactly that function's answer at each Monday, for any candle order, in O(candles + Mondays), both pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestCandlesAtOrBefore`. Measured 2026-09-27 against the code before DR-75 and against a per-Monday `_candle_at_or_before` lookup: the 54 band passes over the 365-day window's (from 2025-09-24) entered pairs take 3.77 s, against 5.15 s and 6.25 s (best of 3 runs each); Phase 1 on the DR-73 calibration corpus (10,733 pairs, start 2020-01-01, ladders on; both tier settings' no-band pre-pass and rescans) 5.77 s, against 6.54 s and 7.42 s (best of 2); and a synthetic pair qualifying on all 52 Mondays of its one-year window, over two years of hourly candles per leg, 1.56 ms a pass, against 0.57 ms (which recorded its first Monday only) and 39.66 ms. Reading on past the first qualifying Monday (every Monday of the window is looked up, those `max_horizon_days` then skips included) means a malformed candle there — a missing price key (`KeyError`), or a `"ts"` missing or not comparable with an int (`KeyError`, `TypeError`) — now raises where the first-hit scan left it unread, the failure a pair that never qualified always had; the candle cache holds none (0 malformed of 4,629,341 candles in 99,001 cached files, scanned 2026-09-27). Each entry pass logs one INFO line, zero included (DR-66): `Qualifying Mondays at band <floor-ceiling>[ with the tier floors off]: N over M entries (P qualify on more than one)`, from `_prepare_entries` right after its `Prepared …` line and from `_sweep_from_candidates` right after each band's tier-on and tier-off `Prepared …` line (36 + 18 lines on a default CLI run); it shares no prefix with the lines tests filter on. **Retention**, supplementing the size-cap paragraph's "one small entry dict per band per entry": each later Monday adds one small dict per band per entry, referencing the pair's own two market dicts, so no market dict is added. On the 365-day window from 2025-09-24 (measured 2026-09-27) that is, per band, 272 at the no-band band (677 qualifying Mondays over 405 entries; 133 pairs qualify on more than one, at most 11) and at most as many at any other single band, whose qualifying Mondays are a subset of the no-band band's pair by pair, and 812 at the tier-off no-band band (1,523 over 711; 322 on more than one, at most 20) and at most as many at any other single binding band; over the whole sweep, 4,930 across the 36 tier-on bands and 6,778 across the 18 tier-off ones, about +3.9 MB traced. Their quotes are the candles' own float objects, shared by every band's dict for one Monday, so once the candles are released they keep alive only 1,434 floats (34 KB) that no first-Monday entry already holds. **The Kelly gate walks them.** `_simulate_at_discount`'s Pass 1b computes the same leg prices, fee, `profit_ratio_entry`, `kelly_b_entry` (DR-62's fee-in-the-denominator arithmetic, unchanged), `p`, `q` and `kelly_f` for each of a pair's qualifying Mondays in date order (`for monday in _entry_mondays(entry)`, the `time_series_profit_prob` call still inline there, as `test_ast_backtester_prices_through_helper` requires) and stops at the first positive fraction; a pair none of whose Mondays passes is dropped as before. A Monday on which either market's quote has not traded or moved within `config.BACKTEST_STALE_QUOTE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS` (24 hours) of the checkpoint does not pass either (`_quote_is_fresh`, cap-independent): like a crossed Monday it is counted, the pair tries its next qualifying Monday, and it never counts toward `peak_kelly_fraction`; the entries, the split date and `k̂` still read every qualifying Monday. Everything after the gate reads the CHOSEN Monday — its quotes, its date and its legs, which for a same-title pair can be the other way round from its first Monday's — so the trade's `entry_date`, `entry_pA`..`entry_nB`, `ticker_a`/`ticker_b` and every other per-leg field, `event_ticker`, `holding_days`, the ranking key `entry_monthly_ratio` and the orientation `_settlement_receipt` pays on are all that Monday's; only `gap_days` is read off the entry itself, since it is the same on every Monday. The H1 records `_split_halves` rebuilds (below) are transient — a `HalfSplit` keeps numbers only — so the gate adds no retention. The earliest passing Monday wins even over a later one with a larger fraction (the live run would have entered there), and `SweepPoint.peak_kelly_fraction` is the CHOSEN Monday's fraction, never the largest over a pair's Mondays. The choice reads `k` and nothing the cap or the cash touches, so the cap still never moves which candidates pass, which Monday each enters on or the peak — `CapSweep`'s reuse rule stands. Every recorded Monday has already passed the fee check (`net_spread > 0`), so `kelly_b > 0` on each and the gate fails only on `p − q/b ≤ 0` (or skips a time-series Monday whose quote is crossed on either market, DR-78); a same-title pair (fixed `p`) can be rescued too, independently of `k`, so `same_title_point` stays valid simulated once. At a given `k` the premise-violation count can only RISE against the first-Monday rule: every pair that reached the premise check before still does (its first Monday still passes first), and a rescued pair now reaches it too — a time-series pair's legs, and so its settlement cell, are the same on every Monday. **What stays on the FIRST Monday, deliberately:** the interval calibration — read at the Monday a simulation traded on, that Monday, and whether the pair trades at all, would depend on `k`, each `k` would produce its own `k̂`, and the pairs a `k` rejects on every Monday would leave the sample: the assumption would shape the measurement meant to test it, the circularity `_interval_calibration` already refuses, so it stays one number per band, unchanged by DR-75; the split date — every band × `k` cell must split at one date or their halves cannot be compared, and the traded Monday moves with `k` while the first does not; and the populations and the band sweep's pre-pass, which read only which pair it is. **The split-half check.** Each pair stays in the half its FIRST qualifying Monday puts it in, so both halves' entry counts are what they were, but `_split_halves` gives an H1 pair only its Mondays BEFORE the split — otherwise the gate could enter an H1 pair on a second-period Monday inside the first-period simulation. It rebuilds the record (`{**rec, "entry": {**entry, "later": kept}}`) only when a Monday is cut and never assigns `entry["later"]` in place: entry records are shared by every population, band and CapSweep holding them (the same-title ones at every band and under both tier settings). An H1 pair is NOT re-listed in H2: each half's candidates must stay a subset of the full run's, each on the Monday the full run chose or absent, or a half could reach a Kelly fraction above the full run's peak (re-listing a pair's post-split Mondays would let H2 enter it on one the full run did not choose) and `CapSweep`'s shared copies of the halves, which build them through the same function, would stop being exact. The residual: an H1 pair whose only Kelly-passing Mondays fall on or after the split trades in neither half, though the full run trades it. **Events on any Monday.** `_ex_top_event` drops every entry whose market-A event ticker is the top event on ANY of its qualifying Mondays, and `CapSweep.entry_events` lists market A's event on every Monday, because a trade records the chosen Monday's market A and a same-title pair's canonical A is decided per Monday; the dashboard reaches entries only through `entry_events()`, so `dashboard.py` did not change. Both are deliberately WIDER than the trades. `_ex_top_event` also drops a flipping same-title entry whose CHOSEN Monday at the point's `k` names another event, taking a trade filed under that other event out of the re-simulation (a hand-built repro: +0.0000% where dropping only the entries whose chosen Monday names the top event gives +7.3407%). That can move the ex-top return either way — down when the extra trade won, as there, up when it lost (the same repro with that trade losing: +0.0000% against −19.9993%). It is latent on the real corpus — none of the 365-day window's same-title entries qualifies on a second Monday (tiers on and off, checked 2026-09-27), and a time-series pair's A never changes — and it leaves `CapSweep`'s reuse rule intact: the re-simulated entries are still a subset of the point's, each entering on the Monday its own gate picks at that `k`, so their peak is no higher. `entry_events()` is a SUPERSET of the events any cell's trades carry: it lists every entry, traded or not (the golden fixture's voided TA/TB and WA/WB pairs never trade — a voided pair cannot — yet their events SNOWA and HAILA are listed), and a flipping entry's event on every Monday. **Measured by the prototype this change was specified from** (2026-09-27, before any code change: the real 365-day corpus's entries re-prepared from the cache, every later Monday enumerated by re-running the pre-DR-75 `_find_entry` from the day after each hit, and replayed through the real `_simulate_at_discount`; band 0–1, population "all", 20% cap, $10,000; trades / wins / return): at `k` = 0.75, tiers on 61/28/+111.8% → 61/30/+117.1% and tiers off 56/32/+131.0% → 79/49/+109.4%. With DR-75 the tiers off trade at least as often as the tiers on at every `k` — more up to 0.85, the same from 0.90, where the two CONVERGE (44/18/−19.8% at 0.90, 27/6/−27.6% at 0.95 either way) — and returns are NOT uniformly better (tiers off at 0.85 fall from +55.9% to +11.2%): a fidelity fix, not a profit fix. Nor are the tiers-on GAINS at 0.65–0.75 (+82.8% → +112.9%, +96.1% → +121.2%, +111.8% → +117.1%) fidelity: they come from one look-ahead swap in one group, without which DR-75 matches or trails the first-Monday rule at all three (see the Residuals). **Residuals.** A pair that passes the gate on a Monday but that Pass 2 cannot take there — no cash, not one contract affordable, a ticker still in an open trade, exact fees eating the profit — is NOT retried on a later Monday, where the live bot would retry it the next week (0–10 pairs per scenario on the 2026-09-26 run's band 0–1, 1 at `k` = 0.75 with the tiers on); that needs a week-by-week Pass 2, a separate change.
