**Strategy change (2026-09) — the time-series bet was inverted.** Until 2026-09 `find_time_series_pairs` fired when the EARLIER-closing contract was priced higher than the later one (`pA - pB >= tier`) and bought NO on the earlier (`market_a`) and YES on the later (`market_b`), on the reasoning that a cumulative-deadline pair must satisfy P(A) ≤ P(B) so a pricier earlier contract is a mispricing with three profitable cells. The bot now trades the opposite anomaly: the LATER contract priced at least the tier ABOVE the earlier (`pB − pA >= min_price_diff_for_gap(gap_days)` — the tier rule as it stood; since the operator decision of 2026-09-27 the live rule is the band floor alone, 0, under a 0.5 ceiling: any strictly positive spread up to 0.5 — see the directional-filter paragraph), buying YES on the earlier (`pA`) and NO on the later (`nB`), on the reasoning that an outsized `pB − pA` is the market overstating the probability that the event first happens between the two deadlines. `market_a` is still the earlier contract; only the sides and the direction of the filter flipped. Three consequences are real-money facts. (1) **It is a directional bet, not an arbitrage.** Three settlement cells exist — event by A (A=YES ⇒ B=YES, the YES on A pays), never by B (A=NO, B=NO, the NO on B pays), in between (A=NO, B=YES, both worthless — the whole stake is lost). At market prices its expected value is zero minus fees; it only profits if the market systematically overstates the in-between mass. "Risk-free"/"guaranteed" wording is gone for time-series everywhere; same-title keeps its co-resolution framing and its wire bytes unchanged. (2) **The Kelly model is `p = 1 − k·spread`** — the spread was the YES-ask gap `pB − pA` when this change shipped, and is the MID spread (the later market's midpoint minus the earlier one's) since DR-78; see the midpoint gotcha below — (`config.time_series_profit_prob(spread, k=None)`, the single definition that `strategy._kelly_p_at`, `backtester._simulate_at_discount` and `dashboard._kelly_fraction(pA, nA, pB, nB, pair_type, k=None, *, spread=None)` all call — pinned by value and by a two-link AST chain, see the calibration note below), with `k = TIME_SERIES_INTERVAL_PROB_DISCOUNT = 0.75` at the time (0.80 since the operator decision of 2026-09-27 — 80%, "prices converge by 20%"): the operator believed 75% of the market-implied in-between mass ("prices converge by 25%"). This is an operator-tunable ESTIMATE; the worked figures in this paragraph were measured at `k` 0.75. With `k = 1` (take the market at face value) or an independence model of the new loss cell, Kelly is ≤ 0 for every time-series pair and nothing ever trades (illustrative fixture 0.30 → 0.60, NO ask 0.40: `k=1` ⇒ p 0.70, f* −0.12; independence ⇒ p 0.58, f* −0.56; `k=0.75` ⇒ p 0.775, f* +0.16). Leaving the LITERAL old expression `1 − pA·(1−pB)` in place would NOT have disabled trading — it would have modelled the impossible A=YES/B=NO cell and sized real trades on it, which is why the formula is pinned rather than merely retuned. (3) **Kelly, not the 20% cap, sized typical pairs** — measured at `k` 0.75 and the `BUDGET_FRACTION` of 0.20 shipped until the 2026-09-27 decision; since then no per-trade cap ships and `1 − k` = 0.20 bounds every time-series trade (`config.max_kelly_fraction`; see the live-defaults decision record of 2026-09-27 under Key Patterns). With `k=0.75`, reading off an earlier contract at `pA` = 0.30 on a tight book (`nB = 1 − pB`), the uncapped fraction is ≈ 0.05 at the minimum short-tier gap and ≈ 0.16 at a 0.30 gap, so `BUDGET_FRACTION` (then 0.20) only bound for gaps of roughly 0.45 or more; **name the reference `pA` whenever quoting these** — the fraction is strongly `pA`-dependent (at the same 0.15 tier it is ≈ 0.14 at `pA` 0.10, ≈ 0.08 at 0.20, ≈ 0.03 at 0.40); a wide book (large `pA + nB` for the same YES-ask gap) drives Kelly negative and the pair is skipped (the same fixture with the later NO ask at 0.50 instead of 0.40 returns `None`). And because a pricier later contract is NORMAL term structure, far more time-series groups qualify than before (one best pair per normalized title+outcome key still applies — `scanner.time_series_group_key`, see the cross-strike gotcha — keyed on the largest `pB − pA`, which no longer bounds a whole daily family to one pair). **What to look for in outputs:** the prod Excel log keeps 18 columns but its x/y headers are now "x — A leg"/"y — B leg", its "Total Cost ($)" column is "Total Cost incl. fees ($)" and its "Min Profit ($)" column is "Profit if won ($)" (headers on new workbooks only — an existing shared `trade_log.xlsx` keeps its old header row, since `_append_locked` writes headers only when creating the file; the VALUES change in every workbook) and every row's Notes cell is prefixed `[<pair_type>: <SIDE_A> A / <SIDE_B> B[ nB=0.xxxx] fees=$x.xx[ adds to N held]] ` (the last part only on a trade that adds to a held pair) — the `fees=` suffix is what makes a row readable under an old header, since the cost beside it is now fee-inclusive (TS-12); for time-series rows the retained "nA (NO ask)" column is A's best NO ask as the finder read it at scan time (reporting only, not a traded price; enrichment does not refresh it, so it is not the book quote the DR-78 forecast reads, whose mid spread only the `Trade computed` log line prints) and the traded `nB` lives in Notes. The dev-simulation candidates sheet gains an "nB (NO ask)" column (13 headers) and its "Price Diff" is `pB − pA` for time-series, `pA − pB` for same-title; `main.print_pairs_table` gains an "nB (NO)" column and its "Min Profit" header is now "Profit (win)" (and, from DR-17, "Outcome A"/"Outcome B" columns carrying each leg's subtitle). `BacktestTrade` carries `entry_nB` (from the candle's `no_ask_close` — old caches need no refresh and there is no `SETTLED_PREFILTER_CACHE_TAG` bump) and `deadline_gap_days` (the calendar-day gap between the two legs' DEADLINES that `_find_entry` selected the price tier and applied the `MAX_DEADLINE_GAP_DAYS` cutoff on, carried out rather than recomputed — the legs' `close_time` gap for a cross-event pair, the two STATED deadlines' gap for a same-event ladder (DR-73), which for a ladder routinely differ (measured on a 1,506-market archive corpus: 101 of 408 ladder pairs have a ZERO-day close gap, 37 closing at the identical instant, while every stated gap is in [1, 30]); it is also what `_interval_calibration` bands `k̂` on, through `CalibrationObservation.gap_days`; `None` for same_title, which has no deadline-gap concept, and for any trade built without it, e.g. test fixtures — reporting only, nothing sizes, prices or settles on it), its `profit` is negative only in the in-between cell, and its `slippage` for time-series is zero in both win cells and negative in the loss cell (there is no positive-slippage cell). `run_backtest` SKIPS (never trades or pays) any time-series candidate that settled A=YES/B=NO and logs ONE summary WARNING with the count (silent at zero): most likely a wording false negative — a snapshot family, or a recurring window worded "before <date>", read as cumulative — though DR-72 widened the WARNING to also name legs ordered on an early REALIZED close (cross-event pairs only — a same-event ladder is ordered on its stated deadlines), and strike-blind grouping on a cache without subtitles, rather than pointing at the wording heuristic alone. **Since DR-67 that is no longer the only signal** — both finders screen the legs' wording first (`scanner.cumulative_deadline_pair`), so the counter is defence in depth behind a heuristic and a non-zero count means one of those causes fired, most likely the wording one. Expect it at or near zero on a post-DR-67 run. That signal is only trustworthy because `_find_entry` orders a time-series pair's legs on the close **DATETIMES**, like the live finder's `scanner.find_time_series_pairs` sorting its group members on `m.close_time`: two contracts closing on the same UTC date at different times of day are a valid zero-day-gap pair, and deciding the swap on the parsed DATES made them a tie, leaving "market A" as whichever the group list held first — so the pair was either dropped (the direction test goes negative) or replayed with its legs inverted, whose genuine in-between settlement then read as the impossible A=YES/B=NO cell and was booked as a *false* premise violation, corrupting the interval-discount calibration's denominator too (TS-06, fixed 2026-09). **That parity is on the DATETIME-vs-DATE fix only, not on what `close_time` means.** The two paths read the same field name on the close datetimes, but not the same MOMENT: live reads the SCHEDULED close of a still-open market, while the backtest reads the REALIZED close Kalshi recorded for a settled one, which for an event that resolved early (a sports market decided before its listed deadline) can sit before the original schedule. A later-deadline leg that resolved early is therefore ordered first, on the realized rather than the scheduled gap — a recorded, pre-existing residual (backtest-only; it needs a scheduled-close field via a refetch, or span-date parsing off the wording), not something this fix addresses. With `config.BACKTEST_SCHEDULED_CLOSE` on (the default), the backtest orders and gaps a cross-event pair on each market's scheduled close wherever its series has a rule learned from the live-close captures that fits the market's fingerprint (`backtester._maturity_close`); the residual remains for a market with no matching rule, and the time-series sweep window still reads realized close dates (see the realized-close bullet under the DR-67 residuals). `_extract_pairs`' close-time sort deliberately stays a DATE sort (a datetime sort would raise on a naive/aware mix from a hand-edited cache); only the swap decision reads datetimes, keeping the date comparison as its `TypeError` fallback. **Not updated by this change:** `kalshi_bot_flowchart.pdf` predates it (it already showed `|pA−pB|` and the wrong dedup order) and is NOT regenerated; `BUG_SWEEP_FINDINGS.md` is a dated, frozen record and is left untouched; `scheduler.py`'s cosmetic "arbitrage" wording is left alone (it never parses trader or main log text). **Deferred follow-up:** fractional-contract sizing remains outstanding (`_format_count` still always emits `.00`). Follow-up (a) — calibrating `TIME_SERIES_INTERVAL_PROB_DISCOUNT` from the backtester — shipped 2026-09; see the next note for what it built.

**A time-series pair must be two CUMULATIVE-deadline markets, not two snapshots (DR-67, fixed 2026-09).** The bet buys YES on the earlier contract and NO on the later one. That is only coherent when both contracts ask "will X happen **by** \<date\>", because only then does the earlier deadline's event nest inside the later one's — which is what makes the FAIR VALUE of YES-A + NO-B at most $1 (not a guarantee about the quoted asks: a wide book can still quote pA + nB >= 1 on a genuine ladder), gives the pair exactly three settlement cells, and rules out A=YES/B=NO. Kalshi also lists **snapshot** markets — "what is X **on** \<date\>", "X **in** \<Month\>" — whose probabilities do not nest at all: SOL ≥ $180 on Sep 14 does not imply SOL ≥ $180 on Sep 18. Before this, the codebase asserted the premise and never checked it, and said so in four places (this file three times, README once): "the live scanner cannot tell them apart from prices… this counter is the only signal". **The exposure was real and measured against the repo's own `_DATE_PATTERNS`:** `"Bitcoin price on Sep 15, 2026?"` and `"…on Sep 18, 2026?"` both normalize to `bitcoin price on ?`, so a dated snapshot family landed in ONE time-series group and was eligible to be sized and traded. The same holds for `"Solana price on Sep 14/18, 2026?"` (`KXSOLD`), `"Highest temp in NYC on Oct 1/Oct 2"`, `"Will the Fed cut by 50 bps in December/November 2026?"`, and — from the other direction — `"after March 1, 2026"` against `"after June 1, 2026"`, where probability DECREASES with the date so the earlier/later leg assignment means the opposite of what it says. Only the year-less full-month shape (`"on June 30"` vs `"on July 1"`) stayed split, which is the one shape `tests/test_scanner.py`'s snapshot pin covers. DR-01's own worked example is one of these families.

**The fix is one classifier on both paths.** `scanner.deadline_phrasing(event_title, title, subtitle)` returns `"cumulative"` / `"snapshot"` / `"unknown"`; `scanner.deadline_profile` pairs that verdict with the deadline phrases spelled out by the one field that decided it (DR-69, see detail (1)); `scanner.cumulative_deadline_pair(profile_a, profile_b)` is the eligibility predicate, and BOTH `scanner.find_time_series_pairs` and `backtester._extract_pairs` call it (pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_both_finders_apply_the_cumulative_deadline_rule` and `::test_ast_the_deadline_classifier_has_one_definition`). Only the FIELD EXTRACTION differs between the paths — attributes live (`scanner._market_deadline_profile`), cache-dict keys in the backtest (`backtester._deadline_profile_dict`, reading every key as `.get(...) or ""` because a cached `subtitle` is legitimately `None`). Eight details are load-bearing.

(1) **The three fields are scanned SEPARATELY and combined by MOST-SPECIFIC FIELD WINS — `subtitle` → `title` → `event_title`** — with a snapshot marker beating a cumulative one *within* a field. They are never concatenated: joining them can manufacture a match across a field boundary (a title ending "…on" beside a subtitle beginning "June 30"). The subtitle leads because it is the **sub-contract**, the thing that actually resolves, so its wording is the most authoritative statement of what the contract settles on and must be able to override a parent event title. That is what keeps an MVE market whose sub-contract is a "by \<date\>" label eligible even under a snapshot-titled parent event. It does NOT weaken the snapshot refusals, and the reason is structural rather than lucky: in the `KXBTCD`/`KXSOLD` families the subtitle is a bare strike ("$82,750 or above") carrying no marker, so the verdict falls straight through to the title, where "price **on** \<date\>" lives. **Since DR-69 the deadline spans come from that same deciding field and no other** — `scanner._deciding_field` is the one field walk, read by both `deadline_phrasing` (the verdict) and `deadline_profile` (the verdict plus the deciding field's spans), pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_the_deadline_classifier_has_one_definition`. Before it, the verdict came from one field while the spans were gathered from all three, and fixing that moves pair ELIGIBILITY (`cumulative_deadline_pair`) in BOTH directions — never a phrasing verdict, since the field walk itself is unchanged — each pinned by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestSpansFromDecidingField` and its backtester mirror: it can refuse a pair whose deciding field states one or no deadline while another field carries distinct dates (an "At any time" subtitle no longer borrows the event titles' "by Sep 14" / "by Sep 18"; a shared "$1 by 2027" sub-contract is one deadline stated twice, whatever the titles say), and it can admit a pair whose deciding fields differ while other fields restate both (each title stating its own deadline, each event title the other's). DR-69 changes the outcome for a stale sub-contract label only toward refusal (seen once in cache: APPLEFOLD-26DEC31); under either rule a stale label can still make one deadline read as two. Measured on the 2026-09-22 live snapshot (113,303 markets): zero verdict flips; spans changed on 558 markets (493 cumulative, 65 snapshot), every one a strict SHRINK to the deciding field's own phrases and none leaving a cumulative market spanless; and zero eligibility flips among the 13,096 time-series candidates the finder compares (52 eligible before and after). (2) **"by" must be followed by a DATE token** — since DR-68 a narrowed set: a month name ending at a word boundary, a weekday (with any following month-name date), a calendar m/d not followed by vote/majority/of, a 2020–2099 year, an ISO date, or a Q/end-of noun form — or "cut **by** 50 bps", "win **by** 10 points" and "pass **by** a 2/3 majority" read as deadlines; the first of those is a real Kalshi shape whose two months merge into one normalized group. (That last example is stopped by the article "a" alone; the m/d token's vote/majority/of lookahead is what stops the article-less "pass by 2/3 majority".) (3) **A bare CLOCK TIME is deliberately not a date token**, so "$82,750 or above by 5pm" is not cumulative — with the subtitle leading, an intraday snapshot family would otherwise fail OPEN through its own sub-contract label instead of falling through to its title. (4) **The date token must cover the NOUN forms** (`Q1 2026`, `end of [the] year`, `year-end`, `EOY`). `_DATE_PATTERNS` already strips `Q1 2026` and `end of [the] year`, so a table accepting only `by <Month>` would refuse markets the grouping key already treats as deadline-dated; `year-end` and `EOY` are NOT in `_DATE_PATTERNS` at all — they are covered here for the same reason as the rest of the token, coverage of common Kalshi phrasings, not because the grouping key needs them. (5) **`after <date>` is a REJECT marker and requires a date token of its own**, or "Will BTC top $100k by Dec 31, 2026, after the halving?" is wrongly refused. (6) **`in <Month>` / `in <Year>` is a WINDOW, not a deadline** — top-10 in October does not nest inside top-10 in November (the FIDE markets this file already names). (7) **The month fragment takes an optional day AND year, with `(?!\d)` on the day group.** Without it "by March 2026" captures only "by march 20" — the day group eats half the year — and every deadline in one month looks identical, which silently refuses genuine pairs; `"by Dec 31"` vs `"by Dec 20"` must read as two different deadlines. (8) **Fails CLOSED**, like `_same_series`: `unknown` wording is refused, and so is a cumulative leg whose DECIDING field names no comparable date ("within 30 days"), even if a less specific field of that leg does (DR-69), because two deadlines that cannot be shown to differ are not a two-deadline pair. Deadline phrases are compared as normalized strings, never as parsed calendar dates: one deadline spelled two ways reads as two ("Oct 1, 2026" vs "October 1, 2026"). Two deadlines truncated to one span read as one: fixed for a weekday plus month-name date by DR-68, other truncations remain (Known residuals below).

**DR-68 tightened the date tokens (fixed 2026-09).** A month name must end at a word boundary on the cumulative side (`_DEADLINE_MONTH_FULL`'s `\b`) and at the next LETTER on the snapshot side (`_SNAPSHOT_MONTH`, used by the `on <Month>` and `on the <Month> <day>` entries and by the `after` marker, which also inherits the `\b`-bounded month through the shared `_DEADLINE_DATE_TOKEN`), so "wins **by Dec**ision", "produced **by Mar**vel Studios", "land **on Mar**s" and "**after May**or Adams resigns" are no longer dates, while an unspaced "on Sep30" / "after Sep30" still fires its reject marker; a calendar m/d must be a real month (1–12) and day (1–31) and must not be followed by vote/votes/majority/supermajority/of, so "by 2/3 vote", "ratified by 3/4 of states" and "by 3/2027" are not deadlines; a bare year must be 2020–2099, so "decrease by 2000" is an amount; `\bever\b` is no longer a cumulative marker (it fired on names — "Chaco For Ever", "Worst Neighbor Ever" — and on "the hottest year ever"); a weekday keeps a following month-name date, so "by Friday, Sep 19, 2026" and "by Friday, Sep 26, 2026" are two deadlines rather than one truncated "by friday" stated twice (a genuine pair used to be refused); "on **the** \<Month\> \<day\>" is a snapshot like "on \<Month\> \<day\>" (the CFP-rankings families), but only with a day, and `(?!\d)` keeps that day from eating a year; and the dotted clock forms ("at 5 p.m. ET") no longer need a following word character. The two older REJECT markers deliberately keep their DATE alternatives as wide as before DR-68 — `after <date>` keeps the wide numeric alternatives (any m/d, any `20xx`) beside the tightened token, and both it and `on <Month>` end a month at the next letter rather than at `\b`, so an unspaced genuine date still matches — because narrowing a reject marker for a GENUINE date fails OPEN: snapshot → cumulative, the one direction this rule must never move for a genuine date. What DR-68 does narrow on the snapshot side, deliberately, is month-prefixed NON-date words: "land on Mars", "decide on declaring", "after Mayor Adams resigns", "after Marvel's release" and "after Decision Day" move snapshot → cumulative, because none of them names a date (detail (5) already requires `after` to be followed by one); the Mars, declaring and "after Mayor" rows of `TestDateTokenBoundaries` pin that move. Measured on the 2026-09-22 live snapshot (113,303 markets): 65 verdicts cumulative → unknown (KXMOVIECAST 27, KXPERFORMROLEBPANTHER 10, KXUFCMOV 10, KXUFCVICROUND 5, KXUFCMOF 5, KXARGNACBGAME 3 — the "Chaco For Ever" club — KXGTEMP 2, KXDTEMMYS 1, KXHMONTH 1, KXVMA 1), 1,060 unknown → snapshot (KXNCAAFTOPCFPPOLL 760, KXNCAAFCFPPOLL 300), **zero** snapshot → cumulative and no other flip, three span-only changes (two KXNHSPLTRBREAK markets lose `'by dec'`, KXGOVTCUTS-26-2000 loses `'by 2000'`), and zero eligibility flips among the 13,096 time-series candidates the finder compares (52 eligible before and after); on the 1,004,591 records of the 2026-09-08/09 day slices, 545 cumulative → unknown — every one a "by Decision" leg, 525 of them combo fight-method tickets (every combo record that read cumulative) — and nothing else. Other deliberate changes (fail closed): "by Friday at 5 p.m. ET" now reads snapshot (the dotted clock fires); "by 12/31 of this year" reads unknown (the `of` lookahead); an m/d followed directly by a word character ("by 6/30th", "by 6/30pm") reads unknown (the m/d token's trailing `\b`); and an unspaced month-name date on the CUMULATIVE side ("by Sep30") reads unknown (`_DEADLINE_MONTH_FULL`'s `\b` — the snapshot side still reads it). No deadline preposition directly followed by either of the last two shapes occurs on the 2026-09-22 snapshot or in those day slices. Tests: `tests/test_scanner.py::TestDateTokenBoundaries` — a regression row per tightened token (each calendar range, month and day, on its own row), plus controls that each name the over-tightening, reject-narrowing or over-widening mutant they kill; the one surviving mutant, a letter lookahead in the `on the` branch, is equivalent, because that branch's required day already excludes a letter after the month — and its finder-level mirror in `tests/test_backtester.py::TestDateTokenBoundaries`.

**The classifier is computed ONCE PER MARKET, never per pair, and that is a hard constraint.** A group of N markets produces O(N²) candidate pairs; `tests/test_backtester.py::TestExtractPairsPerformanceSmoke` sweeps a 50,000-member group under a 30-second budget, and measured on the real tables (on that synthetic 50k fixture, 2026-09-19, before DR-68/DR-69/DR-70), classifying per pair costs **14.08s** against **0.47s** per market — about 47% of the whole budget before the sweep does any work of its own. That is a 30× TIME ratio on that fixture, even though per-pair classification makes about **60× more CALLS** (2 legs × ~31 in-window neighbours per member); quote the two separately. `find_time_series_pairs` memoizes by ticker across the run; `_extract_pairs` memoizes positionally **per group** and drops it with the group, because a corpus-wide ticker→verdict map would add residency in exactly the place TS-07 did work to remove it, and `_extract_pairs` is called twice per run. Measured after, on the same synthetic 50k fixture (2026-09-19): the 50k sweep runs in 7.8s and still emits 1,549,504 pairs. **DR-70 then made each classification cheap (fixed 2026-09).** `_field_phrasing` tests each table with ONE precompiled alternation (`scanner._ANY_SNAPSHOT` / `_ANY_CUMULATIVE`) instead of searching every entry in turn, and `_deciding_field` skips a field identical to the one scanned just before it (every combo record's title repeats its subtitle verbatim). Table entries must stay alternation-safe — zero capturing groups and no inline flags — or "some entry matches" stops equalling "the alternation matches"; `tests/test_scanner.py::TestPhrasingTableInvariants` pins that, and `::TestAlternationEquivalence` checks both alternations against the per-pattern lists (which stay, for `_deadline_spans`' index 0 and as that reference) over 112 distinct wording strings — those of the classifier's parametrized DR-67/DR-68 tests, read off the tests' own parametrize marks so the reference cannot drift from them, plus eight capitalisation cases that no table spells. Measured 2026-09-22 through `deadline_profile` on the first 200,000 records of the 2026-09-08 day slice: **45.7 → 12.3 µs/record** (the alternation alone 23.0, the skip alone 23.9); output identical on all 113,303 markets of the 2026-09-22 live snapshot and, by SHA-256 over the profiles, on the 1,004,591 records of the 2026-09-08/09 day slices. The outcome-label census's own cost is this classifier, not its walk (12.5 vs 0.3 µs per record on the same sample). Since SS-1 the census rides `backtester._index_eligible_keys`, `_prepare_candidates`' first walk over the corpus (fed through `_OutcomeLabelTally.add`), and that walk also computes both grouping keys per eligible record — on a combo-heavy corpus the time-series key dominates it (~135 µs/record, `normalize_title` on long combo titles, measured on the 2026-09-17 7-day window), so there the census is the smaller share.

**What this deliberately does NOT touch.** `_DATE_PATTERNS` and both grouping keys are unchanged — a snapshot family still GROUPS, it is refused at PAIR time — which keeps the blast radius to eligibility and avoids the pattern list this file already flags as regression-prone. The phrasing tables are a SEPARATE structure and must not be merged into `_DATE_PATTERNS`: that list exists to ERASE dates so two deadlines collapse onto one key, these exist to READ the surrounding preposition and decide what kind of question it is. The same-title finder is untouched (no deadline concept), and so is `_extract_pairs`' same-title branch — that function serves both types and dispatches only on `isinstance(key, str)`, so the rule goes on the string-keyed branch ONLY. `_can_ever_enter` is untouched, so **`config.SETTLED_PREFILTER_CACHE_TAG` is NOT bumped** (same precedent as DR-02); do not "optimise" the rule into that predicate, which is the one placement that would silently corrupt every assembled cache on disk. Kelly, `k`, fees, tiers, leg sides, enrichment, `_settlement_receipt` and the trader are all unchanged.

**Two consequences to expect.** (a) **The two finders are now MUTUALLY EXCLUSIVE on one ticker pair.** A same-title pair requires identical wording; a time-series pair requires two DIFFERENT stated deadlines; identical wording cannot state two of anything. So the cross-type collision `main._dedup_pairs` and `backtester._drop_cross_type_duplicates` exist for can no longer arise from the finders. Both helpers stay — they are still right if a collision ever arises another way — and `tests/test_backtester.py::TestRunBacktestCrossTypeDedup` now pins the exclusivity instead of the drop, alongside `tests/test_scanner.py::TestDeadlineGuardFinders::test_dated_identical_wording_is_same_title_only` and its backtester mirror `tests/test_backtester.py::TestRunBacktestCrossTypeDedup::test_dated_identical_wording_is_same_title_only`, which pin the same exclusivity on a fixture whose legs DO each carry a "by \<date\>" deadline stated identically (not the deadline-less class-level fixtures, titled "Q", that `TestRunBacktestCrossTypeDedup`'s OTHER tests use). Since DR-74 each of those fixtures comes in two versions: legs closing at ONE instant, which yield the same-title copy alone (`_ALIGNED_MARKETS`, and the `_is_same_title_only` tests' fixtures), and legs closing apart, which yield NO pair of either type (`_MARKETS`, and `::test_identical_wording_closing_apart_forms_no_pair_of_either_type` on both paths) — the relabel guard for a close gate that sits on the same-title finder only (see the close-gate gotcha). (b) **Skipping is not only subtractive**, exactly as for DR-02: both finders keep ONE best pair per group, so refusing a group's widest candidate PROMOTES the runner-up, and a diff of two runs' pairs tables is not a pure deletion.

**Observability, because this rule can empty the strategy.** Absence of a warning must never be the only signal (DR-66). The live finder logs a per-market census on EVERY run — `Deadline phrasing of actively priced markets: N cumulative, N snapshot, N unknown` — plus, since DR-72, THREE silent-at-zero skip counts for refused candidates split by REASON (`scanner.deadline_pair_refusal`, over `REFUSED_SNAPSHOT` / `REFUSED_NO_STATED_DEADLINE` / `REFUSED_SAME_DEADLINE`) rather than one folded count: a leg's deciding field is snapshot wording; a leg's deciding field carries no recognised deadline wording or no comparable date; or the two deciding fields state the same deadline, or truncate to one. A fourth silent-at-zero line covers candidates that WERE worded as two different cumulative deadlines but were refused only for sitting more than `MAX_DEADLINE_GAP_DAYS` apart ("... refused at the %d-day gap cap (tier and price not evaluated): %d") — the live signal that cumulative families exist but sit too far apart to trade, distinct from the three wording refusals, which mean no genuine two-deadline pair was ever established. And another silent-at-zero line for the `pA + nB >= $1` guard. And, since M10 (2026-09-24 review), a silent-at-zero line for the one-series conjunct that runs before the wording check (`Time-series candidates skipped as two instances of one event series (identical wording, different fixture; counted before the gap and price filters): N`) — until then the one refusal in the cross-event branch ahead of the price filters with no count (the price-parse `continue` after it is still uncounted, on both finders, and so is the same-title finder's own threshold test; on the time-series finder the live spread rule's entry-floor and band-ceiling refusals are counted, each on its own silent-at-zero line — `Time-series candidates refused below the entry floor (<rule>): N` and `… refused above the spread band's <ceiling> ceiling (pB - pA; before the one-best-per-group contest): N`, with `Same-event ladder candidates …` twins for a ladder — while a spread that is not strictly positive, the direction filter, stays uncounted; and the rule itself is logged on EVERY run as `Time-series entry rule: …`, since the admission rule now depends on the run's settings). `backtester._extract_pairs` mirrors the three wording-refusal lines (worded "... within the deadline-gap window, before price filters" — it has no gap-CAP line of its own, since its close-time sweep window is a performance bound rather than a rule), and since M10 it accounts for every pair of every group's members it is handed, each on its own silent-at-zero line (pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestOneSeriesAndSameEventCounts::test_every_candidate_is_returned_or_counted_exactly_once`; the one `continue` still uncounted is the `seen` guard, which fires only on a ticker repeated inside one group — a corpus the assembly's first-wins dedup never produces — and cannot cause a zero even then, since it refuses only a repeat of a pair already returned): the time-series one-series line, which differs from the live one only in that same parenthesis; the same-title branch's four lines — both markets on one event ticker, two events of one series, (since DR-74) two markets closing more than `SAME_TITLE_MAX_CLOSE_GAP_SECONDS` apart, and (since DR-79) two series filed under different Kalshi categories or tags — which are `scanner.find_same_title_pairs`' lines VERBATIM (the same-event one also new in M10, on both paths), plus a fifth same-title line that is BACKTEST-ONLY, `Same-title candidate pairs refused because a market's close_time cannot be read (fail closed; the live finder drops such markets before grouping): N` — a same-title group is not close-filtered (`_can_ever_enter` keeps a market whose close it cannot parse), while the live finder never reaches a market without a `close_time`, so counting that pair on the close-gap line would make the close-gap line stop being the live one's twin (see the close-gate gotcha); and three BACKTEST-ONLY time-series lines. The first is `Time-series candidates skipped because both markets carry the same event ticker and same-event deadline ladders are off for this run (within the deadline-gap window, before price filters): N`, logged only with the ladder switch off (with it on, those candidates belong to the ladder sub-pass, which counts them on its own lines). Its nearest live line, the `ladder_disabled_skips` count, covers every same-event candidate of a group over actively priced markets; this one counts only those inside the sweep's window, over eligible settled markets, so the two are different numbers and are worded apart. The other two cover what the time-series sweep sets aside BEFORE visiting anything, and neither has a live twin (the live finder sweeps every pair of a group, its gap cap being a counted rule, and drops a market without a `close_time` before grouping, with its own WARNING): `Time-series group members without a readable close_time, left out of pair extraction (...): N` — MEMBERS, not pairs — and `Time-series candidate pairs the sweep never visits because their close dates are more than 31 days apart (...): N`, pairs of dated members beyond the window, on which no rule is evaluated. That window line deliberately avoids the words "gap cap": the live gap-cap line counts pairs already worded as two different cumulative deadlines, this one pairs never looked at, and it can dwarf every other count on a long window, since a group whose members spread over far more than the window has almost all of its N(N−1)/2 pairs beyond it. With the ladder switch on, the sub-pass's lines count every same-event pair of the group, the ones beyond the window included, so the two populations overlap by exactly those pairs. A grouping with NO group of two or more members reaches `_extract_pairs` as an empty dict, which cannot say which grouping it was, so `_prepare_candidates` logs `Groups of two or more markets: N time-series (M markets), N same-title (M markets) — pairs form only inside a group` on EVERY run, zero included, after the phrasing census and before any of these lines. Between them, every zero on the `Potential pairs` line has a logged cause. Those lines exist because a combo-heavy window's zero had no logged cause: the 2026-09-24 7-day run logged `Potential pairs: 0 time-series, 0 same-title` over 184,178 groupable markets with 748 time-series wording refusals and nothing at all for the same-title side — which of the rules above emptied it, or whether its same-title grouping was empty, is unmeasured and will be on the next run's log. The backtest's outcome-label census (counted by `backtester._OutcomeLabelTally` inside `_prepare_candidates`' first walk over the corpus and reported through `_report_outcome_label_coverage`; `_log_outcome_label_coverage` is the one-shot composition of the two) censuses the live census's three phrasing-bucket counts (`Deadline phrasing of actively priced markets`, above) over the eligible corpus in that single pass and carries them on `OutcomeLabelCoverage` (fields appended after `below_floor`, since it is frozen and not `kw_only`) out to `dashboard._deadline_phrasing_html`. That render is DESCRIPTIVE with **no floor and no banner**, deliberately unlike the subtitle census: the cumulative FRACTION has no healthy baseline — most Kalshi markets are not deadline markets at all — so any threshold would be arbitrary and would fire on every run. The reading that IS actionable needs no threshold, and gets its own sentence on the page: **zero cumulative markets on a non-empty corpus means the run could not have produced a time-series trade at all** — but ONLY when the three counts actually sum to the corpus (DR-71, fixed 2026-09): a mismatched or hand-built coverage instead renders its raw counts plus "(counts do not sum to the corpus — not a verdict)" rather than asserting a verdict its own numbers cannot support. The two refusal reasons are reported SEPARATELY rather than folded into one sentence, because they are refused for different reasons: snapshot wording is refused because such probabilities need not nest ("after \<date\>" nests the wrong way; a shared-start "after X and before Y" window CAN nest — a known, accepted over-refusal), while unrecognised wording is refused because nesting simply cannot be shown. DR-71 also made the three `OutcomeLabelCoverage` phrasing fields REQUIRED, with no default — see the DR-01 paragraph's correction to the DR-66b "one remaining place an omission is possible" sentence for why. One divergence no AST pin can catch: `event_title` reaches essentially every LIVE market but only a small fraction of CACHED ones, so a market whose deciding field is its event title (no marker in its subtitle or title) reads as its event title's verdict live (cumulative for a "by \<date\>" event title, snapshot for an "on \<date\>" one) and as unknown in backtest. At the phrasing-rule level, where the cache carries subtitles, the backtest's profile equals the live one or is unknown (DR-69: the spans come only from the deciding field, so a blank cached event title cannot change a profile the subtitle or title decided — before DR-69 the live spans also folded in the event title's dates, and the two paths could differ in either direction). That is not a pipeline guarantee: a blank cached event_title changes the time-series group key itself.

**`find_time_series_pairs` skips candidates whose leg ASK prices already sum to $1 or more, explicitly rather than emergently.** A win pays only $1, so such a pair cannot profit in any cell. It is not impossible: wide books quote it even on genuine ladders (KXDEFAULT-28DEC31/-29DEC31 at 0.11 + 0.96 on 2026-09-21) — nesting makes the FAIR VALUE of YES-A + NO-B at most $1, not the quoted asks. The skip was previously only IMPLIED by the `tradeable` flag, which meant a violating pair was still constructed and could win its group's one-pair slot ahead of a sound runner-up; the explicit skip only changes which NON-tradeable row represents such a group, since `tradeable` already requires `1 - pA - nB > fee`. **The epsilon runs the OPPOSITE way from every other price filter here** and the comment says so: the qualifying-level tests ADD `PRICE_EPSILON` to a keep bound so a level exactly on the bound is not dropped for noise, whereas this is the REJECT side of a strict `< 1`, so it SUBTRACTS — a sum that is really 1.0 but evaluates to 0.9999999 must still be refused. It tightens by at most 1e-6, two orders of magnitude below the finest tick, so it can never reject a genuinely sub-$1 pair, and TS-09's "never reject for representation noise" rule is honoured rather than broken — in practice it is defensive and inert on every Kalshi grid (4-dp prices; complementary pairs sum to exactly 1.0).

**Any backtest baseline taken before this change is not comparable** — fewer time-series candidates and therefore different trade counts, returns, equity curve and drawdown; on the 2026-05-01 cache the empirical `k̂` population isn't merely smaller, it is EMPTY with the ladder switch off (see the dormancy note below). The pooled `k̂` figures quoted elsewhere in this file predate it and are void.

**Dormancy, measured 2026-09-21.** After this rule shipped, the CROSS-EVENT time-series leg forms 0 pairs live, and the 2026-05-01 backtest cache's surviving cross-event time-series candidates are recurring windows (weekly "before \<date\>" families) the backtest cannot enter — see the Known residuals list below for why they are harmless rather than fixed. Genuine cross-event cumulative families exist live (e.g. Premier League managers "cease to be manager before Jan 1 / Jun 1, 2027"), but every one measured spans more than the 30-day gap cap; the real cumulative opportunity sits in same-event deadline ladders (Kalshi listing a question's several deadlines as separate markets inside ONE event). **DR-73 built that expansion; it shipped OFF and is ON by operator decision of 2026-09-26** (`config.TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS`). The cross-event dormancy above is unchanged, but the time-series leg is no longer dormant: on the 2026-09-22 snapshot the switch-on finder emitted 24 ladder pairs in 24 events under the tier-on, no-band rule then shipped (48 in 48 events, 47 of them tradeable, at the defaults shipped since the 2026-09-27 decision — re-measured that day). The switch shipped behind a decision rule — stay off unless k̂ in the widest-spread band is materially below 1 — that its calibration did not meet (0.87 on n=71 over the extended archive). Enabling it bets the operator's belief (`k`) against real scheduled-event information — the risk that rule gated. See that constant's comment for what the decision accepts. The pooled `k̂` 1.114 quoted elsewhere in this file, measured on the strike-blind 2026-05-01 cache, came from 35 observations on pairs this rule refuses (snapshot or unknown wording) and is void; on that same cache the calibration population is empty with the ladder switch off.

**Known residuals of the DR-67 wording rule.** Recorded here, each with its measured bound, rather than fixed.
- **Date-token residuals (DR-68):** season ranges ("2018-19 through 2025-26" reads `'through 2025'` — can turn a verdict cumulative; 1 live instance, KXNBANEWCHAMPION-27-Y); 2020–2099 quantities ("increase by 2030 units"); fractions before other nouns ("by 1/2 point"); month-named proper nouns ("by April Ryan", "Mar-a-Lago"); end-of-period nouns without a trailing boundary ("by the end of the weekend" reads `'by the end of the week'`). Each of these can produce a false cumulative verdict or span; none has a live time-series partner (re-checked on the 2026-09-22 snapshot: no eligible candidate's span rests on one of these shapes — measured over cross-event candidates, before ladders were on). Separately, the snapshot-side `on m/d` and `in/during/for 20xx` tokens were deliberately left wide: they can only produce a false SNAPSHOT verdict — never a span, since spans come only from `_COMPILED_CUMULATIVE[0]` — which fails closed.
- **Weekday + numeric date (DR-68):** a weekday keeps only a following MONTH-NAME date, so a numeric one is still truncated. With no year or a 2-digit one ("by Friday, 9/19", "by Friday, 09/19/26") the two titles do not share a time-series group; with a 4-digit year ("by Friday, 9/19/2026") or an ISO date ("by Friday, 2026-09-19") they do, both legs span `'by friday'`, and `cumulative_deadline_pair` refuses the pair as one deadline stated twice — the weekday-truncation false refusal DR-68 fixed for month-name dates, still present for these. It fails closed (a missed trade, never a false one); a deadline preposition followed by a weekday and a numeric date occurs 0 times on the 2026-09-22 snapshot and in the 1,004,591 records of the 2026-09-08/09 day slices.
- **Recurring rolling windows** ("Will Trump say X before May 11 / before May 18", weekly GPU "Up or Down by", weekly eliminations) pass the wording screen but do not genuinely nest — each is its own week, not a cumulative deadline on one question. Checked against real prices rather than assumed harmless: measured 2026-09-21 on the 2026-05-01 backtest cache, of 1,612 such pairs, 1,448 were never listed together (the backtest's entry rule cannot open a trade on them) and 164 overlapped only in the earlier leg's final 2–10 hours. In 96% of co-quoted admissible hours the earlier leg's YES ask was 0.01, so the pair is effectively a NO bet on the later window regardless of nesting, and nesting does not change the payoff. Kelly would have sized 49 of them: 29 won, 20 lost, net positive. Live scheduled runs (Monday 16:00 UTC) fall after the overlap window closes. Revisit only if a series is found co-listing mid-window rather than only in its final hours.
- **Level at one instant** ("SOFR above X by end of \<period\>"): a value observed at a single moment, worded with a deadline preposition, reads cumulative though it does not nest. Held back today only by the quarterly cadence of the one live family (`KXCREDITC`) — its two nearest quarters are more than `MAX_DEADLINE_GAP_DAYS` apart (measured over cross-event candidates, before ladders were on). A monthly family of the same shape would pass the 30-day gap and needs its own fix (a predicate classifier, not yet designed).
- **Non-monotone predicates** ("exactly N", "remain until \<date\>"): also read cumulative though the underlying condition is not monotone in time. Most instances are same-event ladders, which were refused with every other same-event pair until the 2026-09-26 decision; with `config.TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS` on they reach the ladder branch, which has no predicate check, so there they are bounded only by stated-date parsing, the 30-day stated gap and price — unmeasured on the ladder population. Cross-event instances are still stopped by the 30-day gap: the monthly `KXPARDONSTRUMP` windows open only when the previous one closes, so consecutive instances are never co-tradeable.
- **Recall misses that fail closed:** a deadline whose only qualifier is a non-date period ("before Week 4 of the 2026-27 season") names no date token, so it reads `unknown` and the pair is refused — a missed trade, never a false one. A deadline FOLLOWED by a clock time ("before Dec 31, 2026 at 4pm EST") reads `snapshot`, which also refuses. **Kalshi's shared-start template "after issuance and before \<date\>" is a different case and is deliberately ADMITTED** (it reads `cumulative` with the "before \<date\>" span): two windows that start at issuance and end at different dates DO nest, which is exactly the premise the rule screens for. Measured on the 2026-09-22 snapshot, of the 150 markets whose wording mentions issuance, 100 read cumulative, 40 snapshot and 10 unknown — and none of them reaches a pair with `config.TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS` off (its value until 2026-09-26), because those families list their deadlines inside ONE event ticker and are dropped by the different-event rule, not by the wording rule (see the Dormancy sentence above). **With the ladder switch ON they do pair, and one of them is in the emitted set** — `KXALBUMRELEASEDATE-NEWDRA` ("Will Drake release new album after issuance and before Sep 26, 2026") is 1 of the 24 pairs the 2026-09-22 snapshot emitted under the rule shipped until 2026-09-27 (and 1 of the 48 it emits at the defaults shipped since) — which is the intended outcome, not a regression: two windows that both start at issuance and end at different dates DO nest. An earlier version of this bullet claimed all three shapes read `unknown` and were refused; that was false of the code and is corrected here.
- **Realized-close ordering in the backtest:** `_find_entry` reads the REALIZED close of a settled market where the live finder reads the SCHEDULED close, so an event that resolved early can compress or invert the measured deadline gap for a genuine cumulative pair. Backtest-only, and **CROSS-EVENT-only since DR-73c**: a same-event ladder is ordered and gapped on its two STATED deadlines, which is span-date parsing off the wording applied to exactly the population where realized close is worst (681 of 1,821 archive same-event pairs have a close gap of ZERO days; on the 101-slice sub-corpus, 101 of 408 ladder pairs, 37 of them closing at the identical INSTANT — the zero-DAY reading is the one that matters, since `_find_entry`'s cross-event branch orders on close DATETIMES). With `config.BACKTEST_SCHEDULED_CLOSE` on (the default; `backtest.py --no-scheduled-close` turns it off for one run) a cross-event pair is ordered, gapped and held to the horizon on each market's scheduled close wherever its series has a learned rule that fits the market's fingerprint (`backtester._maturity_close`, `market_details.learn_close_rules`), and keeps the realized close otherwise; the scan's last Monday still reads the realized close. Two residuals remain with the switch on: a market with no matching rule is ordered on its realized close as before, and the time-series sweep window in `_extract_pairs` still reads the REALIZED close dates, so a pair whose realized closes are more than 31 days apart but whose scheduled closes are within 30 is never visited — pessimistic, since it can only miss a trade (the earlier market closed far ahead of schedule, so its earlier leg's YES, a winning cell, is what is missed).
- **Strike-blind legacy caches:** a backtest day slice written before the 2026-08 subtitle fix carries `subtitle=None` and groups strike-blind. Remedied by the documented cache refresh (delete `backtest_cache/archive_days/` and `backtest_cache/live_days/`, then re-run with `--no-cache`) — see the subtitle-drift gotcha below. **Since DR-73 the same blank subtitle also changes WHICH LADDERS EXIST, in BOTH directions**, because DR-69 makes the subtitle the deciding field and `scanner.stated_deadline` cross-checks all three: blanking it moves the decision to the TITLE, so a rung reads a DIFFERENT day rather than none, and it disarms a cross-check the live path applies. Measured on the 2026-09-22 snapshot (113,303 actively-statused markets, 378 live ladder pairs): the blank-subtitle shape flips 649 markets to undated, 53 the other way and **9 to a different day**, and forms 246 ladder pairs — 136 of the live 378 missed AND **4 admitted that the live finder refuses**. A `k̂` measured on such a corpus is therefore not measuring the live ladder population; see `backtester._stated_deadline_dict`'s docstring for the per-ticker evidence, and the plan's "prove the corpus before spending on it" step. (The `event_title` divergence is far smaller and had no pair-level effect at all on that snapshot: 71 markets narrow, 1 wide — `KXACAREPEAL-29-29JAN20` — 0 dated differently, 378 ladders either way.)

**Two rungs of ONE event's deadline ladder are a time-series pair, behind a switch that is ON by operator decision of 2026-09-26 (DR-73, live path 2026-09; it shipped OFF).** Kalshi lists a question's several deadlines as separate markets inside a SINGLE event — "Will SpaceX launch another Starship before Sep 23, 2026?" and "… by Oct 16, 2026?" are both `KXSPACEXSTARSHIP-14`. Both finders refused every same-event candidate from the first commit on a one-line rationale, "options within a multi-choice event". That is right for an MVE event's option labels and wrong for a dated ladder, whose two rungs are the cumulative-deadline premise itself. `config.TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS` (`True` by the 2026-09-26 decision) admits them; **read that constant's comment before relying on it or turning it back off** — it carries the nesting evidence (0 of 1,821 archive same-event pairs settled in the impossible A=YES/B=NO cell, against 2,872 of 22,080 = 13.01% cross-event), the live funnel, the measured exposure (on the 2026-09-22 snapshot, 24 pairs → 6 selected trades deploying $9,803.66 = 98% of a $10,000 balance at −31% market-implied EV, four at the `BUDGET_FRACTION` cap, the five largest losing together 12.7% of the time under the model's own probabilities — all measured at `k` 0.75 and the 20% cap, tier floors on and no band, the live toggles before the 2026-09-27 decision), the decision rule it shipped behind and its unmet result (k̂ 0.87 on n=71 in the widest-spread band), and the 365-day ladder backtest (+111.8% at `k` 0.75 and the default band, resting on one trade: the run re-simulated without its event returns −92.8%; −82.2% max drawdown on the cost-basis curve). Eight things are load-bearing.

(1) **A ladder is ordered and tiered on its two STATED deadlines, never on `close_time`.** A settled or single-instant event closes every rung together: in the archive 681 of 1,821 dated same-event pairs have a close gap of ZERO days and 13 are ordered the wrong way round by realized close. The arithmetic is DR-73a's `scanner.stated_deadline` (wording → the last calendar day the deadline includes, cross-checked across the market's own fields) and `scanner.same_event_ladder` (two days → `(swap, gap_days)`, the `SAME_DAY` sentinel, or `None`). **Branch on `is None` / `is SAME_DAY`**: `SAME_DAY` is a non-empty string, so `if ladder:` is true for it and `swap, gap = ladder` would unpack eight characters and raise. (2) **The stated gap travels with the pair and has exactly one reader.** `CandidatePair.stated_gap_days` is the gap that ordered the legs, chose the tier and was checked against `MAX_DEADLINE_GAP_DAYS`; `scanner.pair_gap_days(pair)` returns it when it is a real `int` — by TYPE, since a genuine 0-day gap is falsy and `bool` is not an `int` here, the fail-safe rule `leg_sides` follows — and falls back to `deadline_gap_days`. Every downstream site that re-derives the tier goes through it: `_pair_max_sum`, the one definition of the price-sum ceiling enrichment and `validate_pair_price` both read (enrichment's old `ref_yes is None` fallback, which re-derived the tier as a second, separate test, is gone, since a time-series pair with no fresh reference now fails closed), and the live spread rule's gap — enrichment's `time_series_spread_refusal` calls and the `live_time_series_floor` its below-floor WARNING prints, and `validate_pair_price`'s band-ceiling test. Pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_pair_ceiling_reads_the_pair_gap`, which asserts a `pair_gap_days` call and the *absence* of a direct `deadline_gap_days` call in `_pair_max_sum`, `_enrich_pair` and `validate_pair_price`, and BY VALUE by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestEnrichmentRefreshesReferenceQuote::test_ceiling_tier_is_the_stated_gap_for_a_ladder`. A missed site re-tiers a one-instant ladder from the 0.70 ceiling to 0.85. (3) **The loop variables were renamed to `m_outer`/`m_inner` and each candidate binds `mA, mB = m_outer, m_inner`.** A ladder may SWAP its legs, and `mA` used to BE the outer loop variable, so an in-place swap would have leaked into every later `m_inner` of that iteration; `find_same_title_pairs` already used fresh locals for exactly this reason. Pinned by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestSameEventDeadlineLadders::test_a_swap_does_not_leak_into_later_candidates`. (4) **The branch keeps its OWN counters** (`ladder_disabled_skips`, `ladder_no_event_skips`, `ladder_identical_wording_skips`, `ladder_snapshot_skips`, `ladder_no_deadline_skips`, `ladder_same_deadline_skips`, `ladder_undated_skips`, `ladder_field_conflict_skips`, `ladder_same_day_skips`, `ladder_gap_cap_skips`, `ladder_price_sum_skips`, and the live spread rule's `ladder_floor_skips`, `ladder_ceiling_skips`), each on its own silent-at-zero INFO line, and its gap cap sits INSIDE the branch so `gap_cap_skips` keeps counting exactly the cross-event candidates it counted before. Folding them into the DR-72 counters was measured to move `snapshot_skips` 13,043 → 13,055 and `price_sum_skips` 0 → 2 — precisely the blurring DR-72 had just split apart. `ladder_identical_wording_skips` is the LARGEST single ladder refusal on the 2026-09-22 snapshot (502 of 3,354), so leaving it as a bare `continue` dropped 15% of the branch's input out of the funnel with nothing in the log to reconstruct it from. `ladder_disabled_skips` stays FIRST, ahead of the empty-ticker and identical-wording checks, deliberately: it is the funnel's first arrow (3,354) and an operator must be able to read that number off a switch-OFF log — it says what the switch held back, not that every one of them would have paired. The undated/conflict split is made by re-reading the rung with the cross-check disarmed (`stated_deadline(profile, "", "", "")`, which reads only the three FIELD arguments), so there is no second copy of the reader. (5) **One always-logged line while the switch is on** — "Same-event ladder pairs among the time-series pairs: %d", zero included — because a switch that silently produces nothing must be distinguishable from one that is working and finding nothing (DR-66). (6) **Fails CLOSED at every step**, like the wording rule it sits behind: an empty shared event ticker, identical wording (both now counted on their own lines), a non-cumulative verdict, a deadline that states no placeable day, fields that name irreconcilable days, both rungs naming ONE day, or a stated gap over the cap all refuse the pair. (7) **Admitting is not purely additive.** Both finders keep ONE best pair per group, so a ladder can DISPLACE a cross-event candidate by having the wider `pB − pA` — a diff of two runs' pairs tables is not purely an insertion. No cross-event pair is ever re-priced or re-ordered by this change (verified: with the switch off the 2026-09-22 snapshot replays byte-identically through this tree and its parent, same pair lists and same log lines but for the new disabled-count line, `gap_cap_skips` still 52 and `snapshot_skips` still 13,043; with it on the same replay emits 24 pairs in 24 events, all tradeable — under the tier-on, no-band rule then shipped; 48 in 48 events, 47 tradeable, at the 2026-09-27 defaults). (8) **The deadline the outputs RENDER is still `close_time`.** The prod log's and the dev simulation's "A Deadline"/"B Deadline" columns and `main.print_pairs_table`'s render `market_*.close_time`, which for a ladder is the exchange close rather than the stated deadline the pair was ordered and tiered on — already true of 1 of the 24 the 2026-09-22 snapshot emitted under the rule then shipped (`KXMEDIARELEASEDATEAHS-26`, stated gap 8, close gap 7). That is a reporting gap, not a pricing one: nothing sizes or settles on those cells.

**The backtester mirrors it (DR-73c), and one run can override the switch.** The wording and one-series rules are on both paths precisely so the backtest cannot replay a defect the live path has (see the What NOT To Do bullets); a ladder-enabled LIVE run measured by a backtest that still refused every same-event pair would measure the wrong strategy. **One standing caveat**: the two paths pick the rung by DIFFERENT rules — each live run keeps one pair per group, ranked tradeable-first then by largest `pB − pA`, before Kelly, while the backtest (since DR-76) takes, Monday by Monday, the Kelly-passing candidate with the largest `entry_monthly_ratio` whose ladder is free — so they can replay different rungs of the same ladder; on the 2026-09-22 snapshot, under the live rule shipped until 2026-09-27 (tier floors on, no band), the live funnel narrows 87 eligible ladder candidates to 24 emitted, so that contest decides 63 of them (at the defaults shipped since, the same snapshot emits 48; the eligible count was not re-measured). **And the setting now reaches the dashboard too, not just `kalshi_backtest.log`**: a later commit (the scenario-explorer PR) added `dashboard._run_settings_html(sweep)`, one line printed under the page's "Period:" header — `Primary spread band: … | same-event ladders: on / off / not recorded | per-trade cap: … (size-cap sweep …)` — read straight off `BacktestSweep.same_event_ladders` and `primary.spread_band` (and, for the cap clause this branch appended after the ladder one, `primary.size_cap` and whether `cap_sweep` ran and the filter bar could use it), the ladder reading a recorded on/off followed by `(same as this checkout's config)` or by a departure note — so a ladder-enabled run's dashboard is no longer indistinguishable from a switch-off one and no longer needs hand labelling. When a run's setting departs from the configured switch, three places say so: that header line (`… (departs from this checkout's config.TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS, which was on|off: not a replay of its live rule)`, reading `backtester`'s binding through `BacktestSweep.config_same_event_ladders`, which the sweep records), the pre-fetch echo (`ladders=on|off (config: off|on)`, from `backtest.py`'s own binding, before any sweep exists) and `run_backtest_sweep`'s ladder line (from `backtester`'s binding). In production those bindings are the one config value; a test or harness that patches one module's binding but not another makes them disagree — the by-value trap described below. They compare against THIS checkout's config, which is what the live bot trades only when it runs the same code. Both settings shape the population every section reports on (the ladder switch decides which pairs exist, and the band decides which of them are ever entered — it acts inside `_find_entry`, after pair extraction), so, like DR-66b's strike-blind notice, they belong in the header every section is read beneath, not only inside the scenario explorer. "not recorded" prints rather than a guess whenever the sweep carries no value at all — no sweep (the four-positional `generate_dashboard` call) or a hand-built one. Six things are load-bearing on that side. (a) **`backtester._extract_pairs(groups, *, same_event_ladders=None, series_categories=None)` forms ladders in a SEPARATE per-event sub-pass**, not by relaxing the windowed cross-event sweep, which keeps its own `continue` on equal event tickers unchanged — so the two populations cannot overlap and no cross-event pair is re-ordered or re-counted. The buckets carry POSITIONAL indexes into the sorted `dated` list, because `group_profiles` is positional. (b) **The sub-pass is deliberately UNWINDOWED.** The close-date window exists because `_find_entry` caps a CROSS-EVENT pair on its close gap, while a ladder is capped on its STATED gap: in the archive 463 pairs would be admitted on their close gap with a stated gap beyond the cap, and exactly 1 sits the other way — inside the cap on its stated gap while its realized closes are further apart — which a windowed sub-pass would silently drop. Pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestExtractPairsSameEventLadders::test_a_35_day_close_gap_with_a_20_day_stated_gap_is_still_formed`. (c) **It is O(B²) per event bucket, not the sweep's O(N·window)**, and a canary covers ONE runaway bucket: a single same-event bucket past `LARGE_GROUP_WARN_THRESHOLD` logs a WARNING naming the event. Measured bucket sizes (2026-09-22): max 26 on the live snapshot's 113,303 markets (`KXNHLHART-27`; 3,704 same-event candidate pairs in all) — counted over every actively-STATUSED market, which is what `_extract_pairs` sees, where `config.TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS`' live funnel quotes 3,354 and max bucket 21 for the same snapshot AFTER the live price filter, so the two pairs of numbers describe two populations rather than disagreeing — max 3 on `live_days/2026-09-08.json.gz` (391,609 records, 6 candidates), max 75 on a strike-blind legacy slice where a blank subtitle collapses a daily strike family onto one key — `archive_days/2026-05-01.json.gz` reaches 615,266 candidates that way. **The canary would stay silent through an exact repeat of that 615,266 figure** (its largest bucket is 75, against a 1,000-member threshold): the AGGREGATE cost is bounded by measurement instead, and it is small — that whole sub-pass costs +0.3 to +0.5 s against a ~19 s switch-off call (measured 2026-09-23), paid only with the switch on — every default run since the 2026-09-26 decision. The whole-run cost is larger than that sub-pass: on the 365-day window from 2025-09-24 (2026-09-26, one cached corpus) the default run went from 22m05s with ladders off to 22m50s with them on, fetching candles for 2,377 tickers instead of 1,462, with peak RSS after pair extraction 2,129 MiB instead of 2,106. A per-call aggregate guard is the alternative and is deliberately not built. (d) **With the switch OFF the sub-pass does NO pairwise work at all**, not even the bucketing, which is why that 615,266 figure cannot be paid on a switch-off run; the disabled census (`ladder_disabled_skips`) therefore stays LIVE-only, where the memoized profiles make it free. What the backtest does report with the switch off (since M10) is the same-event candidates its windowed cross-event sweep already visits and skips — counted as they are visited, so at no pairwise cost of its own, and not the live census's number (a windowed subset, over a different population — see the DR-67 observability paragraph). The backtest also has no `ladder_price_sum_skips` — `_extract_pairs` applies no price filter of any kind — and its always-logged line counts CANDIDATES ("Same-event ladder candidates among the time-series candidates: %d") because which rung trades is decided later here, in `_simulate_at_discount`. (e) **`_find_entry` gains a ladder branch gated on the SAME flag, never on `event_ticker` equality alone** — a caller that builds a same-event pair itself must not get ladder semantics from a switched-off tree. It orders and gaps on the stated deadlines and returns `None` when either cannot be read or both name one day, and it classifies PER PAIR: a deliberate, commented exemption from DR-70's once-per-market rule, bounded by the ladder pair count (975 over the whole 284-slice archive at ≤ 30 days, ~12.3 µs each) and impossible to memoize in a function that receives two dicts. `scan_end` and the `max_horizon_days` check still read `close_time` ON PURPOSE: they bound when candles exist and when a market is open, which is an exchange fact about the listing, where the stated deadline is a fact about the question. (f) **The flag rides UNRESOLVED through `_prepare_candidates(..., same_event_ladders=None)` — which hands it to both `_extract_pairs` calls and stores it on the returned `_Candidates` — into every `_entries_for_band` pass's `_find_entry` calls** (`_prepare_entries` composes those two halves directly; `run_backtest_sweep` composes `_prepare_candidates` with `_sweep_from_candidates`, which runs the `_entries_for_band` passes) — one resolution, at call time, and the same one on both sides only while `backtester.TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS` is not rebound between the halves (see `_Candidates`); a def-time default would freeze the constant at import and ignore both a monkeypatch and a run-level override. **What that call-time lookup resolves is `backtester.TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS`, the module's own by-value binding of the config constant — patching `config.TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS` is a silent no-op here, exactly as it is for `scanner`.** It is NOT the `time_series_profit_prob(k=None)` idiom, which works only because that helper lives in `config.py` and reads config's own global: `scanner.py`, `backtester.py` and `backtest.py` each bind this constant at import, so a test or harness patches the MODULE it is exercising (the suite does: `backtester.…` and `backtest.…`, never `config.…`), and a backtest's supported lever needs no patching at all. `run_backtest_sweep(..., same_event_ladders=None)` passes it through and logs the resolved value WITH ITS SOURCE; `run_backtest()` takes no ladder parameter (its positional parameters are unchanged, and its one keyword-only parameter is the /series listing, DR-79) and passes nothing, so it reads the config; and `backtest.py --same-event-ladders / --no-same-event-ladders` (`BooleanOptionalAction`, `default=None`, echoed beside `k` in the pre-fetch summary) is how one backtest departs from the switch — `--no-same-event-ladders` replays the pre-2026-09-26 rule — and `scanner.py` binds the constant at import, so a backtest override can never reach the live finder. **The AST pin is now both-sided:** `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_both_finders_apply_the_same_event_ladder_rule` asserts the two `scanner.find_time_series_pairs` calls, the two `backtester._extract_pairs` calls, `_stated_deadline_dict`'s single call to `stated_deadline`, and `_find_entry`'s calls to both helpers. **A ladder-enabled backtest is not comparable to any baseline taken without it** — and since the 2026-09-26 decision every DEFAULT run is ladder-enabled, so a default-run figure recorded before then is a switch-off baseline — different pairs, so different trade counts, returns, equity curve and drawdown — and `_simulate_at_discount`'s premise-violation counter can only RISE, never fall. The sub-pass is purely ADDITIVE — no cross-event pair is re-ordered, so none stops violating — and every ladder pair that clears the Kelly gate and settles A=YES/B=NO is a new count. Measured on a 1,506-market archive corpus: switch off 1,490 pairs, switch on 1,898, the off list an exact ordered subsequence of the on list (0 reversed, 0 lost, 408 added), and **0 of those 408 ladder pairs settle the impossible cell** against 207 of the 1,490 cross-event ones (13.89%) — so on that corpus the counter does not move at all.

**Cross-strike time-series pairs — the group key must carry the outcome label (DR-01, fixed 2026-09).** A Kalshi daily price family lists dozens of strikes under ONE market title (event `KXBTCD-26SEP1517`: 80 markets, all titled "Bitcoin price on Sep 15, 2026?", the strike only in the subtitle — "$67,500 or above" … "$87,250 or above"). While the time-series key was `normalize_title(pair_key(m))`, every strike of every deadline in such a family landed in ONE group, and the one-best-pair rule (tradeable first, then largest `pB − pA`) then selected the highest earlier strike against the lowest later one: two different questions sized and traded as one cumulative-deadline pair, with no in-between cell at all. Measured on the 2026-09-15 prod dry run's market snapshot, roughly 974 of 979 tier-qualifying combinations in one BTC group were cross-strike; a second read of that snapshot counted 1,021 of 1,027, the two differing in how the group was delimited rather than in the finding — both are over 99%, and it is the proportion, not the count, that the fix rests on. And **four of the six trades that dry run selected were cross-strike** (a fifth was the same-series pair DR-02 names); the six together were ~98% of the cash balance. No money was lost only because the scheduler daemon has never been started. **The fix is one helper on both paths.** `scanner.time_series_group_key(combined_title, subtitle)` returns `"<normalize_title(pair_key)> | <normalized subtitle>"` (the title alone when the subtitle is empty, `""` when the title itself normalizes away), and BOTH `scanner.find_time_series_pairs` and `backtester._group_by_normalized_title` call it — the latter, since SS-1, through `backtester._ts_group_key`, the backtester's one definition of the key, which `_index_eligible_keys` also reads to decide which records are materialized for grouping at all — pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_both_finders_group_through_the_shared_key_helper` (a two-link chain on the backtester side), because the backtester previously keyed the same wrong way and therefore reproduced the defect rather than detecting it. Three details are load-bearing. (1) **The subtitle is normalized by `scanner._normalize_subtitle`, NOT `normalize_title`.** That pass strips any bare `20xx` token as a year, and a subtitle is exactly where a bare 4-digit strike ("2050 or above") lives — running it over an outcome label would merge every strike in 2000–2099 into one key, reintroducing DR-01 through the subtitle. `_normalize_subtitle` strips only the unambiguous DATE shapes — `_EXPLICIT_DATE_PATTERNS`, every entry taken BY INDEX from `_DATE_PATTERNS` so a copy can never drift: the month-name / abbreviated-month / numeric / ISO shapes that carry a year, the deadline-anchored month + day shape from detail (2), and the pre-existing bare abbreviated month + day — plus trailing punctuation, so "$80,000 by June 30" and "$80,000 by July 31" are one strike at two deadlines and "Donald Trump" and "Donald Trump." are one option label. Whitespace and punctuation are trimmed in ONE `rstrip`, because a stripped date leaves a space in FRONT of any trailing punctuation ("$80,000 by June 30." → "$80,000 by .") and trimming them in sequence left that space in the key, silently dropping a genuine same-strike pair. (2) **`_DATE_PATTERNS` gained ONE shape — deadline preposition + full month name + day ("by June 30") — placed AHEAD of the `by/before/until/through/after <Month>` clause** — that clause's optional day group only matches when a year follows, so from behind it consumed "by June" and stranded the day, leaving "30" against "31" to split two titles that differ only in their deadline. **It is anchored to the preposition deliberately.** A bare `<Month> <day>` also erases the date from SNAPSHOT titles ("... on June 30"), which would put two snapshot markets of one family into a single time-series group — the shape DR-67 now screens at pair formation on the legs' wording, with the backtester's counter as defence in depth. Anchored, the pattern fires only where that clause already fired and stranded a day, so no title that used to stay apart is merged; the ABBREVIATED spelling ("by Oct 1") is deliberately left behind the clause and still splits, unchanged. `tests/test_scanner.py::TestNormalizeTitle` is the gate CLAUDE.md names for any `_DATE_PATTERNS` change, and it now pins both directions ("by June 30"/"by July 31" merge; "on June 30"/"on July 1" do not). (3) **Non-string subtitles read as absent, never raise** (a `MagicMock` auto-attribute, `None` from an old cache record) — the same fail-safe-by-type rule `leg_sides` and `strategy._depth_levels` follow. **Cache caveat:** backtest day slices written before the 2026-08 subtitle fix carry `subtitle=None`, so a backtest over them still groups time-series candidates by title alone and stays strike-blind; refreshing them is the deletion procedure in the subtitle-drift gotcha below. **And the guard is only as good as the field.** `subtitle` is normalized in at ingest from `subtitle`/`yes_sub_title` (see that same gotcha, which records that the field comes and goes): if BOTH ever go missing again, every market keys with an empty outcome label and the time-series key silently reverts to the pre-fix, strike-blind `normalize_title(pair_key(m))` — with real money on it and no warning of any kind; and when only ONE leg of a genuine pair carries the label, the two legs key apart and the pair is silently dropped (a missed trade, also unlogged). **The live and backtest paths have DIFFERENT symptoms, and the long-standing counter covers only one of them.** `scanner.find_time_series_pairs` — the LIVE finder, and the counter's only home — logs `Distinct normalized title+outcome keys with >= 1 market`; a count collapsing toward the market count, or a time-series pair count dropping to zero, is the symptom to check first on the live path. That is the ONE-leg-labelled case, where the two legs key APART and keys SPLIT. It says nothing about a backtest, for two independent reasons: the backtester never calls that function (it calls `_group_by_normalized_title` directly), and when BOTH legs are unlabelled the keys MERGE instead, so the count moves the other way entirely. `backtester._log_outcome_label_coverage` is the backtest-path signal (DR-66; since SS-1 `_prepare_candidates` runs its two halves, `_OutcomeLabelTally` and `_report_outcome_label_coverage`, directly — see below): an INFO census of `subtitle` and `event_title` coverage over the eligible markets on every run, escalating to a WARNING naming the consequence and the cache-refresh remedy when subtitle coverage falls below `config.BACKTEST_OUTCOME_LABEL_WARN_FRACTION`. It exists because a backtest over a pre-2026-08-14 cache reported pair counts, trades, a return figure and an empirical `k̂` recommendation for `TIME_SERIES_INTERVAL_PROB_DISCOUNT` that all described the strike-blind strategy, while being indistinguishable in its own output from a run on a good cache — measured on the assembled 2026-05-01 cache: 2,344,886 records, 98.14% with a blank subtitle and 87.89% with a blank event title, against ZERO blank subtitles on either post-fix assembled cache. **The DASHBOARD says so too, and that half is DR-66b.** The log alone was not enough: `backtest.py`'s log ends by pointing the operator at the HTML (and then at its "Save as live defaults…" button), and a dashboard generated from a run whose census had logged 0.00% coverage contained none of the strings "subtitle", "coverage", "strike-blind" or "cache" anywhere in its 140 KB, rendering a bare `Pooled empirical k̂` card — a recommendation for the real-money `TIME_SERIES_INTERVAL_PROB_DISCOUNT` — with no caveat of any kind. (A reviewer was fooled by exactly this and reported a pooled `k̂` of 1.114 upstream as a finding about the strategy. "The number is arithmetically correct for the corpus it was handed" is the same defence that would have excused the log silence, and it is not enough.) `_log_outcome_label_coverage` therefore RETURNS the frozen `OutcomeLabelCoverage(total, with_subtitle, with_event_title, subtitle_fraction, event_title_fraction, below_floor, cumulative_markets, snapshot_markets, unknown_deadline_markets)` it just logged — same single pass, same position in `_prepare_candidates` (the first half of `_prepare_entries`), same log wording byte-for-byte (since SS-1 `_prepare_candidates` uses the census's two halves directly: it feeds an `_OutcomeLabelTally` inside its first walk over the corpus and logs through `_report_outcome_label_coverage` at that same position; `_log_outcome_label_coverage` is their one-shot composition and still accepts any iterable, walked exactly once) — which rides out on `_Candidates.label_coverage` onto `BacktestSweep.label_coverage` and into `_section_interval_discount` (`_prepare_entries` also returns it as element 1 of its pair, which `run_backtest` discards). Four things are load-bearing. (1) **A RETURN VALUE, not an optional sink.** An `emit`-style callback (the idiom `_fetch_archive_day` already uses) would have cost zero test edits, but a caller can FORGET it and silently lose the caveat — reproducing DR-66's own failure mode inside the mechanism built to close it. The tuple cannot be omitted; the defaulted `BacktestSweep` field is the one remaining place an omission is possible, which is why both production constructions pass it together — true only since DR-71 (fixed 2026-09) made the three phrasing fields (`cumulative_markets`/`snapshot_markets`/`unknown_deadline_markets`) REQUIRED, with no default: before it, a construction that forgot them silently defaulted to `0, 0, 0`, a second, unmentioned place an omission was possible — and a bad one, since it made the page's strongest sentence ("could not have produced a time-series trade at all") render as if measured. A forgotten phrasing keyword is now a `TypeError` at construction, the same "cannot be forgotten" reasoning this return value already used. The nine constructions that pass all three explicitly (counted 2026-09-23 by grepping `OutcomeLabelCoverage(` over `kalshi_betting/` and `tests/`): two in `backtester.py` (the empty-corpus branch passes `0, 0, 0` because that IS a measurement, and the main branch reads them off the census); one in `tests/test_backtester.py` (`TestOutcomeLabelCoverageIsCarried::test_the_sweep_carries_the_census`); five in `tests/test_dashboard.py` (the module-level `_coverage` and `_scn_coverage` helpers, `TestOutcomeLabelCoverageIsRendered::test_the_verdict_is_carried_not_recomputed` and `::test_an_empty_corpus_is_not_reported_as_zero_percent`, and `TestDeadlinePhrasingIsRendered._coverage`, whose `**base` dict carries all three); and one in `tests/dashboard_golden.py` (`_coverage`). A tenth, `tests/test_backtester.py::TestOutcomeLabelCoverageIsCarried::test_the_phrasing_fields_are_required`, deliberately omits one of them per case to prove the `TypeError`. (2) **ONE measurement, so the page and the log can never disagree** — including the threshold, which is evaluated once and carried as `below_floor`. Measuring over `_interval_calibration`'s entries instead was rejected for the sharper reason that it would measure the SURVIVORS of the very grouping under suspicion, and would vanish entirely on a same-title-only run — exactly the run where the lost same-title discriminator (TS-11) is the whole exposure. (3) **The rendered figure describes the ELIGIBLE-MARKET CORPUS, not the `k̂` population**, which is a far smaller, differently-selected subset (entered, binarily settled, non-premise-violating time-series candidates). Phrase it as "N of M eligible markets", never as "of the pairs behind `k̂`", and never put it inside the calibration table, whose rows are per-bucket counts of the `k̂` population. The banner's consequence sentence is the one claim that legitimately spans both. (4) **`subtitle_fraction`/`event_title_fraction` are `None`, not `0.0`, on an empty corpus** (undefined rather than zero), and the carrier holds scalars only (counts, fractions and one verdict) and no reference to any market dict — enriching it with sample tickers or a per-category breakdown would pin every record alive past `_prepare_candidates`' release of the corpus and the groupable subset and undo TS-07's residency work. Every number rendered is this run's own count or the configured floor; the historical figures above stay in `config.py` and this file, never in an emitted string (TS-07). **Concentration moved too:** the one-best-pair-per-group rule used to bound a whole daily family to ONE pair, and now bounds one STRIKE to one pair, so an N-strike family can contribute up to N pairs — all on the same underlying over the same window. `strategy.select_portfolio` used to dedup tickers only, with no per-event or per-family cap; since DR-76 it takes at most one time-series trade per ladder (per event, or per question), and a daily family lists every strike of one deadline in ONE event, so any two of its pairs that share a deadline's event never both trade in one run — and a later run refuses a new pair on a ladder the account still holds (see the DR-76 gotcha). Two strikes' pairs over four different events share no label and can still both trade. **Sibling (DR-17): the outcome label is now visible.** `scanner.display_title` appends " — <subtitle>" unless the subtitle IS the label already (`market_title` falls back to it when the title is empty), which is what the prod Excel "Market A"/"Market B" cells render; and `main.print_pairs_table` gained its own "Outcome A"/"Outcome B" columns, because `main._truncate` cuts the two title cells at 40 characters and a real `display_title` is well past that — so appending alone would have left the four cross-strike rows of that dry run looking identical, which is exactly why nobody caught them in review. `main._print_portfolio` — the log of the trades actually SELECTED — names both tickers for the same reason: it prints `canonical_title[:55]`, and a time-series canonical title now ends with the outcome label, which that cut drops. **A strike spelled inside the TITLE is a different case and is NOT addressed here** (DR-26, deliberately out of this plan): a `$80k`-shaped strike survives `normalize_title`, so two such markets already key apart, but a bare 4-digit one is eaten by the standalone-year pattern and nothing added here guards that.

**`normalize_title()` pattern order matters.** `_DATE_PATTERNS` in `scanner.py` applies patterns sequentially. Longer/more specific patterns must come before shorter ones to avoid partial matches leaving date fragments. If adding new patterns, prepend them before similar shorter ones.
