**Identical wording across two events of ONE series is two fixtures, not one question (DR-02, DR-54, fixed 2026-09).** A Kalshi event ticker is a series prefix followed by an instance stamp (`KXNPBRFI-26SEP160500FUKORI`, `KXTTELITEMATCH-26SEP101835KKAMOL`, `KXMVECROSSCATEGORY-SHARD1-S6471E4699E9`), so two events of ONE series are two instances of one recurring fixture — two ball games, two 15-minute price windows, two combos of different games. The date lives only in the event ticker, the event sub-title, the close time and the rules text — none of which any grouping key reads — so the `event_ticker` inequality guard both finders already had *passes* on exactly this shape. Identical wording across them is the same question asked about two DIFFERENT events, and neither strategy's premise survives it: the 95% co-resolution prior is simply false (the 2026-09-15 dry run's NPB pair — `KXNPBRFI-26SEP160500FUKORI-Y` and `…150500FUKORI-Y`, identical title, subtitle and event title, closes 24 h apart — was quoted 0.97 and 0.01, and `strategy._kelly_p` priced it at the flat `SAME_TITLE_CO_RESOLVE_PROB` 0.95, sizing 16 contracts a side for $12.39 against a reported "$3.61 profit floor" whose true expected value, on the two markets' own prices with independent games, is negative), and there is no cumulative-deadline premise either, because the deadline lives entirely outside the wording. 71 of that run's 75 same-title rows were this shape. **The rule is fixture identity, not close time** — for the ONE-SERIES rule; since DR-74 a close gate sits ON TOP of it for CROSS-series same-title pairs (see the next gotcha), as a second condition, never as a replacement. The sweep's adversary critic measured 117,801 identical-wording NON-combo pairs off the settled cache: they co-resolved 96.3–98.2% of the time in EVERY close-gap band, so no threshold isolates the bad ones (that figure pools both sides of this rule — the same-series pairs it now rejects and the different-series ones it keeps — and it is an UNCONDITIONAL rate, whereas the finder only ever prices a pair the market has already diverged 5% on; treat it as the reason a close-gap gate was rejected, never as a co-resolution estimate for what survives) — while identical-wording COMBO pairs closing within one hour of each other still settled differently 13.8% of the time (an admitted example closed 96 seconds apart and settled yes vs no). A one-hour gate was therefore rejected: it would drop pairs that co-resolve almost always and still admit two matches of one table-tennis player (`KXTTELITEMATCH-26SEP101835KKAMOL` vs `…101810MOLJMI`, about half an hour apart, settled yes and no) and two consecutive 15-minute gas windows. (That rejection was of a one-hour gate as a REPLACEMENT for the series rule, measured on a different corpus. DR-74's one-hour gate is an ADDITIONAL condition on pairs the series rule already admits, and on its 365-day corpus identical-wording non-combo pairs settle the same way 68.2% of the time same-series and 65.6% cross-series, while cross-series pairs settle the same way 99.2% of the time at a 0 s close gap against 60.7% beyond 1 min — so on the cross-series side proximity DOES separate the two, which is all DR-74 relies on.) **Both finders carry it, and that is the whole point.** `scanner.find_same_title_pairs` skips a candidate when `_same_series(m_outer, m_inner)` (its group key already guarantees identical wording), counting the skips and reporting them once as `Same-title candidates skipped as two instances of one event series (identical wording, different fixture): N`; `scanner.find_time_series_pairs` skips when `_identical_wording(mA, mB) and _same_series(mA, mB)`, and since M10 counts those skips too (`Time-series candidates skipped as two instances of one event series (...): N`), as `backtester._extract_pairs` does on both of its branches — see the DR-67 observability paragraph for the exact lines. Every one of these lines is silent at zero, and counting moves no control flow: M10's pair lists were compared byte for byte with its parent's on seven synthetic corpora (one combo-heavy, six seeded fuzz), ladders on and off, through both `_extract_pairs` branches and both live finders — 28 cases, every one identical. Gating only the same-title finder would have RELABELLED the trade rather than removed it: the same two NPB tickers clear the time-series filter too (`pB − pA` = 0.96, gap 1 day), and `main._dedup_pairs` only ever dropped that copy because a same-title copy existed — 39 of that run's 75 same-title rows would have come back as time-series pairs. `backtester._extract_pairs` mirrors both branches through `_same_series_dicts` / `_identical_wording_dicts`, which derive the prefix from the same `scanner.event_series`, so live and backtest can never disagree about what a series is; all of it is pinned by AST in `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_both_finders_apply_the_one_series_rule` and `::test_ast_the_series_prefix_has_one_definition`. **`event_series` fails CLOSED.** An empty, hyphen-leading or non-string event ticker yields `""`, and `_same_series` reads an unknown prefix on either side as the SAME series — a pair whose fixture identity cannot be read must not be priced on the co-resolution prior. **What still pairs under THIS rule:** two DIFFERENT series asking one question (the shape the same-title strategy was built for) — and, since DR-74, only when their two markets also close within 1 hour of each other; and any pair of ONE series whose deadline is IN the wording — `KXSOLD-26SEP14` / `KXSOLD-26SEP18` titled "Solana price on Sep 14/18, 2026?", or the FIDE Oct/Nov markets from the same run, make `_identical_wording` false. Note both of those named examples are nonetheless refused as TIME-SERIES pairs by DR-67, on the separate ground that "price on \<date\>" and "top-10 in \<month\>" are snapshot wordings; `tests/test_scanner.py::TestOneEventSeriesIsTwoFixtures` pins both verdicts on the same fixture, so a reader can see which rule decides what. **This rule decides fixture identity only, in both directions.** It does not certify that a dated one-series pair is genuinely CUMULATIVE — "price on <date>" is exactly the SNAPSHOT shape the time-series paragraph above names (SOL ≥ $180 on Sep 14 does not imply SOL ≥ $180 on Sep 18; top-10 in October does not imply top-10 in November). **DR-67 now answers that separately**: the cumulative-deadline rule refuses both of those examples on their wording, so the `KXSOLD` Sep 14/18 pair and the FIDE Oct/Nov pair named above as "what still pairs" are eligible under THIS rule and refused by that one. And different series is a NECESSARY condition for a same-title pair, never a sufficient one: the same prop listed for two different games in two different leagues shares its wording too (`KXBRASILEIROB1HTOTAL-26SEP10VINGGO-1` and `KXUCL1HTOTAL-26SEP10MUNSBH-1`, both "Over 0.5 1H goals scored", settled no and yes), and only the `event_title` component of the same-title key separates them — which live ingest populates but pre-`event_title` cache records leave `""`, so the backtest path grouped them where the live path usually did not. Since DR-74 they are refused on BOTH paths unless the two games close within 1 hour of each other: the close gate reads no `event_title`, so it does not depend on the cache carrying one. **The accepted cost:** a genuine cumulative-deadline pair of ONE series whose deadline appears *only* in the event ticker — never in the title, subtitle or event title — is dropped along with the recurring fixtures, because at that point nothing in the data the finders read separates the two. That is deliberate, not an oversight: the same-title finder had exactly this ambiguity before and resolved it the other way, with real money on it. **What stops entirely:** every same-title pair between two MVE combo EVENTS. Kalshi lists combos under several series prefixes, all beginning `KXMVE` — a census of `backtest_cache/event_titles.json` (3,996,906 keys, 2,444 distinct prefixes, measured 2026-09-16) found exactly four: `KXMVECROSSCATEGORY` (2,960,840), `KXMVESPORTSMULTIGAMEEXTENDED` (906,157), `KXMVECROSSCATEGORY0` (94,230) and `KXMVENBASINGLEGAME` (61), with no `KXMV*` prefix that is not also `KXMVE*` — so `scanner.event_series` collapses the whole family onto one series (`config.MVE_SERIES_FAMILY_PREFIX = "KXMVE"`, DR-55) and the one-series rule treats any two combo tickets as two instances of one recurring fixture. Reading the literal prefix DID refuse two combos listed under ONE of those prefixes; what it could not see was a pair spanning TWO of them, which read as two different series and was priced on the 0.95 co-resolution prior. That exposure is measured, not assumed: in `backtest_cache/live_days/2026-09-14.json.gz` (9,009,087 records, 8,939,229 of them `KXMVE*`), 10,643 distinct `(event_title, title, subtitle)` wordings appear under TWO literal `KXMVE` prefixes — every one of them `KXMVECROSSCATEGORY` x `KXMVECROSSCATEGORY0` — forming 26,072 cross-prefix market pairs of which 17,829 co-resolved and **8,243 settled DIFFERENTLY, a 68.4% co-resolution rate against the 0.95 prior**. That 68.4% carries the SAME caveat as the 96.3–98.2% above and for the same reason: it is an UNCONDITIONAL rate over every record in each cross-prefix wording class, with no price, liquidity or activity filter, whereas the finder only ever prices a pair the market has already diverged 5% on — treat it as the measured reason the family rule exists, never as a co-resolution estimate for the pairs that would actually have been traded. A FAMILY PREFIX rather than an enumerated allowlist, because enumerating the combo series would BE an allowlist and the codebase's own prior belief ("every combo sits under `KXMVECROSSCATEGORY`") was already such an allowlist with one of the four entries — the artefact that was measurably wrong here — and it rots every time Kalshi adds a combo series (`KXMVENBASINGLEGAME`, 61 keys, appears in no day slice at all yet). Over-collapsing is the safe direction since `_same_series` only ever REFUSES pairs, so its worst case is a missed trade between two genuinely independent `KXMVE*` series, or a still-eligible runner-up promoted where the refused pair was a group's best candidate, never a trade priced on a false premise. The family literal is deliberately narrow — prefix containment among ordinary series is common (`KXART` vs `KXARTISTSTREAMS` are unrelated), so it must never be widened to `KXMV` or to a generic containment rule. That is a pairing rule, not an ingest exclusion, and it is the intended reading of DR-54; if combo duplicates are ever wanted the discriminator is `mve_selected_legs`, which ingest does not carry today. **Backtest impact:** the `Potential pairs` count drops (fewer, correct pairs), `_can_ever_enter` is untouched so there is no `SETTLED_PREFILTER_CACHE_TAG` bump, and any later profiling baseline must be taken after this change. **Skipping is not only subtractive.** Both finders keep ONE best pair per group, so removing a group's top candidate PROMOTES the runner-up: a group whose widest gap was a one-series look-alike can now yield a time-series pair on tickers the previous code never proposed at all (pinned by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestOneEventSeriesIsTwoFixtures::test_skipping_the_widest_candidate_promotes_the_next_one`). That is intended — the promoted pair is the one the group actually supports — but it means this rule can ADD a trade as well as remove one, so a diff of two runs' pairs tables will not be purely a deletion. **Test fixtures moved with the rule:** every synthetic same-title pair a FINDER actually forms was re-pinned onto distinct series — the one-prefix `EVT-A`/`EVT-B` and `EV-A`/`EV-B` fixtures in `tests/test_scanner.py` and `tests/test_backtester.py` became `EVA-1`/`EVB-1` or `EA-1`/`EB-1`, and the eight event tickers in `tests/test_main.py`'s `_build_events` became eight distinct series. Fixtures that only exercise GROUPING, or that build a `CandidatePair` directly without going through a finder (`_st_candidate`, the enrichment and reachability pairs), keep their one-prefix tickers because no finder ever reads them. The synthetic TIME-SERIES fixtures also kept their one-series tickers and had their titles dated instead (`_ts_pair_markets`, both `TestTimeSeriesBestPairPerGroup` groups and `TestTimeSeriesGrouping::test_pricier_earlier_contract_is_not_a_candidate` now spell each market's own close date, the shape a real two-deadline family has) — that last one matters because without the dated titles the one-series rule, not the directional price filter, was what made its assertion pass. No assertion changed in either group.

**Identical wording on two DIFFERENT series is one question only when both markets close at the same moment (DR-74, fixed 2026-09).** The one-series rule above is NECESSARY for a same-title pair, never sufficient, and this is the shape it misses. The 365-day backtest (`--start-date 2025-09-24`, same-event ladders off — their value until 2026-09-26) made 21 trades, all same-title, for +4.7%, max drawdown −48%, and −37.5% without its best event. Rebuilt with the backtester's own functions, 17 of them paired a **men's** and a **women's** college basketball game between the same two schools — `KXNCAAMBGAME-26JAN13WIUEIU-WIU` vs `KXNCAAWBGAME-26JAN13WIUEIU-WIU`, identical title ("Western Illinois at Eastern Illinois Winner?"), identical subtitle and event title, on two different series, so the one-series rule let them through. One more paired the Champions League and La Liga "Atletico vs Barcelona" matches, 10 days apart; only the 3 Brent/copper weekly-vs-monthly trades asked one question. The 95% co-resolution prior (`SAME_TITLE_CO_RESOLVE_PROB`) is false for such pairs, and **the exposure is live**: the live finder uses the same key and the same series rule, and the 2026-09-23 prod dry run (`kalshi_arb.log:13954-13961`) formed 4 same-title pairs whose two legs close on different days — among them "India A Women vs Australia A Women", 2026-10-06 vs 2026-09-27, and "Texas vs Tennessee", 2026-09-27 vs 2026-09-28. None traded only because the price filter's fee check found them untradeable (each A leg was quoted 95% on both outcomes — an empty book — so their fixture identity is inferred from the dates, not the quotes). Each of the four closes at least 16 h apart.

**The rule.** A same-title candidate is refused when its two markets close more than `config.SAME_TITLE_MAX_CLOSE_GAP_SECONDS` (`60 * 60` — 1 hour) apart, or when their closes cannot be compared: it fails CLOSED like `_same_series`, on anything that is not a datetime and on one naive close beside one aware close. `scanner.closes_apart(close_a, close_b)` is the one definition, with a strict `>` (a gap of exactly the bound is admitted); `scanner._closes_apart(mA, mB)` is the live reader (reading `.close_time` by TYPE, so a `MagicMock`'s auto-attribute fails closed), called in `find_same_title_pairs` immediately AFTER the `_same_series` check, so the two older counts do not move and each candidate lands on exactly one line. `backtester._extract_pairs`' same-title branch mirrors it after `_same_series_dicts`: `backtester._comparable_closes_dicts` parses both cached close strings (`None` when either is absent or unparseable, or one naive and one aware) and the branch calls `closes_apart` on the result. Pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_both_paths_apply_the_same_title_close_gap` (which also asserts the ABSENCE of the gate from `find_time_series_pairs`) and `::test_ast_the_close_gap_has_one_definition`, and by value in `tests/test_scanner.py::TestSameTitleCloseGap` / `::TestClosesApart` and `tests/test_backtester.py::TestSameTitleCloseGapBacktest`. The gate reads `scanner`'s binding of the constant, so a test narrows it by patching `scanner.SAME_TITLE_MAX_CLOSE_GAP_SECONDS`, never `config.*` — the `TIME_SERIES_SAME_EVENT_LADDERS` precedent — and that one patch narrows the backtest's gate too, since there is no second copy. It also moves the bound both refusal lines PRINT: each formats it through `scanner.close_gap_bound_text()`, which reads that same binding at call time (whole minutes as minutes, anything else in seconds, so a patched bound is never truncated into a false one), and `backtester` imports no copy of the constant at all — pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestSameTitleCloseGapBacktest::test_the_gate_is_the_scanner_definition`, which asserts the backtest line under a patched bound, by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestClosesApart::test_the_bound_text_never_misstates_the_bound` / `::test_the_bound_text_reads_the_gates_binding_at_call_time`, and by the AST pin `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_the_close_gap_has_one_definition`, which asserts both line sites call the helper.

**Why close time, and why 1 hour.** Checked against live and archived Kalshi payloads on 2026-09-25. The event's `product_metadata.competition` label separates both bad shapes ("College Basketball (M)" vs "(W)"; "Champions League" vs "La Liga") but is absent for commodities, and over the 1,906 backtest candidates both sides carried it on 1,377, neither on 459 and one side on 70. The event `sub_title` is identical for a same-day M/W doubleheader. A series' `settlement_sources` cannot tell UCL from La Liga and costs an extra `/series/{t}` call. Series titles always differ and tags never do. A market's `rules_primary` is free text the backtest cache lacks, and so are `occurrence_datetime`/`expected_expiration_time`. A series-ticker allowlist rots (DR-55). `close_time` is carried by both paths already. A read-only replay of the real `_extract_pairs` → `_find_entry` → `_simulate_at_discount` over the 365-day corpus's 1,906 same-title candidates (competitions from the public `/events/{t}` for 1,101 events; the regression critic independently reproduced the first and "close" rows):

| Today (no extra gate) | 1,906 | 65.6% | 25 | 21 | +4.7% | −48.0% |
| Same competition (refuse when both named and different) | 635 | 79.1% | 6 | 3 | +4.8% | −1.9% |

Both rules leave the same 3 trades — the Brent/copper weekly-vs-monthly pairs, 6 entries of which cash funds 3. Every candidate closed either at the identical instant or at least 1.17 h (4,217 s) apart: 245 pairs at a 0 s gap, 243 of which settled the same way (including 226/226 commodity and 15/15 financial; the other 2 are the Black Phone 2 anomaly below), and 1,661 pairs at least 1.17 h apart, ~60.7% of which settled the same way — the nearest of those a men's/women's UMES at NCCU pair, which settled the same way, and the nearest that settled DIFFERENTLY 2.17 h apart. What the close rule refuses and the competition rule keeps — 102 `KXMLBGAME`/`KXMLBSTGAME` pairs (39% settled alike) and every unlabelled category (e.g. Mentions) — makes no trade difference here: of the 1,661 refused pairs only 196 were ever open together (183 at a Monday checkpoint), 0 of the 153 mention pairs and 4 of the 140 MLB-vs-spring-training pairs. **1 hour is the operator's choice (2026-09-25)**, and gives exactly the 1-minute and 15-minute result on this corpus; what it gives up is margin (see the residuals). The population is the backtest's: `event_title` is blank on ~95% of its eligible records.

**With the scheduled close on (`config.BACKTEST_SCHEDULED_CLOSE`, the default),** the backtest's gate compares the closes a live run would have read: `_extract_pairs(defer_close_gate=True)` hands back the candidates that passed every check up to the gate, `_prepare_candidates` reads their markets' timing (`market_details.load_market_details`, cached) and the scheduled close their series' rule gives, and `_close_gate_and_labels` applies `scanner.closes_apart` to those closes (the realized close where a series has no rule) and then the label check, on the same two lines, with one more silent-at-zero line counting the pairs it judged otherwise than on the realized closes; with no series rule, or the switch off, the gate runs inside `_extract_pairs` exactly as before. **Where it holds on both paths: games and same-instant listings.** Live `close_time` is the SCHEDULED close — for a game, tip-off plus a fixed number of days, which keeps a doubleheader's two games hours apart — and the backtest's is the REALIZED close. Checked on archived payloads: WIU at EIU M/W 2.5 h apart scheduled (01:30 vs 23:00) and 2.7 h realized; TN Tech at TN St. M/W 2.5 h scheduled; UCL/La Liga days apart on both; Brent weekly/monthly both 21:00Z, identical on both.

**`find_time_series_pairs` is deliberately NOT gated.** Since DR-67 identical wording cannot form a time-series pair (it states the same deadline spans, or none), so a same-title-only gate cannot relabel the trade — the concern that put the one-series rule on BOTH finders (see the dedup note under Key Patterns). A close gate there would also refuse every genuine cross-event deadline pair, whose closes differ by design. The relabel guard is pinned on both paths, undated ("Q") and dated ("by Dec 31, 2026"): `tests/test_scanner.py::TestDeadlineGuardFinders::test_identical_wording_closing_apart_forms_no_pair_of_either_type` (no pair from either finder, nothing out of `main._dedup_pairs`) and `tests/test_backtester.py::TestRunBacktestCrossTypeDedup::test_identical_wording_closing_apart_forms_no_pair_of_either_type` / `::test_nothing_reaches_the_dedup_when_the_closes_differ`.

**Observability.** `find_same_title_pairs` logs one silent-at-zero INFO line, `Same-title candidates refused because the two markets close more than 60 minutes apart (two different games or instants, not one question listed twice): N`, with its own prefix and neither "one event series" nor "same event ticker", which other tests pin as ABSENT. `_extract_pairs` logs that line VERBATIM (pinned by `TestSameTitleCloseGapBacktest::test_the_close_gap_line_is_the_live_finders_verbatim`, on a fixture of two non-combo series that reaches the gate) plus a BACKTEST-ONLY `Same-title candidate pairs refused because a market's close_time cannot be read (fail closed; the live finder drops such markets before grouping): N`. The split exists because the two paths do not see the same population: the cache's closes are all aware (9,156,436 of 9,156,436 end in Z), but a same-title group is not close-filtered — `_can_ever_enter` keeps a market whose close it cannot parse — while the live finder drops a missing `close_time` before grouping. **The two close-gap lines are verbatim twins for READABLE closes only.** A pair whose closes cannot be COMPARED — one naive beside one aware — is refused on both paths, but lands on different lines: the backtest counts it on its unreadable-close line (`_comparable_closes_dicts` returns `None`), while the live finder, whose gate has ONE line, counts it on the close-gap line, whose wording ("close more than 60 minutes apart") is then not literally true of it. That is unreachable today — every Kalshi close string ends in `Z`, so `_market_from_dict` parses them all aware, and every cached close does too — and it fails closed either way; it would first show up on a payload drift that mixed the two formats. `TestOneSeriesAndSameEventCounts::test_every_candidate_is_returned_or_counted_exactly_once` sums both new counts into its same-title identity. `main._no_pairs_msg` names the condition (since DR-79 "same-title: ≥5% price diff on two different series filed under one Kalshi category, tag and competition, closing within 60 minutes", the bound read from the constant and rendered by `scanner.close_gap_bound_text`). With the same-title branch now refusing an undatable pair, the pipeline no longer hands `_fetch_candles_parallel` a market without a readable close, so BS-07's branch there is defence in depth, kept covered by a direct test (`TestRunBacktestMalformedTimestamps::test_the_candle_fetch_still_resolves_an_unparseable_close_to_nothing`).

**Not changed:** `main._dedup_pairs` and the pairs table, `reporter` and its pinned columns, `trader`, `historical` (no new data), `dashboard` and its goldens, `backtest.py`, `_can_ever_enter`, `SETTLED_PREFILTER_CACHE_TAG` and the assembled caches — the rule acts at PAIR time, so every cache stays valid (the DR-02 precedent). **Test fixtures moved with the rule** — the exact gate broke 18 tests, each fixed rather than loosened: `test_dated_identical_wording_is_same_title_only` (`tests/test_scanner.py::TestDeadlineGuardFinders` and `tests/test_backtester.py::TestRunBacktestCrossTypeDedup`) keeps its name and now holds an ALIGNED fixture closing at one instant — its positive control `mB3` shares that instant too, so the control still differs from the refused case in WORDING alone — while the differing-close expectation, NO pair of either type, moved to the new `test_identical_wording_closing_apart_forms_no_pair_of_either_type` on both paths (the relabel guard above), and `TestRunBacktestCrossTypeDedup` keeps `_MARKETS` as its differing-close fixture beside a new `_ALIGNED_MARKETS`; `TestTimeSeriesTieredThreshold::test_same_title_pairs_ignore_deadline_gap`, which pinned that a pair closing 20 days apart "is still a candidate", was inverted deliberately (now `test_same_title_pairs_ignore_the_deadline_tier_but_not_the_close_gap`); `TestActiveTickerRelease` was redesigned (TY now closes with TX and carries a second-Monday candle at 0.60, because aligning its close alone brought TY/TZ inside the 30-day cap); `_p5_corpus` gives each same-title family's two legs one close, the later of the two draws, with its rng draws unchanged; `TestOneSeriesAndSameEventCounts` gained the two prefixes and its `_fuzz` copies its source's close onto every other re-listed duplicate (keyed on the index, no extra draw); a few same-title fixtures (`TestExtractPairsCanonHandling`, `TestOldCacheToleranceMissingTickAndSubtitleFields::test_extract_pairs_same_title_handles_missing_fields`) gained a shared close locally — never in the shared `_md`; and SS-1's `_ss1_corpus` (read by `TestGroupableSubset`, `TestPrepareCandidatesOverASettledCorpus` and `TestPrefilterLinesSayItRanDuringAssembly`) broke no test but silently lost almost all of its same-title coverage — its fill's 50-day random close spread left 4/5/4 same-title pairs for seeds 0/1/2, against 126/117/93 before the gate — so the fill records whose titles can only ever pair as same-title (`_SS1_SAME_TITLE_ONLY_TITLES`) now close at one shared instant, restoring 78/65/50 while the Rain/Snow fill keeps its random closes (so the gate's refusals stay covered too), the rng stream and every time-series pair unchanged, and `TestGroupableSubset::test_prepare_candidates_equals_grouping_the_whole_eligible_list` now asserts at least 40 same-title pairs so that coverage cannot silently drain again.

**Known residuals of DR-74.** Recorded here, not fixed.
- **Placeholder closes (live only).** Futures that can be decided early carry a family-wide PLACEHOLDER as their live `close_time` — all 30 live `KXOSCAR*` events close at 2027-12-31T15:00Z, and 20 of 34 `KXEARNINGSMENTION*` events at 2027-03-31T14:00Z — so two such listings pass live at a 0 s gap while the backtest separates them by realized close. The competition rule would not catch this class either (no label). The backtest's version of the class: 89 co-open non-game pairs refused only on realized close, 71 of which settled the same way (e.g. `KXNCAAFPLAYOFF` vs `KXNCAAPLAYOFF`: 54 pairs, 43 alike). Not a regression — these pairs were admitted before DR-74 too. An admitted cross-series pair is at most 3,600 s apart by construction, so no dry run can reveal a placeholder close; review each admitted cross-series pair by hand for a family-wide shared close.
- **Simultaneous or close tip-offs (live).** A M/W pair whose scheduled closes fall within an hour of each other passes the close gate live: two games at the same time (neutral-site tournaments), or back-to-back games less than an hour apart. The M/W pairs checked were 2.5 h apart. Two different sports starting within an hour of each other pass it too (the 2026-10-16 football/volleyball incident). Both shapes are refused by the sport and competition check since DR-79 (see the next gotcha), not by this gate.
- **1-hour margin (backtest).** The nearest refused candidate on the 365-day corpus closed 1.17 h from its partner (UMES at NCCU, M/W), ~10 minutes beyond the bound, so a M/W pair whose games END within an hour of each other would be admitted there. None did on this corpus. A 15-minute bound would have left 1 h of margin at no cost in trades; the operator chose 1 hour.
- **Realized-close drift (backtest only).** 4 duplicate listings, `KXTOPSONGTOTALGOL-2..5` vs `KXTOPSONGTOTALGOLDEN-2..5`, are refused because their realized closes drifted 1.5 h apart (all settled "no"; none entered). The album-vs-song-chart pairs are rightly refused (their sibling family settled differently on 6 of 13).
- **Settlement anomaly.** `KXRTBLACKPHONE2` vs `KXBLACKPHONE2` have identical rules and close, yet one settled two months later with the opposite result (2 pairs). No rule can see it.
- **Missed trades.** A genuine listing on two series with different scheduled closes is refused (fail closed) — a missed trade, never a false one.
- **Promotion.** Both finders keep ONE best pair per group, so refusing a group's best candidate PROMOTES its runner-up (pinned by `TestSameTitleCloseGap::test_refusing_the_widest_candidate_promotes_the_runner_up`), and a pairs-table diff is not a pure deletion.
- **Baselines.** Pre-DR-74 same-title baselines (21 trades / +4.7% on the 365-day run) are not comparable.

**Identical wording on two series that close together is one question only when both are the same sport in the same competition (DR-79).** **What happened.** At 00:39 PT on 2026-10-16 a real-money run (`live_runs/20261016T073903Z-eedc8d1bd2538639`, code `main` @ 21e25f4) bought two same-title pairs, each joining a women's college volleyball market (`KXNCAAWVMATCH-26OCT161900UKOU`) with a college football market (`KXNCAAFGAME-26OCT16UKOKLA`): "Kentucky wins" (NO on volleyball, YES on football, 137 each, $90.22) and "Oklahoma wins" (NO on football, YES on volleyball, 137 each, $90.30). The bot priced them on the 95% co-resolution prior; they are two different games. The next Live-tab snapshot shows all four positions gone and the cash up about $95, which suggests they were sold by hand for about −$85.49 (inferred from the cash; the fills were not read). Every older same-title check passed: the group key (event title "Kentucky vs Oklahoma", title "Kentucky wins", outcome "Kentucky") on both; two event tickers; two series (DR-02/54); closes 23:00Z and 00:00Z, exactly 3,600 s apart, which the 1-hour gate admits (DR-74); and the 0.95 vs 0.32 YES asks cleared the 5% gate. None of them looks at what sport or competition a market belongs to, and Kalshi's API says it plainly: the series are tagged **Volleyball** and **Football** in its /series listing, and the events' `product_metadata.competition` labels are **"NCAA Volleyball (W)"** and **"NCAA Football"**. **Why it is common now.** Since about August 2026 Kalshi titles a game "<Team> wins" under an event "A vs B", which names neither the sport nor the date, so the same two schools word every sport they both play identically; and a scheduled close is the start time plus a fixed offset (45 h for football, soccer and volleyball), so two sports starting within an hour of each other always pass the close gate — which checks time, not identity (even a 0 s gate passes two sports starting at the same moment). The backtest never showed it: it compares the times games actually ended, which for two different games are hours apart, and the volleyball leg was listed too briefly to reach a Monday checkpoint; it lacked the same check, which it now applies too (see "Where it is read (backtest)" below).

**The rule.** Besides the older checks, two markets form a same-title pair only when (1) both series are filed under one Kalshi category and first tag — `scanner.labels_differ` over `historical.series_labels`' (category, tag), the filing rule the dashboard and the live category/tag filter use, letter case ignored — and (2) both events name the same competition, or neither names one — `scanner.competitions_differ` over `product_metadata.competition` ("NCAA Football", "College Basketball (W)"), letter case and surrounding spaces ignored, one event naming a competition and the other none counting as different. Rule 1 catches two sports or two subjects; rule 2 catches a men's and a women's game, two leagues and two formats of one sport, which share a tag (both "Basketball", both "Soccer"). Both fail closed: labels that are not a (category, tag) pair of strings refuse the pair, and so does a competition that cannot be read — `scanner.event_competition` gives "" for an event that names none (no `product_metadata`, none in it, or a null one) and `None` for a label that is there but is not text (a `product_metadata` that is not an object, a competition that is not a string), and `competitions_differ` refuses anything that is not a string. **Where it is read (live).** `find_same_title_pairs` applies them right after the close gate, in that order (`_labels_differ`, then `_competitions_differ`), each on a silent-at-zero INFO line of its own — `Same-title candidates refused because the two series are filed under different Kalshi categories or tags (two different sports or subjects, not one question listed twice): N` and `Same-title candidates refused because the two events name different competitions (two leagues, genders or formats, not one question listed twice): N` — so the older counts do not move and each candidate lands on exactly one line; neither line contains "one event series", "same event ticker" or "close more than", which other tests pin as absent. `held_pair_kind` applies the same two checks in its same-title branch, so a held pair from two sports or two competitions is no known kind and live trimming (`seller.plan_trims`) never prices it on the same-title chance of paying. `find_time_series_pairs` carries neither, for the reason it carries no close gate: identical wording is never a time-series pair (DR-67). **Where the labels come from.** The competition is read at ingest: `scanner.event_competition(ev)` in both of `fetch_open_events_with_markets`' event loops, handed to `_market_from_dict` and kept as `ApiMarket._event_competition` ("" for none, `None` when unreadable); `scanner._fetch_held_market` reads it the same way for a held market it looks up. One always-logged line, `Markets whose event names a competition: N of M`, shows a label Kalshi moved or renamed (every event then reading as naming none) as a count near zero, and a WARNING counts the markets whose label is not text. The category and tag come from Kalshi's /series listing: every run reads it once, right after the market fetch and the blind-run check (`load_series_categories(client)` in production — the cached file, refreshed with one GET a week; `load_series_categories(None)`, the cached copy only, in dev), and `main._attach_series_labels` sets `ApiMarket._series_labels` on every market before the sale block (since `plan_trims` reads `held_pair_kind`) and both finders; the same copy then goes to the category/tag filter and the run result. With no listing at all, `_attach_series_labels` logs one WARNING (`Same-title sport check: no /series listing this run, so every series is filed by its ticker prefix and only the competition check tells two series apart`). A scheduled run with no filter and no result file now reads it too. `main._no_pairs_msg`'s same-title clause reads "same-title: ≥5% price diff on two different series filed under one Kalshi category, tag and competition, closing within 60 minutes". **Measured** (read-only, by this change's own agents, 2026-10-16): on the live market list, 30 same-title candidates paired two different sports or competitions. The 1-hour close gate already refuses 28 of them, and the new rule refuses the other 2 (the incident) on the label line. With the close gate set aside the rule refuses all 30, split by the listing it reads: against a fresh read of each series, category and tag stop the 4 football-vs-volleyball candidates and the competition label the other 26 (EPL vs WSL, women's ODI vs T20, Sheffield Shield vs ODI (M), Ligue 1 vs the French women's league); against the cached copy a run that day reads (fetched 2026-10-10, under a week old, so not refreshed), which does not list the French women's league series `KXFRAPLWGAME` yet and so files it by its ticker prefix, the split is 7 and 23, the 3 Ligue 1 candidates moving to the label line. It loses no genuine pair; the 245 same-title pairs the 365-day backtest corpus admits (Brent, copper, natural gas and silver weekly-vs-monthly listings, Treasury yields and Black Phone 2) all share a category and tag, and none of their events names a competition (checked on Kalshi). **The one known cost:** the France snap election is one question filed under Politics in one series and Elections in the other, so that pair is now refused live (scheduled closes only; it is not in the backtest). **Known limits, recorded rather than fixed.** Two brand-new series the weekly listing does not hold yet are both filed by their ticker prefix (`infer_category` and "General"), so when neither event names a competition they are compared on wording and time only, as before; a new series beside a listed one is usually refused, since its fallback labels rarely match a listed series' own. An empty listing (no cached copy and a failed read) files every series by its ticker prefix, so rule 1 passes nearly everything while rule 2 still applies (the WARNING above says so). A one-sided competition label is refused, so the CFP qualifiers families, where only one of the two events names a competition, are refused too. A market looked up outside the run's list (`scanner._fetch_held_market`) carries no series labels (its competition is read from its event), so it never reads as a same-title pair; `plan_trims` never trims a pair with such a market anyway. The 5% price gate still does not question a very large gap between two "identical" markets (0.95 vs 0.32 here). **Where it is read (backtest).** The backtest applies the same rule through the same two definitions, in two steps, because a cached market carries its series but no competition. (1) **Labels**, in `backtester._extract_pairs`' same-title branch, right after the close gate: each market's series is filed by `historical.series_labels` (worked out once per series, `infer_category` as the fallback for a series the listing lacks, as `main._attach_series_labels` files a live run's markets) from the listing `_extract_pairs(..., series_categories=)` is handed, and `scanner.labels_differ` refuses two series filed differently, on a line VERBATIM equal to `find_same_title_pairs`' label line (pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestSameTitleSportCheckBacktest::test_the_lines_are_the_live_finders_verbatim`); the M10 counts-identity test (`TestOneSeriesAndSameEventCounts::test_every_candidate_is_returned_or_counted_exactly_once`) adds that count. `backtest.py` reads Kalshi's /series listing once, with the production client, before `run_backtest_sweep`, and hands the same object to the sweep and to `generate_dashboard` (so the pairs are filed as the page files the trades); `run_backtest_sweep`, `run_backtest` and `_prepare_entries` take it as a keyword-only `series_categories` and hand it to `_prepare_candidates`, where `None` reads the cached copy only (`load_series_categories(None)`, no request), and the time-series `_extract_pairs` call takes none. With an empty listing (no cached copy, and a failed read or none made) and same-title groups to check, `_prepare_candidates` logs the live run's WARNING word for word (`Same-title sport check: no /series listing this run, so every series is filed by its ticker prefix and only the competition check tells two series apart`), since rule 1 then passes nearly every pair (pinned by `TestSameTitleSportCheckBacktest::test_an_empty_listing_is_named_in_the_live_runs_words`). (2) **Competitions**, after extraction: `_prepare_candidates` asks `historical.load_event_competitions(live_client, events)` for the events of the same-title pairs still standing — each event's `product_metadata.competition`, read from `/events/{ticker}` by `historical._fetch_event_competition` through `_historical_get` (retried, read-only), parsed by the same `scanner.event_competition` live ingest uses, each read followed by the event-title fallback's pause (`EVENT_TITLE_FALLBACK_RATE_LIMIT_SLEEP_SECONDS`) on `EVENT_TITLE_FALLBACK_MAX_WORKERS` threads, at most `config.EVENT_COMPETITION_MAX_LOOKUPS` (2,000) new events per run in ticker order — and cached in `backtest_cache/event_competitions.json` (`{"competitions": {event_ticker: label}}`, "" for an event that names none; only answers Kalshi gave are stored, so a failed read, a label that is not text, an event past the cap, or one asked with no client is left out and asked again next run, each with a WARNING, and one INFO line, `Event competitions: N events, C from the cache, R read from Kalshi, U unknown`, counts them). Then `backtester._refuse_other_competitions(pairs, competitions)` drops a pair whose two labels differ (`scanner.competitions_differ`), on a line VERBATIM equal to the live competition line, and a pair with an event whose label is unknown on a backtest-only line, `Same-title candidate pairs refused because an event's competition could not be read from Kalshi (fail closed): N` (live ingest reads every event's label as it lists the markets, and refuses a label it cannot read on the competition line, so this line has no live twin) — both silent at zero, both before the `Potential pairs` line, so no candle is fetched for a refused pair. The cache tags and the assembled corpus are untouched (the rule runs at pair time, the DR-02 precedent). **Measured, and what it means for figures:** the read-only measurement for this change found that all 245 same-title pairs the 365-day corpus admits (start 2025-09-24) pass the rule — every pair shares a category and tag and none of their events names a competition — so it should move no backtest figure on that window; the lookup reads 28 events for it (measured 2026-10-16: none names a competition, none unknown), once, since each answer is cached. The dashboard rebuild that would show it is a separate job. **Tests:** `tests/conftest.py`'s session-scoped `_offline_event_competitions_for_the_session` answers every competition read with "" (so no test reaches Kalshi and every older same-title fixture pairs as before) and points both label caches at an empty session directory during class setup; fixtures whose two series fall back to different ticker-prefix categories with an empty listing were given a small listing (`TestOneEventSeriesIsTwoFixturesBacktest._FED_LISTING`), never new expected rows. Pinned by `tests/test_historical.py::TestLoadEventCompetitions`, `tests/test_backtester.py::TestSameTitleSportCheckBacktest`, `::TestSeriesCategoriesReachPrepareCandidates`, `tests/test_backtest.py::TestDashboardHandoff::test_the_series_categories_are_passed_through` and `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_the_backtest_applies_the_sport_check`. Since the backtest compares the times games actually ended, a two-sport pair whose games ended hours apart is still refused by the close gate first and never reaches this check there.

**`subtitle` is not dependable on a market payload — ingest normalizes `yes_sub_title` into it (2026-08 drift).** Live-verified 2026-08-14: the live `/markets` endpoint, `/events?with_nested_markets=true`, and the signed `/historical/markets` archive ALL served payloads with no `subtitle` key; on 2026-09-15 a verification read observed payloads carrying `subtitle` AND `yes_sub_title`, so the fallback is defence against a field that comes and goes, not the only source. The distinguishing outcome label arrives as `yes_sub_title` (e.g. the candidate names under one shared `title` of "Who will the next Pope be?"), with `no_sub_title` carrying the negated phrasing. This is not cosmetic: `subtitle` is the only intra-title discriminator in BOTH grouping keys — the same-title key `(event_title, title, subtitle)` used by `scanner.find_same_title_pairs()` and `backtester._group_by_exact_title()`, and (since DR-01) the time-series key built by `scanner.time_series_group_key()` for `scanner.find_time_series_pairs()` and `backtester._group_by_normalized_title()`. With every market parsing to `subtitle=""` the key collapsed to `(event_title, title)`, so two **different** outcomes sharing one question title on different event tickers grouped as the *same* contract and were traded under the 95% co-resolution assumption — a real-money false positive (reproduced offline: two distinct papal candidates paired with `tradeable=True`). The fix is at ingest only: `scanner._market_from_dict()` and `historical._market_to_dict()` read `subtitle` with a fallback to `yes_sub_title`, so the `ApiMarket.subtitle` attribute and the `"subtitle"` cache key keep their existing names and shape and no downstream code changed. Use `yes_sub_title`, never `no_sub_title` — the negated phrasing would make the grouping key asymmetric between the YES and NO framings of one outcome. Display fallbacks (`scanner.market_title`, `backtester._pair_key`, the backtester's trade labels) read the now-populated subtitle and silently improve: a title-less market falls back to its outcome label instead of the opaque ticker. **Cache caveat:** backtest day slices written before this fix carry `subtitle=None` and therefore reproduce the pre-fix weakened grouping — this is backtest fidelity only, with no live-money exposure — and `--no-cache` does NOT refresh them (day slices are deliberately reused unconditionally, see the sharded-fetch gotcha). A genuine refresh requires deleting `backtest_cache/archive_days/` and `backtest_cache/live_days/` **and** re-running with `--no-cache` (equivalently, also deleting the assembled `backtest_cache/settled_markets_*` cache files — the streamed `.jsonl.gz` and any legacy `.json`, since a legacy file is served whenever no `.jsonl.gz` exists at all) — on a default run the assembled per-`start_date` cache is loaded first and returns before the day slices are ever consulted, so deleting the slices alone changes nothing.

**MVE event-title plumbing.** The live scanner attaches an `_event_title` attribute to each `Market` object inside `fetch_open_events_with_markets()`. The backtest stores it as the `event_title` key in cached market dicts. Both pair-finder paths (`find_time_series_pairs`, `find_same_title_pairs`) and their backtester counterparts (`_group_by_normalized_title`, `_group_by_exact_title`) require this to be set for MVE markets — without it they fall back to grouping by market title (plus, on the time-series side, the outcome label) alone, which would let cross-event option-label collisions through. If you add a new entry point that produces markets, attach `_event_title` (or `event_title` in dict form) before passing into the grouping functions. Old backtest caches written before this change have no `event_title` field; re-run with `--no-cache` to refresh.
