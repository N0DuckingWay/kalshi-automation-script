**Pair dedup prefers same-title.** When both scanners detect the same ticker pair, `main._dedup_pairs(same_title_pairs, time_series_pairs)` keeps the same-title entry — a same-title pair is a near-arbitrage (both legs pay under co-resolution, assumed at 95%), whereas the time-series copy of the same tickers is a directional bet on the in-between mass sized by an operator estimate (`k`). This matches `strategy.select_portfolio()`'s same_title > time_series tie-breaker. Don't flip the argument order. **Dedup is not a safety net, and DR-02 is why.** It only ever drops a time-series copy when a same-title copy EXISTS, so a rule that removed the same-title copy alone would hand the identical trade straight through as a time-series bet — which is exactly why the one-series rule is applied inside BOTH finders (see the one-series gotcha below) rather than here. Since DR-02/DR-54 dedup never sees the one shape it was load-bearing for: an identical-wording pair on two event tickers of ONE series is now dropped by BOTH finders, so there is no same-title copy for dedup to prefer and no time-series copy for it to drop. **Since DR-67 the finders cannot produce a cross-type collision on the same ticker pair at all**: a same-title pair requires identical wording, a time-series pair requires two DIFFERENT stated deadlines, and identical wording states identical spans, or none — never two different ones. Dedup stays as a guard for a collision that could arise another way, and its argument order still matters. **That exclusivity is also why the DR-74 close gate sits on the same-title finder ONLY.** The one-series rule had to gate both finders because, before DR-67, a same-series pair refused as same-title could come back as a time-series bet. A same-title candidate the close gate refuses has identical wording, so it can never be a time-series pair. The gate therefore removes the trade rather than relabelling it, and nothing reaches dedup — pinned end to end by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestDeadlineGuardFinders::test_identical_wording_closing_apart_forms_no_pair_of_either_type` (no pair from either finder, nothing out of `main._dedup_pairs`) and its backtester mirrors in `TestRunBacktestCrossTypeDedup`. The same preference runs through `select_portfolio`'s ladder rule (DR-76, see its gotcha): a same-title pick blocks a later time-series spec on its ladder, while a time-series pick never blocks a same-title one — the rule itself refuses only time-series specs. That does not make it free for same-title: a refused spec keeps its cash, so the greedy walk can then take a lower-ranked spec of either kind that it would otherwise have skipped, or take it at a larger size than it would otherwise have shrunk it to (see the `select_portfolio` paragraph below), and that spec can use up the cash (or a ticker) a later same-title spec needed. So one run's portfolio can lose a same-title trade, or gain a time-series one, beside the specs the rule refused — reproduced on synthetic specs in DR-76's review, when the walk still skipped rather than shrank a spec that did not fit (a $110 balance picks time-series STAR-1, time-series STAR-10 and same-title GOLD-A without the rule, and time-series STAR-1 and RAIN-1 with it); on the 2026-09-27 live books, with nothing held, the rule refused no spec, so nothing moved there.

**The live category/tag filter files a pair as the backtest dashboard files a trade, and lists the categories and tags NOT to trade.** `main._filter_by_category(pairs, settings, listing_client, *, series_categories=None)`, the second pair-list filter beside `_dedup_pairs`, runs right after dedup and before enrichment (so a dropped pair costs no order-book request) in both run modes, and keeps a pair by `config.trade_filter(settings)`, the ONE definition of the filter: the pair's category is not one of `LiveSettings.exclude_categories` and its first tag is not one of `LiveSettings.exclude_tags` (`None` excludes none), where a plain tag ("Basketball") is excluded under every category and a tag tied to a category ("Sports · Basketball", `config.TAG_SCOPE_SEPARATOR`) in that category alone. So every category and tag not excluded trades, one the backtest never saw included (`--exclude-category Politics --exclude-tag "Sports · Hockey"` is every category but Politics, and of Sports every tag but Hockey). Categories and tags are both EXCLUDE lists (categories since the change of 2026-10-23, tags since the later change of that day, below). Matching is case-insensitive; with plain tags only, a pair is kept when neither its category nor its tag is excluded (pinned on random filters by `tests/test_config.py::TestTradeFilter::test_plain_tags_keep_what_two_exclusions_say`, `::test_tied_tags_keep_what_a_category_by_category_exclusion_says` and `tests/test_main.py::TestCategoryFilter::test_random_filters_keep_what_two_exclusions_say`, and a category or tag the filter has never seen by `tests/test_config.py::TestTradeFilter::test_a_category_or_tag_never_seen_trades`). `LiveSettings` refuses a tied tag with a blank half or a tag half reading `any`, and never reads it as a plain tag (`config._check_tied_tags`); a tied tag needs no category named, and one whose category is excluded too is allowed, since it excludes nothing more. It refuses an excluded category written as a tied tag, whatever tags are excluded (`config._check_category_names`: `--exclude-category "Sports · Basketball"` exits 2, `exclude_categories: 'Sports · Basketball' is a tag tied to a category; only a whole category can be excluded, so give just the category name`). A blank half is the dot alone, a name starting "· " or one ending " ·"; "·Basketball", "Sports·" and "A·B" are plain names. `--exclude-category` replaces every saved excluded category for the run, so a category the saved defaults exclude trades this run unless it is listed again, and `--exclude-tag` every saved excluded tag, likewise (no extra WARNING: the field is marked `(default: …)` on the `Live settings:` line and a run sending orders under a flag WARNs). `--category`, `--any-category`, `--tag` and `--any-tag`, the include-list flags they replaced, are hidden from `--help` and exit 2 naming the replacement (`--category no longer exists: the category filter is now a list of categories NOT to trade, and every other category trades. Use --exclude-category NAME (repeatable), or --no-exclude-category to exclude none`; `--tag no longer exists: the tag filter is now a list of tags NOT to trade, and every other tag trades. Use --exclude-tag NAME (repeatable), or --no-exclude-tag to exclude none`; `--any-category` and `--any-tag` point at `--no-exclude-category` and `--no-exclude-tag`), never read the old way. (The filter comes from the saved live defaults' `exclude_categories`/`exclude_tags`, `null` — nothing excluded — in the seed; `main.py --exclude-category NAME`/`--exclude-tag NAME`, each repeatable, or `--no-exclude-category`/`--no-exclude-tag` to clear a saved exclusion for one run.) **The rule is the dashboard's, by construction:** MARKET A's LITERAL series (`historical.series_ticker` — never `scanner.event_series`, which collapses the whole `KXMVE*` family onto one series for the one-series pairing rule, while Kalshi files each literal series under its own category) is looked up in Kalshi's /series listing (`historical.load_series_categories`, cached a week in `backtest_cache/series_categories.json`, `config.SERIES_CATEGORY_CACHE_MAX_AGE_SECONDS`) and filed under its category (`"Uncategorised"` when Kalshi gives none) and FIRST tag (`"General"` when it has none) by `historical.series_labels` — the function the dashboard's Returns Decomposition, filter bar and `k̂` breakdown file by (it moved there from `dashboard.py`, which imports it as `_series_labels`; pinned by `tests/test_dashboard.py::TestReturnsByCategory::test_the_page_files_by_the_one_rule_the_live_filter_shares` and, by value against `dashboard._trade_category`, `tests/test_main.py::TestCategoryFilter::test_it_files_every_pair_as_the_dashboard_files_the_trade`); a series the listing lacks is filed under its ticker-prefix label (`historical.infer_category`, the fallback `BacktestTrade.category` carries) and `"General"`. **While any category or tag is excluded, a pair filed that way is not traded:** its category is a guess from its ticker prefix and its tag reads "General", so it could trade through the exclusion of its real category or tag. `_filter_by_category` leaves out, before the rule is asked, every pair whose market-A series is not a key of the listing or whose event ticker is blank, and logs one WARNING (`Category/tag filter: N candidate pairs left out because their series is not in Kalshi's /series listing, so their category and tag are not known and may be ones this run excludes (series: …, and M more)`, naming up to `config.CATEGORY_FILTER_MAX_NAMED_SERIES` (5) series, the most frequent first; A series newer than the cached listing (refreshed weekly) is the usual case, and its pairs wait for the next refresh. **Directional, deadline-gap-tiered price filter — the run's live spread rule.** `find_time_series_pairs()` admits a candidate only when `config.time_series_spread_refusal(pB − pA, gap_days, settings)` returns `None` — the ONE live definition of the time-series spread rule, shared with enrichment and `validate_pair_price` and applied under the run's `config.LiveSettings`: `pB − pA` strictly positive (the LATER-deadline leg's YES ask priced HIGHER than the earlier's — never `abs(pB - pA)`), at or above the entry floor `config.live_time_series_floor(gap_days, settings)`, and at or under the spread band's ceiling, in that order and with `backtester._find_entry`'s `PRICE_EPSILON` placement. A pricier EARLIER contract is never a candidate: the strategy disputes an outsized in-between mass, and a pair with no in-between mass has nothing to dispute (reintroducing `abs()` would admit such pairs; the forecast reads the mid spread rather than this YES-ask gap, and the sizer refuses a time-series pair whose mid spread is not above zero, which the forecast's zero clamp, `time_series_profit_prob`, would read as riskless). The entry floor is `max(tier, band floor)` with `LiveSettings.tier_floors` on and the band floor alone with it off; the live toggles are the saved live defaults' `tier_floors` and `spread_band` (see the saved-live-defaults paragraph above; the seed saves `False` and `(0.0, 0.5)` — the band floor alone (0, so any strictly positive spread) under a 0.5 ceiling — which are also config.py's `TIME_SERIES_TIER_FLOORS` and `TIME_SERIES_SPREAD_BAND` since the operator decision of 2026-09-27, and `True` and `(0.0, 1.0)` before it: the tier alone and no ceiling, exactly the rule before the toggles existed); `main.py`'s `--tier-floors`/`--no-tier-floors` and `--spread-min`/`--spread-max` override either for one run (the run logs its rule, and a production run that submits orders under a departure from the saved defaults WARNS; see the live-toggle flags note under What NOT To Do). `gap_days` comes from `scanner.deadline_gap_days(market_a, market_b)`, which is order-independent (`abs(close_b − close_a).days`), so the tier never depends on which market was passed first — except for a same-event ladder (DR-73), whose gap is the one between its two STATED deadlines and is carried on `CandidatePair.stated_gap_days`. Downstream of pair formation there is exactly ONE reader of either, `scanner.pair_gap_days(pair)`, and every site that re-derives the floor goes through it: `_pair_max_sum` (the price-sum ceiling, the one definition enrichment and `validate_pair_price` both read), enrichment's spread rule (`time_series_spread_refusal`, and `live_time_series_floor` in its below-floor WARNING) and `validate_pair_price`'s band-ceiling test (`time_series_spread_refusal` again); calling `deadline_gap_days` at any of them would re-tier a ladder whose rungs share a `close_time` from 0.70 down to 0.85. The tier is set by the deadline gap between the two legs: 15% (`MIN_PRICE_DIFF_SHORT_GAP`) when the deadlines are ≤ 15 days apart (`SHORT_DEADLINE_GAP_DAYS`, inclusive), 30% (`MIN_PRICE_DIFF_LONG_GAP`) for 16–30 days; gaps over `MAX_DEADLINE_GAP_DAYS` (30) are never candidates — a wider gap leaves more room for the event to genuinely land between the deadlines, so more of the market-implied in-between mass is real rather than mispricing, and a bigger gap must be demanded before disputing it. **Where each toggle bites.** The floor (tier floors and band floor) is tested in the finder on the two scan-time YES asks, and sets the order-book price-sum ceiling `1 − floor` through `_pair_max_sum` in both `scanner._enrich_pair` (which `enrich_with_orderbook_prices()` calls for each tradeable pair whose two books it read) and `validate_pair_price()` (0.85 / 0.70 with the tiers on; 1.0 at a floor of 0 with them off) — the ceiling applies to the LEG prices (`pA + nB` for time-series, `nA + pB` for same-title — `scanner.leg_prices(pair)`), not to the YES-ask gap, and with the tiers on it already implies an executable gap of at least the tier. The band's CEILING is tested three times: in the finder on the scan-time YES asks — right after the `$1` guard, so a pair refused by both still counts there, and BEFORE the group's one-best contest, so an in-band runner-up represents a group whose widest candidate is refused, exactly as `_find_entry` refuses the Monday; in enrichment on the refreshed reference minus the TOP of the qualifying YES book (the widest spread any size prices at); and in `validate_pair_price` on a freshly fetched book's reference minus its best YES ask. The finder counts a floor refusal and a ceiling refusal each on its own silent-at-zero line (cross-event and ladder apart, per DR-73) and logs the rule it applied on EVERY run (`Time-series entry rule: …`, worded by `config.describe_time_series_rule`); a spread that is not strictly positive stays uncounted, like the direction filter it always was. **`min_price_diff_for_gap()` takes a band floor, `spread_min`** (`config.min_price_diff_for_gap(gap_days, spread_min=None)`), layered on the tier as `max(tier, spread_min)` — `backtester` passes one (`_find_entry` to filter, `_interval_calibration(spread_min=…)` only to label each gap band's tier with the floor its entries cleared, and `_tier_floors_bind` only to ask, both ways, whether a tier sits above a band's floor), and so does `config.live_time_series_floor`, with the run's band floor. **It also takes a keyword-only `tier_floors` switch** (`min_price_diff_for_gap(gap_days, spread_min=None, *, tier_floors=True)`): only an explicit `False` throws it, and the tier is then not applied at all — the result is the band floor alone (`spread_min`, or `0.0` with none), which drives both the gap test and the leg-price-sum ceiling (`1 −` the floor) exactly as `max(tier, floor)` does. Anything but `False` keeps the tier. The backtest's band sweep passes it only through its tier-floors-off family (`run_backtest_sweep(..., band_sweep=True, tier_off_sweep=True)`, which the CLI turns on with the band sweep), re-running the bands whose floor sits below a tier (on the shipped grid floors 0, 0.20 and 0.25 — `backtester._tier_floors_bind`, which asks this helper both ways at every admissible gap) with the tiers off; the live rule passes it through `config.live_time_series_floor`, from `LiveSettings.tier_floors`. At a floor of 0 what is left of the gap and sum tests is that `pB` must EXCEED `pA` — `_find_entry` refuses a time-series Monday whose spread is not strictly positive (`pB − pA ≤ PRICE_EPSILON`), and `time_series_spread_refusal` refuses it first, because a pair with no in-between mass has nothing to dispute; the refusal is inert with the tiers on, where every tier demands more — and a sum ceiling of 1, which the fee check beside them already implies. The backtest's primary band is resolved by `config.time_series_spread_band()`, from the tuple `backtest.py` builds out of `--spread-min`/`--spread-max`, or from `config.BACKTEST_DEFAULT_SPREAD_BAND` when none is given; each grid band of a band sweep goes through the same function, and so does `LiveSettings.spread_band` (which refuses `None` rather than read it as the backtest's default). The live rule and `_find_entry`'s per-Monday tests agree on every spread BY VALUE: `tests/test_backtester.py::TestLiveBacktestSpreadParity` calls the unmodified `_find_entry` across the 36 sweep bands and the no-band band, tiers on and off, gaps 0/15/16/30 and every boundary's float-noise neighbours. The backtester's `_find_entry` mirrors all of it (at the default `tier_floors`), including the DR-73 same-event-ladder gap (DR-73c), which it reads through the same `scanner.same_event_ladder` behind the same switch. Never bypass `min_price_diff_for_gap()` with a flat threshold (the tier-floors-off rule is flat by design and still goes through the helper) — a flat 15% admits wide-gap pairs whose in-between mass is largely genuine. **After enrichment a time-series pair must survive the spread rule on the FRESH book, and three checks keep Kelly under `1 − k` (TS-34, and the live toggles).** These checks live in `scanner._enrich_pair`, which `enrich_with_orderbook_prices` calls for each tradeable pair whose two books it read. `enrich_with_orderbook_prices` used to rewrite only the two LEG prices and leave the non-leg YES ask — `pB` for time-series, `pA` for same-title — at its scan-time value, so `strategy._kelly_p` (whose forecast then read `pB − pA`) subtracted a scan-time `pB` from a depth-weighted `pA`; once the fresh `pA` reached the stale `pB`, `time_series_profit_prob`'s `max(0, pB − pA)` clamp returned `p = 1.0`, the pair modelled as RISKLESS and Kelly sized it at the cap — the same failure the `abs()` warning above describes, reached from the other end. `scanner._reference_yes_ask(pair, ob_a, ob_b)` re-derives that quote from the book already in hand and enrichment writes it back beside the leg fills when that side had resting bids. The refresh costs ZERO extra API calls: in each pair type `_leg_ask_levels` consumes only one side of each book, and on the market that does not carry the YES leg the unused side is exactly the one whose complement is the reference ask (`ob_b["no"]` for time-series, `ob_a["no"]` for same-title); for a time-series pair the other book's unused side, the earlier book's YES bids, is read too, also at no extra call, and its mid spread (`CandidatePair.mid_spread`, the forecast's input) reads the best level of all four sides, both unused ones included (see the DR-78 gotcha's residual (5)). For a TIME-SERIES pair enrichment then applies, in order: (1) it FAILS CLOSED when the later book has no YES ask (no resting NO bids) — the later market then has no midpoint, so there is no mid spread to forecast at and no fresh quote for the spread rule (the scan-time `pB` used to stand in, and under the ask-gap forecast a stale `pB` below the book's own YES bid let Kelly exceed `1 − k`: pA 0.30, bid 0.90, stale pB 0.50 gave f* 0.74 at k 0.75, 0.72 at k 0.80, which no size cap bounds once the per-trade cap is lifted, as it is since the operator decision of 2026-09-27); (2) it drops a CROSSED later book — `ref_yes + no_levels[0][0] < 1 − PRICE_EPSILON`, the market's YES ask below its own YES bid — and then a CROSSED earlier book — `yes_ask_a + no_ask_a < 1 − PRICE_EPSILON`, its top YES ask below its own YES bid (DR-78) — the two states in which Kelly can exceed `1 − k`, since the forecast reads both books' midpoints; (3) the spread rule — positivity and the floor on the reference minus the YES leg's prefix-average fill (the YES-ask gap at what the YES leg pays), the band's ceiling on the reference minus the TOP of the qualifying YES book. Each drop is one WARNING naming its reason. Once the guards pass, every qualifying level has `yes ≤ bid − floor + ε ≤ ref − floor + 2ε`, so the floor cannot fire beyond `2·PRICE_EPSILON`, and the fee truncation (see the marginal-fill paragraph) leaves a spread above the fee: those two checks are defence, and only the ceiling is live there. With neither book crossed, `f* < 1 − k` for every time-series pair — `f* = 1 − k·s/(1 − c)` with `s` the mid spread and `c = pA + nB + fee` at the fills; the later market's midpoint is at least its YES bid `≥ 1 − nB`, and the earlier market's at most its YES ask `≤ pA`, each to within `PRICE_EPSILON/2`, so `s ≥ 1 − c + fee − PRICE_EPSILON > 1 − c` — which is what makes `config.max_kelly_fraction` a true bound for a sizer pricing with the same `k` and cap (see the marginal-fill paragraph). A burst of crossed-book WARNINGs on a healthy exchange is a stop-and-report signal, not something to tune away. **same_title is deliberately NOT guarded**: its model is the fixed co-resolution prior, so `pA` is not a model input and there is no clamp to protect, and it keeps its scan-time reference when that book side is empty. **`validate_pair_price` adds two time-series checks of its own** on the freshly fetched books, beside the price-sum ceiling it already re-applies (which is the floor there): it fails closed when the later book has no YES ask now, and refuses a spread above the band's ceiling on the fresh reference minus the fresh best YES ask — each its own single WARNING. It adds no crossed-book guard and no floor test beyond that price-sum ceiling — but it does cut the fresh book at the first level with no edge left after the fee, `scanner._levels_with_edge_after_fee`, the one definition enrichment prices with, and counts reachable depth only over what remains (a book under the ceiling with no such level is dropped with its own WARNING, `no contract pair on the book now keeps an edge after the fee`). At a floor of 0 (tier floors off, as shipped since the 2026-09-27 decision) the 1.0 price-sum ceiling re-checks no edge at all, so without the cut a book that moved a tick against a thin-edge spec passed, and the FoK caps' one tick of slippage per leg filled it where every settlement cell lost (reproduced on the shipped settings: pA 0.01 / pB 0.03 / nB 0.97 sized at n=748 on $10,000, the book then one tick worse on both legs, win cells −$2.06; pinned by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestValidatePairPriceSpreadRule::test_a_fresh_book_with_no_edge_after_the_fee_is_dropped` and `tests/test_config.py::TestShippedLiveToggles::test_validate_re_checks_the_edge_after_the_fee`). The cut is inert while the ceiling leaves more edge than any fee (every same-title pair and every tier-on rule). **Known residual:** a book that moves AFTER `validate_pair_price`, before submission, can still fill within the caps' tick of slippage per leg, and a spec whose edge is thinner than that then loses in the win cells too — with the tiers on, the 0.85 / 0.70 ceiling's edge of at least 0.15 absorbed that.

**Known residual of the live spread rule: the backtest can enter on an older candle where live now fails closed.** Live enrichment and `validate_pair_price` refuse a time-series pair whose later book has no YES ask at that moment. `backtester._find_entry` has no equivalent — it prices each Monday checkpoint on the latest candle at or before it (`_candle_at_or_before`), which has no staleness bound — so a Monday whose later leg's latest candle is hours or days old is still entered on that older quote, where the live path would have found no current ask and dropped the pair. `_find_entry` does not apply the crossed-book guards either, but the Kelly gate does: a time-series Monday whose quote is crossed on either market (`pA + nA` or `pB + nB` under 1) is skipped there, walked book or not, and counted on its own silent-at-zero line (DR-78), so the entry still counts toward the entries and the `k̂` calibration but never trades on that Monday. Recorded, not fixed: bounding candle age would move every backtest result, and `TestLiveBacktestSpreadParity` pins only the spread tests the two paths share.

**Time-series legs and submission order.** Which side each leg buys is a property of the pair type, read through exactly one helper chain: `config.SAME_TITLE_LEG_SIDES` `("no", "yes")` / `config.TIME_SERIES_LEG_SIDES` `("yes", "no")` — `(side bought on market_a, side bought on market_b)` — exposed as `scanner.leg_sides(pair_type)` (anything but the exact string `"time_series"`, including `None` or a mock's auto-attribute, resolves to same-title, so an unknown pair type can never be traded as the directional bet), `scanner.leg_prices(pair)` (`(nA, pB)` for same-title, `(pA, nB)` for time-series — the two prices everything sizes, fees and submits against; it reads `nB` directly with no default, so any time-series stub must carry a real float `nB`), and `trader._ordered_legs(spec) -> (no_leg, yes_leg)` (frozen `trader._Leg` records: `market`, `side`, `price_dollars`, `count`, `label`). `market_a` is the pricier side by YES ask for same-title and the EARLIER contract for time-series; `nA` is still read for time-series (reporting only — it keeps the prod-log "nA (NO ask)" column meaningful) and `nB` is populated fail-soft (0.0) for same-title (reporting only, never priced). The trader always submits the NO leg first and it is the leg that gets unwound — `market_a` for same-title (today's wire behaviour, byte-identical), `market_b` for time-series — then the YES leg; rollback, the position-delta signs (a NO fill reads as `−count`, a YES fill as `+count`) and the once-per-process V2 NO-mapping backstop all operate on "the NO leg", never on "market_a". Never hardcode `spec.pair.market_a` as the NO leg anywhere — `_ordered_legs` is the only source of truth, and every builder and rollback-price helper (`_build_no_order[_v2](leg)`, `_build_yes_order[_v2](leg)`, `_build_rollback_order_v2(no_leg)`, `_rollback_floor_cents(no_leg)`, `_v2_rollback_price(no_leg)`) takes a `_Leg`, not a spec (`_v2_top_of_grid_price(market)` alone stays market-taking). Label rule for logs and rows: "A"/"B" always mean `market_a`/`market_b` (keep the `(A=%s B=%s)` ticker labels), "NO leg"/"YES leg" always mean submission legs; trader execution and dry-run logs list legs in SUBMISSION order (NO first), while `main`'s pairs table, `_print_portfolio` and the reporter rows list MARKET order (A then B) with the side rendered next to each count (`"5× YES(A) + 5× NO(B)"`). `TradeSpec.x`/`y` are the counts on `market_a`/`market_b` (both `n`), and `TradeSpec.cost_with_fees_a`/`_b` are MARKET_A's/MARKET_B's leg costs whatever their sides — `trader._required_cents_by_shard` pairs them with `market_a`/`market_b.exchange_index`, so that invariant is load-bearing for collateral. The backtester's `_settlement_receipt(n, outcome_a, outcome_b, pair_type)` pays `n` per leg whose market resolved to the side that leg bought (via `leg_sides`; the same-title table is unchanged, the time-series table has exactly three cells and raises `ValueError` on the impossible A=YES/B=NO cell as a defensive guard behind `run_backtest`'s exclusion), and `backtester._leg_prices_for(pair_type, pA, nA, pB, nB)` is the dict-world mirror of `leg_prices`.
