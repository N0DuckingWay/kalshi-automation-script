**Live selling (take-profit) — sales before purchases (operator decision of 2026-10-08).** Until then live trading held every position until it paid out, and only the backtest sold (see the selling gotcha below). A production run whose `LiveSettings.sell_at` is set now sells the held positions that have reached that share of their potential profit, by the backtest's rule, before it buys anything. `sell_at` is `None` in the seed, in config.py and in a saved file that leaves the key out, and a run with it `None` is what it was (two prod dry runs on 2026-10-08, back to back, one on the code from before this change and one on it with selling off, sized the same pairs at the same sizes and logged the same orders; the `Live settings:` line differed by its two new fields, and the market list moved by a few markets between the runs). The weekly scheduler passes no toggle flag, so it sells at the saved level; a dev run holds nothing and never sells. **The rule** is `seller.plan_sales`, which decides and sends no order. A position is an exact held pair (`scanner.held_pairs(..., log=False)`) or a lone held market with exactly one paid-out partner among the account's settlements (`scanner.get_settlements`, asked with `min_ts` for the markets that paid out in the last `SALE_PARTNER_MAX_AGE_DAYS` days and one second, so an unreadable record older than that cannot stop a sale; the seller's own age check still applies to what comes back): a settled market the account no longer holds, held on the other side with the same count (and none on the held side), that carries the held market's question label (`scanner.market_for_labels`, at most `config.SALE_PARTNER_MAX_LOOKUPS` lookups per run), paid out at most `config.SALE_PARTNER_MAX_AGE_DAYS` (60) days ago, settled yes or no, and is not recorded as settling after the run's moment. Anything else is never sold, and a `Not selling …` line gives the reason: a held market's ladder is unknown (then no position is judged at all), three or more held markets share its ladder, the counts differ, one side is held twice, a count or cost is unreadable or too small, the partner is missing or ambiguous, the market is not in this run's market list, or its count is not whole. With `sell_min_days`, `config.days_to_maturity` of the held markets' scheduled `close_time` dates (UTC, against today) must be at least the minimum, and an unknown date fails it; this is checked before anything is valued, so a position too near maturity reads no book. Then come `config.TAKE_PROFIT_HOLD_DAYS` (3) checks, 24 hours apart from the run's moment, the last one now. Now, each held market's real order book (`scanner._fetch_orderbook`) gives its bids for the side held (`scanner.bid_ladder`), walked for the position's count (`scanner.walk_bids`); a book that holds fewer contracts means no sale. Each earlier day reads the market's last hourly candle that ended in the 24 hours before that check (`historical.recent_candles`, never cached, then `historical.bid_before`), as one bid in any size; a partner that had paid out by the check counts at its settlement's revenue, otherwise at its candle bid. A market is worth its count at the price less `config.fee_leg_exact` on that sale (a paid-out partner's revenue carries no fee). The position's cost is each held market's listing exposure plus the fees paid, plus the partner's cost basis on the side held and its fees; its potential profit is its count at `CONTRACT_PAYOUT_DOLLARS` less that cost. The verdict is `config.take_profit_reached` at each check, stopping at the first below the level (so earlier days' candles are read only for positions still in line to sell), and `config.reached_every_check` over all of them: the functions the backtest decides with (see the selling gotcha for its one-line wrappers). A position that gets as far as its checks logs one `Take-profit check (sell at N%): …` line (`-> sell`, or `-> keep` with the share it reached at each check read, which stops at the first one below the level), and a summary reads `Positions to sell this run: N of M`.

**Where live selling differs from the backtest** (all recorded, none fixed): the earlier days' checks read one candle bid in any size (the backtest walks a modeled ladder when it has depth data), and the check now walks the real book; the days rule reads each held market's scheduled close (the backtest the realized close, which for an event decided early is earlier) and only the held markets' (the backtest reads both markets of every trade, a paid-out one's too); the sale's fee is charged once per market (the backtest per trade); a paid-out partner counts at the revenue its settlement reports (the backtest at $1 or $0 a contract by the result), and one that settled other than yes or no is never used; a ladder with three or more held markets, or two of unequal count, is never sold; a ladder whose rungs get different question labels (a question relisted in other words) leaves its lone legs unsold, because the partner must carry the held market's question; and the partner is found by side, count, question and age alone, since the settlements do not say which markets were bought together, so a held market that was never one half of a pair (an orphan left by a failed unwind, or one bought by hand) is judged with a settled market that fits as if it were its partner, and two that fit leave it unsold. Also recorded: the minimum-balance gate (`MIN_BALANCE_CENTS`, on the portfolio value) runs before selling, so a run below the minimum sells nothing, though a sale needs no cash; a lone held market that was partly sold is never judged again, since its partner paid out at the old count and no longer matches it (the rest is held to settlement unless sold by hand); whether selling a held NO (a reduce-only YES bid) needs cash on its shard is not verified — if the exchange refuses it for want of cash, a pair's sale ends `unbalanced` (a CRITICAL, exit 20); and, as in the backtest, a held YES whose candle YES bid is 1c or less has no bid at an earlier day's check (its NO ask sits at the candle fetch's 0.99 clamp, which is not a usable ask), and neither has a held NO whose candle YES ask reads 1.00, so a position whose losing YES leg has gone to about zero is not sold (a held NO near zero still has its 1c bid).

**The sale orders** (`trader.sell_positions`, called once, one position at a time, before any buy). Each held market is sold by one `reduce_only`, `immediate_or_cancel` order for exactly the contracts the plan sells there (all it holds for a take-profit sale, part for a Kelly trim, whose legs carry the holding apart as `held`), never a count read from the account: a held YES by an ask on the YES book (`_V2_LEG_SIDE["close_yes"]`), a held NO by a YES bid (`"close_no"`, the unwind's side). `trader._sale_limit` prices it from the lowest bid the walk reached, on the market's own grid: the YES ask is rounded UP and the NO's YES bid (1 minus the NO bid) DOWN (`scanner.floor_to_tick`), so neither sells below the walk, and the price is kept between `scanner.v2_bottom_of_grid_price` (the one definition of a grid's lowest level, which `v2_limit_price` and the probe read too) and `_v2_top_of_grid_price`. A pair is two orders: first the market with less depth left past its walked count, for the plan's count at no slippage; then exactly as many contracts of the other as the first sold, at no worse than the bid its walk reaches for that count less `config.SALE_HEDGE_SLIPPAGE_TICKS` (1) tick of its grid (where the grid is coarser just past that bid, at a band boundary, the rounding takes the tick back and the order sits at the bid), so what is left is still an exact pair. Before any order of a position, every held market is read (the retried `_position_count`) and must still hold what the plan was made on, on its side (`_holding_problem` over `_plan_holding`: `leg.held`, else `leg.count`), else nothing is sent (`not_sold`); nothing is sent either while `_V2_NO_MAPPING_DISPROVEN` is set. A part fill is a normal outcome of an immediate-or-cancel order, so a reply is read by `_sale_fill_count`, never by `_v2_fill_status`, which raises on one; a reply that says nothing, or a POST that raised, is judged as a buy leg's is, by the account: how far the position moved toward zero (`_contracts_sold`), read again after each pause of `config.V2_MAPPING_ZERO_RECHECK_DELAYS_SECONDS` (1, 2 and 4 s, the schedule the buy path's NO-leg mapping check reads a lagging ledger on) while the move is short of the whole count, stopping at the first reading that shows it; the last reading decides, a reading that fails at any point leaves the count unknown, and those reads are made after the `except` clause has ended. A count that cannot be known stops the position at `manual_review` with no further order. `_sell_leg` returns the count with whether the account decided it, and the sale is marked `SaleResult.decided_by_account`: even the last reading can trail the fills, so `main._run_prod` checks such a sale against the positions listing (next paragraph). For a pair, when the first order's reply is unclear, those retried reads and pauses (up to 7 s of pauses, and up to about a minute more for each read that has to be retried) stand between its two orders while the pair is uneven — the trade-off a buy pair's YES leg makes too, since acting on a reading that may lag could sell the wrong count. `_submit_sale_v2` takes one place on the write pacer and makes one POST, never retried (`tests/test_trader.py::TestSaleOrdersAreSentOnce` pins by AST that it makes one POST after one pacer place with no loop or `try`, that no other sale function posts, and that none reads a reply as a buy leg's). Statuses (`reporter.SaleResult`, a vocabulary of its own): `sold`; `partly_sold` (a pair's rest is still an exact pair); `not_sold` (nothing sold or nothing sent, in a dry run too for a plan whose orders cannot be built; the error says which); `unbalanced` (a pair's second order sold fewer than its first: a CRITICAL naming what each market still holds and the extra to sell by hand); `manual_review` (a CRITICAL; an unexpected error in `sell_positions` is one too, logged with its traceback); `simulated` (a dry run). `unbalanced` and `manual_review` (including a sale `main._run_prod` turns into one because the listing read back shows more sold than an account reading recorded) make the run exit `EXIT_TRADES_NEED_ATTENTION` (20) at every clean return, even when nothing is bought after the sales, and end its closing line with " Sales that need a person to check: N (see the CRITICAL lines above)."; the scheduler's exit-20 message still says "trades" only.

**Where selling sits in a run** (`main._run_prod`). After the balance, positions, ladders and market list are read, and before add-ons or either finder: with `sell_at` set, and only when every held market was identified and the positions listing was read to its end (else one `Not selling this run: …` WARNING, as adding to held pairs fails closed), `plan_sales`, then `sell_positions`, then `_record_sales` writes the sales to `trade_log.xlsx` before anything is bought (a dry run writes them right after sending; a live run that sold writes them once it has read its positions and cash back, or at once if that read raises): a banner row, then one row per position under the 18 trade columns (Status `sold`/`partly sold`/`not sold`/`unbalanced`/`check`/`simulated`; Notes `[sale: YES A / NO B, 84% of potential profit (level 80%), 9 days left, fees=$0.42]`; the cost and profit cells hold the plan's figures, the profit only when the whole position sold). If that write fails the sales are dumped as CRITICAL `RESCUE SALE` lines and the error stops the run before it buys. The dashboard's Live trading tab tells these rows by their Notes prefix, `[sale: ` (`live_portfolio._SALE_NOTE`, so the two change only together), and reads them as the run's sales, never as purchases or warnings, checks their banner's cash just before the run's first fill on the markets their orders may have filled on, and reads the sale orders themselves as sales by hand (see "The Live trading tab" above). `--result-file` gets `sales` (one `SaleRecord` each) and `cash_after_sales`, and `submission_started` is set before the first sale order. A **live** run that sold anything, left a sale for a person to check, or had any sale's count decided by an account reading (`SaleResult.decided_by_account`, even one that read nothing sold) reads its positions again first (`_positions_after_sales`), then the balance. The listing is read once more after `config.SALE_READ_BACK_RECHECK_SECONDS` when it differs from what the sales recorded. When an account-decided sale came up short (`_account_decided_short`: partly sold, not sold or unbalanced), it is read again after each pause of `config.V2_MAPPING_ZERO_RECHECK_DELAYS_SECONDS` (1, 2 and 4 s) instead, stopping early only once a read after a pause shows more sold, since the listing reads the same ledger as the trader's own position reads and may trail the fills too. `_listing_misses_sales` compares it in two directions: fewer sold than recorded means the sale does not show yet; more sold than an account-decided sale recorded (its markets checked even where it recorded 0) means the account reading trailed the fills, and after the wait `_flag_sales_sold_more` logs a CRITICAL (`SALE RECORDED SHORT for '…': <market> held N YES: this run recorded n sold, the account now shows m left (k sold)`, which says it replaces every earlier line about that sale, an `unbalanced` sale's extra to sell by hand included, since those were built on the short count) and turns that sale into `manual_review` with that error, so the run exits 20, its held markets stay blocked for the rest of the run, and the trade log (Status `check`) and the run result (and so the defaults server's run page) show it. For a sale whose replies gave every count, a listing that differs either way is read as not shown yet. A market missing from a listing read to its end holds nothing; one missing from a listing cut short may still be held, so it is not checked in either direction (the value is refused anyway, since the listing was cut short). **Residual, recorded rather than fixed:** a ledger that still trails when the last of those listing reads is made (more than about 7 s of the trader's re-reads plus 7 s of the listing's) leaves an account-decided short count standing, unflagged: a pair's sale reads `not_sold` or `partly_sold`, the run exits 0, and one market can hold contracts alone — the class of lagging-ledger residual the buy path accepts too (DR-63/DR-64); nothing the run can read tells such a lag from the truth. The run then sizes its buys on the cash the sales freed and on Kalshi's value of what is left (`_value_after_sales`, which checks that value as at the start of the run, refuses it while the listing still does not show every sale, and refuses it when contracts were, or may have been, sold but it is not below the value kept at the start (`value_before_sales`), since either way it may still count what was sold — the buys are then sized on the cash alone — and does not apply the minimum-balance check again; only a value that did not fall at all is refused, so one that fell by less than what was sold, still counting part of it, is used); it logs `After the sales: portfolio value $X = cash $Y + open positions $Z`. A **dry run** sends nothing: it adds each sale's estimated proceeds, its count at the average walked price less the fee, floored to the cent, to the cash and to the shard its market is on (`_with_simulated_proceeds`), raises the portfolio value to that cash when it was below it (a start value read as the cash alone grows with the proceeds, as a live run's read back would), and logs `Dry run: sizing as if the sales filled — cash $X (+$Y)`; its sales' banner in `trade_log.xlsx` shows the cash read as both before and after, as its trades' banner does, while the estimate stays in the run result's `cash_after_sales` and that log line. In both, the markets sold stay out of every purchase (`blocked_tickers = (held_tickers - add_on_tickers) | sold_tickers`, which also holds both markets of a sale left for a person), no position the rule picked is added to, whatever its sale's outcome (`_planned_tickers`: `Held pairs picked for sale this run, not added to: N`, so a live run whose sale filled nothing offers the same add-ons as a dry run of the same account), and a sold position's ladders are free again (`held_ladders` is rebuilt from the markets still held), as the backtest frees them. Pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_the_live_run_sells_before_it_buys` (the order of the calls, `dry_run=args.dry_run`, `settings=settings`, the read-backs, `sold_tickers` in `blocked_tickers`) and `tests/test_main.py::TestRunProdSellsInADryRun`, `::TestRunProdSellsLive`, `::TestLiveAndDryRunsAgree` and `::TestSaleHelpers`.

**Measured and first-run order for selling.** Prod dry runs on the live account on 2026-10-08 (read-only): with selling off, the same pairs, sizes and orders as the code before this change; `--sell-at 80`, 16 positions judged and none sold; the first `--sell-at 5` run, the GTA VI trailer pair (`KXGTATRAILER-26NOVE` YES, `KXGTATRAILER-26DEC` NO, 9 each) at 7%, 9% and 12% of its potential profit on its three checks, would have sold for an estimated $6.13 (checked by hand against the live books), and the buys were then sized on $7.67 of cash ($1.54 plus the proceeds); a run on the final code about an hour and a half later judged the same 16 positions and sold none, the pair then reading −2% now. **Before turning selling on:** (1) you run `python3 -m kalshi_betting.v2_probe --ticker <TICKER> --step yes-close` (about one cent, on a liquid, cheap market the account holds nothing on) and see it PASS, since no other step has ever sent the reduce-only ask that sells a held YES; (2) run a prod dry run with `--sell-at` at the level you intend (and `--sell-min-days` if you want one) and check its `Take-profit check` lines against the Kalshi UI; (3) save a Sell scenario from the dashboard's filter bar, whose Save button sends the Sell level and Min. days shown (from a page with the Rebalance view it also sends rebalancing off, since Sell shows a level only while Rebalance is no, which turns saved rebalancing off: to keep it, use the link instead; it sends the categories ticked in the Exclude categories menu and the tags ticked in the Exclude tags menu as the live exclusions, replacing the saved ones), or open `/confirm?tier_floors=…&spread_min=…&spread_max=…&k=…&size_cap=…&sell_at=0.85` (and `&sell_min_days=7`), which names no rebalance setting and so keeps the saved ones, with the five required fields set to the values you want kept (and `exclude_category` and `exclude_tag` when a filter is saved: a link without them proposes nothing excluded), and check that the next run's `Live settings:` line reads `sell at 85% of potential profit`.

**Live Kelly trimming — part sales of a held pair, before buying.** With `LiveSettings.rebalance` on (the saved live defaults', or `main.py --rebalance` for one run; on in the seed and in config.py, off in a saved file that leaves the key out), a production run also sells part of each exact held pair that has outgrown its Kelly share of the portfolio value, in the sale block above and before anything is bought. `main._run_prod` asks for the trims right after `plan_sales`, at the same moment: `seller.plan_trims(..., settings=settings, now=moment, cash_cents=cash_cents, positions_value_cents=checked_value_cents, skip=_plan_tickers(plans))`, sized on the cash and on Kalshi's value of the open positions as kept at the start of the run, never on a pair the take-profit rule picked; `trader.sell_positions` sends the trims after the take-profit sales, in its one call, with the same reduce-only orders. It trims nothing, with one `Not trimming this run: …` WARNING, when a held market could not be identified, when the positions listing was cut short, when the cash on a shard could not be read (`AccountBalance.cash_left_out` names an entry, so `cash_complete` is False: the balance reply's `balance_breakdown` carried an entry with no readable shard or balance, or two entries for one shard, and `auth._balance_cents_by_shard` left that cash out; the WARNING names the first entry and how many more), when Kalshi's value of the open positions could not be read, was refused or is $0, or — read last, once every other gate holds, so a run with rebalancing off or nothing held makes the requests it always made — when an order rests on the account or the account's resting orders could not be read (`scanner.get_resting_orders(client)`, the raw, retried, paged read-only `GET /portfolio/orders` with `status="resting"`: Kalshi's balance is the cash available for trading and its value covers the positions held, so the cash it holds for a resting order, which only a person or another program leaves since the bot's buys are fill-or-kill and its sales immediate-or-cancel, is in neither): a trim grows as the value it is sized on shrinks, so a value that leaves out cash or positions would sell too much (buys are only made smaller by it, and go ahead). An account that holds nothing logs no trim WARNING and reads no fills. `plan_trims` fails closed on checks of its own (its docstring lists them): among them, the fills listing must be readable and reach back past the wait, `config.REBALANCE_MIN_DAYS_SINCE_TRADE` (7) calendar days on the bot's own clock since the pair's last fill, and with `min_price_move` set the pair's price must have moved that far, up or down, since that fill. The size it trims to is the backtest's rule (`config.kelly_trim_count`) over the real book: the count is sized, and the plan priced, only on the bids within `KELLY_TRIM_MAX_BID_DROP` (20c) of each held side's best bid, and on the cash plus the larger of Kalshi's value and what the held markets would fetch at this run's bids (`scanner.held_positions_bid_value`); how live and the backtest differ is listed in the trim gotcha under Known Gotchas. Because the size is the lower of the pair's size cap and Kelly's own share, which reads `k` for a time-series pair, a lower per-trade or same-title cap (through `config.pair_size_cap`) or a higher `k` sells more of the held pairs at the next run while rebalancing is on, whether it comes from a flag, a saved setting or Confirm and trade: `config.rebalance_change_warnings(current, proposed)` says so in one sentence, which `main._log_live_settings` logs as a WARNING (`Live settings: with Rebalance yes, these settings can sell more of the held pairs: …`) on a real-money run whose flags depart from the saved defaults, after the override WARNING and before any trim, and the confirmation page shows in red beside `live_rule_warnings` (the three flags' help says the same). A pair trimmed in part is still held, in a dry run as in a live run: a live run reads its listing back, and a dry run takes out of its held set only what its sales would empty (`main._emptied_tickers`), so its ladder stays held and no new time-series pair is opened on it. It is neither bought nor added to that run (`Held pairs picked for a Kelly trim this run, not added to: N`). **Residual, recorded rather than fixed:** after any sale, `_value_after_sales` refuses Kalshi's value read back when it is not below the value kept at the start. That test spans the whole account, so after a small trim ordinary price moves elsewhere can make it refuse the value; the buys are then sized on the cash alone and nothing is added to (`tests/test_main.py::TestRunProdTrimsLive::test_a_value_that_did_not_fall_after_a_trim_sizes_the_buys_on_cash_alone`). **Residual, recorded rather than fixed:** a `balance_breakdown` that leaves a funded shard out altogether cannot be told from one whose shards are all listed, so the run still trims on the cash listed; the reply's `balance_dollars` is not compared with the shards' sum. **Not verified live:** that `GET /portfolio/orders` still answers (Kalshi's June 2026 deprecation names only the legacy order mutation endpoints) and lists every resting order, the Kalshi UI's included; a read that fails makes every run's trim gate fail closed, its WARNING naming the read, which the first dry run with rebalancing on shows. Pinned by `tests/test_main.py::TestRunProdTrimsInADryRun`, `::TestRunProdTrimsLive`, `::TestRunProdTrimsAfterAPriceMove`, `::TestRunProdTrimsOnAnyDay` and `::TestLiveAndDryRunsAgree::test_a_trimmed_pair_stays_held_and_is_not_added_to_in_both`, and by AST in `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_the_live_run_sells_before_it_buys`.

**First-run order for rebalancing.** Nothing is added to or trimmed until `rebalance` is saved on, or `--rebalance` is given for one run: config.py (`REBALANCE`) and the seed ship it on, but a saved file that leaves the key out reads it as off. Once it is saved on, every live run, the scheduled Monday one included, trims before it buys and may add to the pairs it holds (a dev run holds nothing and does neither), and a lower per-trade or same-title cap, or a higher `k`, then also sells: each exact held pair worth more than its new Kelly size is trimmed at the next run once its wait has passed, so dry-run new settings with `--dry-run` first. The wait and the minimum price movement (`min_price_move`) hold back add-ons and trims alike (`seller.plan_add_ons`, `seller.plan_trims`): a pair is added to or trimmed only once 7 calendar days have passed since the last fill on its markets and, with a minimum, once its price has moved that far since. A file an older version saved is no first run: save its values again as the saved-live-defaults paragraph says (with both old switches on it reads as Rebalance yes, as those runs already added and trimmed), then run (3) as a check; with only one on it reads as Rebalance no, and turning it on starts at (2). **Before turning rebalancing on:** (1) save the live defaults with rebalancing off, since the seed has Rebalance yes: start the defaults server (`./start_dashboard.sh`), open `http://127.0.0.1:8765/confirm?tier_floors=off&spread_min=0.0&spread_max=0.5&k=0.8&size_cap=0.1&same_title_size_cap=0.2&rebalance=off`, review the table, click Confirm and save, and check that `/saved` names that checkout's `live_defaults.json` (the link carries no `source` note because `defaults_server._proposal` refuses the seed's note on any values but the seed's own; the seed caps each trade at 10%; a `main.py` flag still overrides a toggle for one run); from then on every live run, the Monday one included, trades these values and neither adds to nor trims a held pair, and a checkout whose saved file already reads rebalancing as off (one that leaves the key out) starts at (2); (2) you run `python3 -m kalshi_betting.v2_probe --ticker <TICKER> --step yes-close` (about one cent, on a liquid, cheap market the account holds nothing on) and see it PASS, the prerequisite live selling has, since a trim sends the same two order shapes (the reduce-only ask that sells a held YES and the reduce-only YES bid that sells a held NO); (3) run `python3 -m kalshi_betting.main --mode prod --dry-run --rebalance --min-price-move 10` (or without the minimum; no orders, nothing saved), the first time a trading run reads the fills list and the orders resting on the account, and check in `kalshi_arb.log`: the `Sizing on portfolio value $X = cash $Y + open positions $Z` line shows the open positions as a dollar figure (not `not read`), with no `Kalshi's value of the open positions (…) is not used` WARNING after it — either means the run adds to no held pair and trims nothing, and says so (`Not adding to held pairs this run: …`, `Not trimming this run: …`); each `Held pair to add to: …` line's (and each `Held market to add to (its partner has paid out): …` line's, for its one market) `cost $C (fees $F)` against that pair's fills in the Kalshi UI (count × price + fees for the cost, the fees alone for `$F`, both legs together) and its `worth $W at today's prices` against the asks the Kalshi UI shows (count × the ask of the side held, both legs together); each `Add-on check: … -> may add` line (the day the pair last traded and, with a minimum, its price then and now, against the Kalshi UI) and each `Not adding to …: <reason>` line, which holds that pair back this run, then `Held pairs that may be added to this run: N of M`; the `Resting orders on the account: N` line (nothing is trimmed while N is above 0, so cancel a resting order of your own first; a `Not trimming this run: the account's resting orders could not be read, …` WARNING on every run means `GET /portfolio/orders` no longer answers, and trimming stays off); the `Fills read: …` line (it can appear twice, read once for the trims and once for the add-ons; does the list reach back as far as the account's history? it must reach back the 7 days of the wait, or nothing is trimmed or added to;
