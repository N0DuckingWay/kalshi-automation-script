In this file:

- Kelly's denominator is the dollars AT RISK, and the fee is one of them (DR-62).

**Kelly's denominator is the dollars AT RISK, and the fee is one of them (DR-62).** `b = net_spread / (price_a + price_b + fee_per_pair_approx(price_a, price_b))` — the fee sits in the DENOMINATOR as well as the numerator, because fees are cash out the door at execution and the losing cell loses `total_cost_with_fees` in full, exactly as `TradeSpec.min_payoff`'s docstring and the time-series settlement table already said. With the fee-less `(price_a + price_b)` the gate passed whenever `p·net_spread > q·(price_a + price_b)`, while true positive EV needs `p·net_spread > q·(price_a + price_b + fee)`: **the gate overstated EV by exactly `q·fee` on every pair**, so `f* > 0` did NOT mean positive expected value. Reproduced on a time-series pair the scanner admits (pA 0.16, nB 0.21, pB 0.98): `f*` +0.0113 as coded, −0.0099 fee-fair, true EV −0.0060 against a modelled +0.0069. (Every figure in this paragraph was measured with the forecast on the YES-ask gap `pB − pA`, before DR-78 moved it to the mid spread; the arithmetic of `b` did not change. Under the mid-spread forecast both quoted pairs size differently — the headline pair, with the NO ask of 0.85 on A its test gave it, has a mid spread of 0.73 and is accepted at `k` 0.75 (`f*` +0.1010), and the small-`n` pair (NO ask 0.88 on A) sizes 876 — so `TestKellyRiskIncludesFees` pins two complementary pairs that show the same two effects: pA 0.20 / pB 0.30 / nA 0.80 / nB 0.70, accepted by the fee-less denominator (`f*` +0.0141) and rejected fee-fair (−0.0121) at `k` 0.75, and pA 0.31 / pB 0.44 / nA 0.69 / nB 0.56, accepted at n = 31 with an exact-fee expected value of −$0.0025.) **Quote the population figure with the grid it came from — it is strongly grid-dependent** and two independent measurements disagreed by 4x on differently-delimited grids: on a whole-cent grid over the short-tier admissible region (`pA`, `pB`, `nB` each 0.01–0.99, `pB − pA >= 0.15`, `pA + nB <= 0.85`, `net_spread > 0`), 2,748 of the 132,179 specs the OLD gate admitted — 2.1% — had non-positive true EV; at the 0.30 tier, 1,992 of 82,715 (2.4%). Replaying the 22 `Trade computed` lines of the 2026-09-16 22:56:53 prod dry run (sized at `k` 0.75 and the 20% `BUDGET_FRACTION` then shipped) at their logged 2-dp precision: **0 of 22 were EV-negative** (the closest, the FIDE pair, lands at `f*` +0.0011 under the new gate and survives), against an aggregate EV overstatement of $1.56 on a modelled $17.47, spread over the $190.32 of contract cost those 22 computed trades represent (the run then SELECTED 8 of them, $61.68 fee-inclusive — quote whichever denominator you mean, and do not invent a third). That is small next to the operator's own `k` uncertainty (a pooled empirical `k̂` measured against a configured 0.750 — the value then; 0.80 since the 2026-09-27 decision — the figure once quoted here was measured pre-DR-67 on strike-blind, wording-unscreened data and is void; see the DR-67 gotcha's dormancy note); the point is that the gate now means what it says. **Same-title is nearly immune** — `q` is the fixed `1 − SAME_TITLE_CO_RESOLVE_PROB` = 0.05, so `q·fee` is small and the fraction barely moves; a verdict flips on roughly 0.3% of admissible whole-cent price points (measured: 12 of 4,465), always accept → reject — while the time-series bet moves far more, because `q = k·spread` (the mid spread; the YES-ask gap when this was measured) routinely exceeds 0.5 on exactly the wide-gap pairs the strategy targets. **The gate is strictly TIGHTER**: every size is the same or smaller and a small number of marginal time-series pairs are now correctly rejected, so **any backtest baseline taken before this change is not comparable**. The oversizing bias was `q·fee/net_spread` — that is the ABSOLUTE drop in `f*`, not a percentage of anything, and the two readings must not be conflated: across those 22 trades the absolute drop runs 0.009–0.030, which is a 6%–96% RELATIVE cut with a median near 8% (the near-cap wide-gap pairs move least; marginal pairs move most — the FIDE pair loses 96% of its fraction). **`f* > 0` means positive expected value under the CONTINUOUS fee approximation, which is what the gate prices with — not on the spec's own exact-fee fields.** `fee_per_pair_approx` underestimates the ceiling-rounded `fee_leg_exact` that `min_payoff` and `total_cost_with_fees` are built from, so a spec within roughly `q·(exact − approx fee)/net_spread` of the boundary can still be marginally EV-negative at single-digit `n` (measured at a $10,000 balance: pA 0.12 / pB 0.33 / nB 0.70 accepts at n=30 with `p·min_payoff − q·total_cost_with_fees` = −$0.005). `compute_trade`'s `min_payoff > 0` check bounds that small-`n` rounding residual; it does not eliminate it. Do not restate the guarantee without that qualifier — an over-charitable reading of exactly this shape ("a negative result means negative expected value") is what hid DR-62 in the first place. **Kelly's `b` and the REPORTED `profit_ratio` are deliberately DIFFERENT quantities and must not be collapsed back together**: `TradeSpec.profit_ratio` keeps the fee-less denominator as a return-on-contract-cost figure, because it feeds `monthly_profit_ratio` — `select_portfolio`'s ranking key — and the prod Excel log's Profit Ratio column, and changing the ranked value would change which trades are preferred. The Kelly quantity is named `kelly_b` in `strategy._evaluate_size`, `kelly_b_entry` in `backtester._simulate_at_discount`'s Kelly gate (at the top of the book; its `profit_ratio_entry` likewise stays fee-less and still drives `entry_monthly_ratio`, and the count a trade then buys comes from `kelly_b` itself, through `compute_trade`), and the local `b` in `dashboard._kelly_fraction`. **All three carry the same arithmetic and must move together** — the backtester replays the live admission rule, which is why no backtest could ever have surfaced this, and a dashboard left on the old formula would plot a model the live sizer no longer uses. **The AST parity pins do NOT check this arithmetic** (they only pin WHICH helper each sizer calls), so it is pinned by VALUE in `tests/test_strategy.py::TestKellyRiskIncludesFees`. The CAP on the fraction has one definition too, `config.pair_size_cap` — the per-trade cap for every pair, and for a same-title pair `SAME_TITLE_SIZE_CAP` as well — which `strategy._evaluate_size` (with the run's `LiveSettings`) and `backtester._simulate_at_discount` (with its run's cap and its own binding of `SAME_TITLE_SIZE_CAP`) both call, so the live sizer and the backtest cap a pair alike (pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_every_sizer_caps_through_pair_size_cap`); `dashboard._kelly_fraction` plots the UNCAPPED `f*` by design.

**Kelly sizes on portfolio value; only cash is spent (2026-09-29).** Every Kelly fraction on a live run is taken of the account's PORTFOLIO VALUE — the cash on every shard plus what Kalshi says the open positions are worth — and no trade may draw more than the cash on hand: `budget = min(portfolio value × capped fraction, cash)` (`config.kelly_budget`). An add-on to a held pair (DR-77) is sized by the same rule: `config.held_pair_fraction` first lowers its fraction by the pair's stake — what the pair is already worth plus the fees paid for it — so its budget is `min(fraction × portfolio value − the pair's stake, cash)`, never more than a new pair's. While every budget was a fraction of the cash alone, the money held in open positions (7+ time-series pairs since 2026-09-28) fell out of the bankroll and every run sized smaller than Kelly on the account's equity. **The field and its evidence.** `main._run_prod` sizes on one balance read, `auth.read_account_balance` — an `AccountBalance`: `shard_cash_cents`, the per-shard cash dict `verify_auth` returns, and `positions_value_cents`, the reply's top-level `portfolio_value` — and `main._bankroll_cents(cash_cents, positions_value_cents)` adds the two. `portfolio_value` is the open positions' value ALONE, integer cents, every shard (the request names no shard): two live replies of 2026-09-28, with positions held, read `{'balance': 13245, 'balance_dollars': '132.4521', 'portfolio_value': 7850}` and `{'balance': 11615, …, 'portfolio_value': 9527}` — below the cash both times, so it cannot include cash (cash plus it was $211.42 on the second read; see the money-units paragraph for how it is parsed). **The fallback.** A reply with no readable value (`positions_value_cents is None`) sizes on the cash alone, with one WARNING (`Kalshi's balance reply carried no readable portfolio_value — sizing on cash alone ($X) this run, as if no position were held`): smaller, never larger. So does a value the positions-value check refuses (risk (4) below). Every production run logs `Sizing on portfolio value $X = cash $Y + open positions $Z` right after the read — before the gate, so a skipped run shows it too; with no readable value `$Z` reads `not read (counted as $0.00)`, never a measured $0.00 — and the pairs-table banner reads `Kalshi Pair Scan — Portfolio value: $X (cash $Y) | Mode: PROD`. **The gate.** `MIN_BALANCE_CENTS` ($50) reads the portfolio value: below it the run stops with `EXIT_SKIPPED_LOW_BALANCE` (10) and the message `Portfolio value $X (cash $Y) is below minimum $50.00 — skipping run.` (the scheduler's own `balance below minimum` line and the exit codes are unchanged). Cash alone below $50, with the portfolio value at or above it, is a WARNING (`Cash $Y is below the $50.00 minimum but the portfolio value is not: the run goes on, and no trade spends more than the cash left`), logged after the positions-value check so that it describes the value the run sizes on, and the run scans; with cash near 0 enrichment drops every pair of which the cash cannot buy one contract pair (its INFO line says `the $C of cash binds`), and the run ends on its clean exit — 0, or 40 when a held market could not be identified — with that WARNING the line that says why. **The wiring.** Both numbers go to enrichment (`enrich_with_orderbook_prices(client, pairs, portfolio_value_cents, settings=, cash_cents=)`) and to the sizer (`main._compute_trade_specs(pairs, portfolio_value_cents, settings, *, cash_cents)`, whose `cash_cents` is required, → `compute_trade(pair, portfolio_value_cents, settings=, cash_cents=)`) — the same two numbers, or enrichment's depth bound no longer bounds the size — and the cash alone to `select_portfolio(specs, cash_cents, held_ladders=…)`, which spends it in whole cents and shrinks a spec that no longer fits the cash left (the `select_portfolio` paragraph below). `main._display_specs` shows each SELECTED spec in the pairs table, a shrunk one included (the marginal-fill paragraph's display note). `_run_dev` holds nothing, so its virtual `--sandbox-balance` is both numbers. **The single definitions:** `config.kelly_budget` (what one trade may spend), `config.leg_cash_cents` (one leg's dollar cost rounded up to the whole cent: the rounding `select_portfolio` spends with and `trader._required_cents_by_shard` funds each shard with) and `strategy._order_cash_cents` (`TradeSpec.cash_need_cents`, the worst-case reserve the walk takes from the cash: per leg, `n` × the higher of its limit and its fill plus the fee at the price between them nearest 50c, so Kalshi checking cash at the limit rather than the fill still finds it on the account as a whole — not on every shard, see the per-shard residual in the `select_portfolio` paragraph). **Shrink to fit, and why it repeats no Kelly, spread or edge check:** a smaller count on a book enrichment built only lowers both leg prices, so every continuous-fee check the larger size passed (Kelly, the spread, the edge after the fee) still passes; `strategy._spec_at_count` re-checks only what a smaller count can break — that the book holds it, that it is reachable at its own fill-or-kill limits (the supported sizes can have holes), and that a win still pays and the expected value stays positive at the exact fees, whose cent rounding can turn a small shrunk trade EV-negative (see the `select_portfolio` paragraph). **What stays on cash:** the trade log's separator-row balances and its `run_note`, the run result's `balance_before`/`balance_after` (`reporter.RunReport.portfolio_value_before` beside them is the portfolio value read before trading, what Kelly sizes on), `ensure_shard_collateral`'s per-shard plan and the post-trade read (`read_account_balance(client).shard_cash_cents`, summed). Pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_the_live_run_sizes_on_portfolio_value_and_spends_cash` (both run modes hand enrichment and `_compute_trade_specs` the name `portfolio_value_cents` and `cash_cents=cash_cents`, and `select_portfolio` the name `cash_cents`; `_run_prod` reads through `read_account_balance` and `_bankroll_cents`, never `verify_auth`) and `tests/test_main.py::TestSizesOnPortfolioValue` (the gate on the portfolio value, the cash WARNING, the fallback, the sizing line, what each site is handed, a live-shape reply carrying `portfolio_value`, a shrunk spec in the pairs table, and dev) and `::TestPositionsValueCheck` (the positions-value check of risk (4): a value kept and one refused, a cut-short listing (which keeps only a value of 0, `::test_a_cut_short_listing_refuses_any_value_but_zero`), an unreadable count, holding nothing, the minimum applied again, a run stopped by the first minimum reading no positions, and the low-cash WARNING after the check). **Accepted risks, recorded:** (1) FULL DEPLOYMENT — each run aims its fraction at the whole portfolio value however much of it is already committed, so only the cash stops it, and single-bet Kelly on correlated time-series bets overbets; the per-trade cap (10% in the seed) bounds each pair, and a total-exposure cap is not built. (2) A small shrunk time-series trade locks its ladder (DR-76) until it pays out, where skipping it would have let a later run retry at full size; no minimum size is enforced (the operator chose shrink-to-fit). (3) `portfolio_value` is Kalshi's mark (it tracked cost right after the 2026-09-28 fills), includes any position opened by hand, and could briefly count a payout twice if it lags the cash credit — which is why the sizing line logs all three numbers every run. (4) A `portfolio_value` in the wrong unit — Kalshi re-scaling the field in place, say to centicents, would read 100× too large. `auth._positions_value_cents` bounds only its type, sign and size, and the balance reply does not list the positions, so the value is checked once `_run_prod` has read the positions listing (`get_held_positions`, each held market's signed count), which comes after the gate: `main._checked_positions_value` always keeps a value of 0, even from a listing cut short or with a count it cannot read (it adds nothing, so the run sizes on the cash either way), and keeps a value above 0 only when it is at most `config.CONTRACT_PAYOUT_DOLLARS` ($1, what a contract pays if it wins) × the contracts held (every held market's count, YES or NO), over a listing read to its end (`complete_out`) whose every count can be read — so, holding nothing, any value above 0 is refused; an unread value is left to the fallback's own WARNING. A refused value is one WARNING (`Kalshi's value of the open positions ($V) is not used: <reason> — sizing on cash alone ($C) this run, as if no position were held`, where `<reason>` is `the list of the account's positions was cut short, so not every contract held is known`, `the contract count of N held market(s) could not be read` or `that is more than the N contract(s) held can be worth at $1.00 each`); the run then sizes on the cash alone, records the cash as `RunReport.portfolio_value_before`, applies the $50 minimum to the cash again with the gate's own message (exit 10 below it), and adds to no held pair (DR-77). Unchecked, an inflated value would make `config.kelly_budget` return the whole cash for nearly every spec, so the first-ranked trade could take nearly all of it and the per-trade cap would stop bounding one pair. What still passes is a value too large by less than the bound allows ($1 a contract leaves room when the contracts were bought cheaply); the loss is bounded by the cash, and the sizing line, logged before the check, prints the value as read. **The backtest mirrors this rule, valuing open trades at market (user decision, 2026-09-30; at cost before).** `backtester._simulate_at_discount`'s Pass 2 sizes every candidate of one Monday on that checkpoint's opening portfolio value — the cash after the day's pay-outs plus every trade still open at its value at that checkpoint (`backtester._open_value`: each leg at the latest usable ask of the side it holds on a candle at or before the checkpoint; at its payout once its market has paid out; at the price it paid before its side has had a usable ask; and the whole trade at its cost when it has no quotes, as every hand-built trade has none), the valuation the equity curve uses (see the market-valuation paragraph after DR-61's), taken at the checkpoint's first candidate — and on the cash left, both in whole cents, through the live sizing code (`_size_trade` → `strategy.compute_trade`, so `config.kelly_budget(value, capped fraction, cash)` inside it; over a walked synthetic book when there is one, see the walked-book gotcha), so a candidate the cash left cannot buy in full is shrunk to what it can buy (`compute_trade`'s own shrink), and skipped when not one contract pair fits after the exact fees, or when the exact fees leave no profit in a win at the count that fits — a time-series pair is then tried again on its next passing Monday (DR-76). `BacktestTrade.balance_at_entry` records that value (taken at the checkpoint, so once a trade has quotes it matches no day-end row of the curve exactly), so `dashboard._kelly_points` still plots the fraction of the base Kelly sized against. What a trade spends is its spec's `total_cost_with_fees`, which `compute_trade` fitted to a budget of whole cents of the cash left; the guard beside it compares in whole cents too (`invested > _cents(cash) / 100.0`), so it is defence only: a trade sized to exactly the cash left is made, as live makes it. **The two paths value open positions differently, in no fixed direction:** live reads Kalshi's mark, while the backtest values each open leg at the ask of the side it holds (the price live pays to add to a held pair), from its hourly candles; the two can differ by the spread and by when each was read. A held pair's stake differs between the paths too, and both count the fees paid for the pair: live, `HeldPair.stake_dollars`, its contracts at the ask of the side held on each market (`HeldPair.value_dollars`; a market with no usable ask at its exposure) plus both legs' fees (`HeldPair.fees_dollars`); in the backtest, its open trades at market plus their fees (for each open trade of the pair, what it spent plus its change in value since: `paid + (_open_value(t, d) − t.total_cost)`). Both portfolio values leave those fees out, since they are spent, so the check leans toward adding less: at the prices it was bought at, a pair bought at its full Kelly share adds nothing. Three smaller differences: the backtest spends a trade's fee-inclusive cost at the prices it walked (its spec's `total_cost_with_fees`), with no reserve for the orders' limit prices (`cash_need_cents`, which only `select_portfolio` spends); it sizes each candidate in rank order on the cash left, through `compute_trade` alone, where live sizes every candidate on the whole cash and `select_portfolio` then shrinks one that no longer fits (`_shrink_to_cash`, which also requires a positive expected value at the exact fees); and it ranks candidates by their top-of-book `entry_monthly_ratio`, where live ranks by the spec's `monthly_profit_ratio` at the sized prices. The backtest's running cash is a float built by repeated subtraction; the sizer and the guard read it in whole cents (`_cents`: rounded to 6 decimals, then down, so float noise never costs a cent), as a live run's cash arrives. The cap reaches sizing through the live sizer's own cap (`pair_size_cap` on the simulation's settings; `kelly_f_capped` mirrors it at the top of the book, for the add-on check alone) and, over a walked book, through the cap on how deep enrichment averages a book, so `CapSweep`'s reuse rule is that every cap at or above a point's `max(peak_kelly_fraction, cap_free_from)` sizes identically: the value, the cash, the tickers, the ladders and, with `add_to_held`, each open pair's stake are walk state that caps at or above that floor share. Pinned by `tests/test_backtester.py::TestSizesOnPortfolioValue` (an open trade raises the next Monday's size and `balance_at_entry` to the equity curve's value; the cash still caps it; after the pay-out the value is the cash again, and a trade paying out on the checkpoint Monday itself counts once, as cash, never as its receipt and its cost too; each against live `compute_trade` on the same value and cash), `::TestSizesAtMarket` (a NO ask that moves before the second Monday's checkpoint sizes the second trade on the new value, equal to live `compute_trade` on it; a leg paid out mid-trade; a dead quote at its entry price; a trade paying out on the Monday counted once), `::TestKellyShrinkParity::test_with_an_open_trade_n_matches_compute_trade_on_value_and_cash`, `::TestCheckpointOpeningBalanceSizing::test_greedy_fit_shrinks_to_the_cash_left` (766 then 511 contract pairs on $1,000), `::TestOpenLadderExposure::test_a_time_series_pair_short_of_cash_is_tried_again_and_a_same_title_one_is_not` (now on a $1.20 balance, where the cash left buys no contract pair; its comment derives the range, $1.13 to just under $1.28) and the AST pin `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_every_sizer_budgets_through_kelly_budget` (`_simulate_at_discount` sizes through `_size_trade` and names no budget rule of its own: the budget is `compute_trade`'s). **Every backtest result recorded before this change sized each Monday on the checkpoint's opening CASH and skipped a trade the running cash could not afford whole, so none is comparable** — trade sizes, which trades find cash and on which Monday, returns and drawdowns all move. Nor is one recorded before 2026-09-30, which valued open trades at cost.

**Budget fitting in `compute_trade()`.** After Kelly sizing, `n` is shrunk until the fee-inclusive total cost fits within the Kelly-capped budget — `config.kelly_budget(portfolio value, capped fraction, cash)`: the fraction of the portfolio value, never more than the cash on hand (`compute_trade(pair, portfolio_value_cents, *, settings=None, cash_cents=None)`; with `cash_cents=None` the value is all cash and the budget is exactly the float `(portfolio_value_cents / 100.0) * fraction` it always was, so every field a spec had before the cash existed is unchanged — `cash_need_cents` and `interval_discount` are new — checked against the code before the cash was added by two scratch-harness runs over different random populations, neither harness kept, each finding every old field identical: 5,807 specs from 8,000 random pairs over four grids, both pair types, booked and bookless (the run that commit's message quotes), and 6,365 specs from 7,520 random booked and bookless pairs across four settings). Don't remove the shrink loop — sizing on price alone systematically overshoots the per-trade cap (`BUDGET_FRACTION`, or the tighter same-title cap). If the shrink moved `n` and the pair carries a book, the leg prices are then re-derived at the new `n`, the reachability backstop runs (see the TS-08 paragraph below), and the shrink runs again at the new prices, repeating until `n` stops moving (`n` only falls, so it ends). **The repeat is required:** a smaller `n` reaches fewer, cheaper levels, so the contract cost falls, but a leg priced above 0.5 that gets cheaper has a larger `p(1 − p)`, so its ceiling-rounded exact fee (`config.fee_leg_exact`) can rise a cent, and the re-priced cost can overrun the budget the shrink had just met. Until 2026-09-30 there was one pass, and the returned `total_cost_with_fees` could exceed the budget (then `balance × kelly_fraction`) by a cent or two: on a reviewer's same-title book (NO 0.64 / 0.646 / 0.653 against YES 0.11 / 0.11 / 0.106, 31 / 25 / 32 contracts) at 4,082 cents with no caps, the shrink stopped at 41 costing $31.7644 at the solved (42-contract) prices against a $31.768 budget, and the re-price at 41 (NO leg 0.641463, its fee $0.66 → $0.67) cost $31.77; it now sizes 40 at $30.984. The repeat moves a live size only where the single pass overran, and there by one contract in every case measured (668,800 synthetic book × balance cases with a leg above 0.5 on a ladder: 5 overran by at most 0.63 cents, each now one contract smaller, and no other size moved). Pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestRepricedCostFitsTheBudget` and, for the pass after the backstop snaps, `::TestReachableDepthSizing::test_the_budget_is_checked_again_after_the_backstop_snaps`. `compute_trade` fits each trade to its own budget; `select_portfolio` is what makes the whole portfolio fit the cash, at what its orders can draw at their limit prices (next paragraph). `profit_ratio` and `kelly_fraction` are deliberately left at their pre-shrink values (`p` reads the mid spread and is the same at every size), since they are reporting/ranking figures and recomputing them would change a spec's rank for no gain.

**`select_portfolio` spends the cash in whole cents, and shrinks a trade that no longer fits.** `select_portfolio(specs, cash_cents, *, held_ladders=frozenset())` walks the ranked specs holding the cash left as whole cents and takes from it each spec's `TradeSpec.cash_need_cents` — what its two fill-or-kill orders can draw at their limit prices (`strategy._order_cash_cents`: per leg, `n` × the higher of the leg's limit, `scanner.v2_effective_cap`, and its fill, plus the fee at the price between the two nearest 50c, rounded up by `config.leg_cash_cents`; `compute_trade` sets it on every spec). Per leg that is never below `leg_cash_cents` of the leg's fee-inclusive cost at its fill, which is exactly what `trader._required_cents_by_shard` asks each shard to hold, so `sum(trader._required_cents_by_shard(selected).values()) <= cash_cents` always: the cash a portfolio the walk admitted always covers every shard's funding together, so `ensure_shard_collateral` is never short of cash to move (pinned over random portfolios, books, grids and shards by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestSelectPortfolioShrinksToCash::test_the_shard_funder_never_finds_the_portfolio_short`). **The headroom above the fill is held on the total only, not per shard** — a known residual: `_required_cents_by_shard` asks each shard for its legs' cost at the FILL prices and no more, and `_plan_transfers` measures every shard against that figure, so it tops a short shard up to that cost, can draw a SOURCE shard down to that cost (its surplus is its balance minus its own legs' fill cost), and leaves alone a shard holding between the fill cost and the reserve. Any shard can therefore end up below its legs' reserve, not only one a transfer tops up: on hand-built specs with shards `{0: 5000, 1: 0, 2: 5000}` cents and legs on shard 0 (fill 3,800, reserve 4,100), shard 2 (3,800 / 4,100) and shard 1 (1,600 / 1,800), the walk admits all three (the reserves sum to the cash) and the plan `[(0, 1, 1200), (2, 1, 400)]` leaves shard 0 at 3,800 and shard 1 at 1,600, both below their reserves (the final review's harness, 2026-09-29). When the account's cash sits on one shard (the final review read it all on shard 0, `{0: 11615, 1: 0, 2: 0, 3: 0}` cents), then whenever the walk spends nearly all of it — the normal state once cash binds — and any transfer runs, that shard's own legs lose their headroom too. If Kalshi holds cash at the limit rather than at the fill, a YES leg on such a shard can be refused for want of that headroom after its NO leg filled, and the NO leg is then unwound. Funding each shard at its legs' reserve would need per-leg needs on `TradeSpec` (`_order_cash_cents` already computes them leg by leg) summed per shard by `_required_cents_by_shard`; the plan would stay feasible, since the walk already keeps the reserves' total within the cash. It is not built: it changes what `ensure_shard_collateral` asks of every shard, which this change deliberately left alone, and it would drop a trade on a shard whose transfers are off when that shard holds its legs' fill cost but not their reserve — while whether Kalshi holds cash at the limit at all is unverified. A spec whose need exceeds the cash left is shrunk (`strategy._shrink_to_cash`) to the LARGEST smaller count whose own spec fits: it scans down from the most the cash could buy at the book's cheapest level and verifies every candidate through `strategy._spec_at_count` — the book holds `n`, `n` is reachable at its own FoK limits (`_reachable_contracts`), a win still pays after the exact fees (more than `PRICE_EPSILON`, since a payoff that is exactly zero can evaluate a hair above it), the trade's expected value at the exact fees is still positive under the model's own `p` (`p × min_payoff > (1 − p) × total_cost_with_fees`, through `_kelly_p_at` at the spec's `interval_discount`, the `k` `compute_trade` records on every time-series spec; `p` reads the mid spread, so it is the same at every size), and the need fits — because the supported sizes can have holes and a step down at one fixed price stops at the first and under-sizes (on NO 0.51×50 / 0.56×600 behind a flat YES 0.29, counts 51–125 cannot be filled by their own orders; 11,420 cents takes 128, 11,241 takes 50 — `::test_a_booked_spec_comes_back_at_the_largest_count_that_fits`). `_spec_at_count` recomputes every `n`-dependent figure as `compute_trade` does and re-prices the pair through `strategy._priced_pair` (`compute_trade`'s own write-back, moved into a helper), and repeats no Kelly, spread or edge check: on a book enrichment built both leg prices only fall as `n` falls, and a lower price never undoes a check the larger size passed (`p` is the same at every size and `b` rises, so `f*` rises for either pair type; the spread only widens and stays under a ceiling tested at the book's top; every qualifying level keeps an edge after the fee — its docstring gives the argument). That argument holds for the CONTINUOUS fee Kelly prices with, not for the exact fees, each rounded up to the cent, which exceed it by up to a cent per leg — the DR-62 residual, which at the small counts a shrink routinely lands on can turn a trade the model rates positive into one it rates negative (measured by C2's adversary critic: of 82,989 shrinks of EV-positive single-level specs to 1–3 pairs, 2,180 were EV-negative at the exact fees, e.g. pA 0.21 / nB 0.45 / pB 0.59 at `k` 0.80, which at one pair pays $0.30 and risks $0.70 at `p` 0.696) — so the exact-fee expected value is checked there. A time-series spec with no `interval_discount` (built by hand) is never shrunk. A shrunk spec therefore meets a stricter test than `compute_trade`'s own, which holds a spec only to `min_payoff > 0`. `kelly_p`, `kelly_fraction`, `profit_ratio`, `monthly_profit_ratio` and `days_to_close` keep the spec's values (ranking/reporting, as after `compute_trade`'s own shrink), so a shrunk spec keeps its rank. A spec is skipped only when not one contract pair fits — a spec of one contract pair has nothing smaller and reads no price — a shrunk spec claims its tickers and ladders like any pick, each shrink is one INFO line (`Shrunk '<title>' from X to N contract pairs to fit the $C of cash left`) plus a silent-at-zero count (`Trades shrunk to fit the cash left: N`), and the `Portfolio:` summary names, beside the fee-inclusive cost at the fills, the cash the walk set aside. A spec with no `cash_need_cents` (built by hand) spends its fee-inclusive cost rounded up to the cent, whole and leg by leg, whichever is more (`strategy._cash_need`). Consequences to know: a shrunk time-series trade locks its ladder (DR-76) until it pays out, at whatever size it was shrunk to; the backtester's `_simulate_at_discount` sizes each candidate through `compute_trade` on the running cash, so a candidate the cash left cannot buy in full is sized to what it can buy (the portfolio-value paragraph above says how the two paths still differ); and `main`'s pairs table shows a shrunk trade at the size that trades (`main._display_specs`; see the marginal-fill paragraph's display note). A shrunk add-on (DR-77) is still an add-on: `_spec_at_count` copies the pair with its `HeldPair`, so it keeps its "adds to N held" marker and the trader's send check (which compares the held counts, never the new one), and it claims its tickers and ladders like any pick (an add-on itself is blocked only by this run's earlier picks — see the DR-76 paragraph); pinned by `tests/test_strategy.py::TestSelectPortfolioAddOns::test_a_shrunk_add_on_keeps_its_held_pair_and_passes_the_send_check` and `tests/test_main.py::TestAddOnMarkers::test_a_shrunk_add_on_shows_its_own_count_and_the_held_count`.

**Marginal fill pricing — every stage prices the quantity it is actually reasoning about.** Enrichment used to collapse the book to two scalars: a quantity-weighted average over the ENTIRE qualifying book. One pair is bounded — by `BUDGET_FRACTION` of the balance until 2026-09-27, by `config.max_kelly_fraction` (0.20 at the shipped defaults) since — and Kelly usually sizes well under that, so on a liquid market that average covered depth no single trade could reach — it inflated the fill price, and both the profitability gate in `scanner._enrich_pair` (which `enrich_with_orderbook_prices` calls for each tradeable pair whose two books it read) and the Kelly gate in `compute_trade` then killed pairs with a genuine edge in their top few levels. Two halves now fix it, and neither may be removed without the other:
- **`scanner.enrich_with_orderbook_prices(client, pairs, portfolio_value_cents, *, settings=None, cash_cents=None)`** fetches each book once (a cache by ticker) and hands each tradeable pair whose two books it read, with those books, to `scanner._enrich_pair(pair, ob_a, ob_b, portfolio_value_cents, *, settings, cash_cents, log=)`, which needs no client and does everything that follows: it bounds its average at `config.max_affordable_pairs(portfolio_value_cents, best level's price sum, config.max_kelly_fraction(pair type, settings), cash_cents=cash_cents)` — the largest capped Kelly fraction the sizer can return for that pair type, of the portfolio value and never more than the cash, over the cheapest possible prefix sum, hence an upper bound on any `n` the sizer can return, so the price written here is never optimistic relative to the one finally traded. That holds because the sizer prices with the same `k` and caps and is handed the same portfolio value and cash (`min(value × f, cash)` only falls with `f`); its INFO line for a pair the budget cannot afford one contract pair of adds `; the $C of cash binds` when the cash, not the share of the value, set the budget: each run mode hands enrichment and `strategy.compute_trade` (through `main._compute_trade_specs`, whose `settings` is required) the run's ONE `LiveSettings`, so the bound and the size read one `k` and one set of caps; a caller that handed the two different settings would break the bound, which is why every live call passes the run's own explicitly. `max_kelly_fraction` is `min(cap, round(1 − k, 12))` for time-series — `f* < 1 − k` on every pair enrichment keeps, which its fail-closed fallback and its crossed-book guards on both books make true (see the directional-filter paragraph) — and `min(cap, SAME_TITLE_CO_RESOLVE_PROB)` for same-title, since `f* = (p − c)/(1 − c) < p`, where `cap` is `config.pair_size_cap(pair type, size_cap, same_title_size_cap)` — the very cap the sizer applies (`size_cap` for time-series, `min(size_cap, same_title_size_cap)` for same-title); both are exactly `0.20` at the shipped toggles — `round(1 − 0.80, 12)` for time-series, whose per-trade cap is lifted since the 2026-09-27 decision, and `SAME_TITLE_SIZE_CAP` for same-title — as they were at the `BUDGET_FRACTION` of `0.20` shipped before it, so the lifted cap never averages depth no trade can use (#51 again). The `round` is deliberate and pinned with `==`: `1 − 0.8` is `0.19999999999999996`, which shifts `max_contracts` by one on round-number books; a bound of 0 (time-series at `k = 1`) drops the pair with its own INFO line naming it. **Before bounding, enrichment cuts the qualifying levels at the first one with no edge left after the fee** (`1 − yes − no > fee_per_pair_approx(yes, no)`): the edge after fee strictly decreases in both leg prices and the levels ascend, so what remains is a contiguous prefix and FoK prefix pricing stays valid. The cut changes nothing while the price-sum ceiling leaves more edge than any fee (`1 − max_sum ≥ 0.05 > 0.035`, the largest `fee_per_pair_approx`) — every same-title pair and every time-series pair whose floor is at least 0.035; at a floor of 0 (tier floors off, as shipped since the 2026-09-27 decision, so the cut is live on every time-series pair) no-edge levels would otherwise pass the 1.0 ceiling and drag the prefix average under the profitability line, dropping a pair a Kelly-sized trade profits on (#51 again — pinned on an adversary's book by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestEnrichmentSpreadRule::test_levels_are_cut_at_the_first_with_no_edge_after_the_fee`). A book with no level left is dropped with its own INFO line (`No contract pair for '…' keeps an edge after the fee`). The cut is `scanner._levels_with_edge_after_fee`, which `validate_pair_price` applies again to the freshly fetched books (see the directional-filter paragraph). `portfolio_value_cents` is REQUIRED, not defaulted: a default would silently restore whole-book pricing on a real-money path with no signal. It also stores the qualifying levels on the pair as `CandidatePair.depth_levels` — `(price_a, price_b, qty)` triples in MARKET order, ascending by combined price, oriented once via `leg_sides` so readers need no pair-type logic — and sets `max_contracts` to that bounded count, i.e. **how many contracts the written prices are valid for**. `_enrich_pair` returns the re-priced pair and why it refused it, the first check that failed: `ENRICH_NO_EDGE_AFTER_FEE`, `ENRICH_NO_QUALIFYING`, `ENRICH_UNAFFORDABLE` (the budget cannot buy one contract pair), `ENRICH_THIN_BOOK` (it can, but the book holds under one contract at the gap), `ENRICH_NO_REFERENCE`, `ENRICH_CROSSED`, `ENRICH_UNPROFITABLE`, or config's `SPREAD_*` code for a refused spread; `None` when tradeable. Its keyword-only `log` takes its own lines (`logging.log` by default); the book reader's WARNING about unusable bid levels still goes to `logging`. The wrapper keeps the book cache, the missing-book branch and the closing "Orderbook depth check" line (`tests/test_scanner.py::TestEnrichPairMatchesTheOldLoop` runs a single-function copy of enrichment, kept in the test, beside the live one on every refusal path and requires the same pairs, log lines and book fetches; `tests/test_strategy.py::TestTimeSeriesKellyParity::test_ast_enrichment_prices_each_pair_through_enrich_pair` pins that the wrapper prices nothing itself).
- **`strategy.compute_trade`** then solves size and price together over those levels. `_evaluate_size(pair, levels, n, portfolio_value_cents, settings, *, cash_cents=None, refusal_out=None)` prices `n` contracts via `scanner.prefix_fill_prices` and runs EVERY gate at that price (`p` at `settings.interval_discount`, through `_kelly_p_at`, whose `k` is required — for a time-series pair it reads the mid spread, so it is the same at every `n` and only `b` moves with the price; the fraction capped at `config.pair_size_cap(pair_type, settings.size_cap, settings.same_title_size_cap)` and, for an add-on, lowered by `config.held_pair_fraction`, with `refusal_out` saying when that refused the size); `_solve_marginal_size` binary-searches `[1, max_contracts]` for the largest `n` that is *supported* (gates pass at the price of `n`, and Kelly at that price still affords at least `n`). The solved prices are written back onto a `dc_replace` copy of the pair through the same `leg_sides` mapping, so `leg_prices(spec.pair)` — the documented single source of truth for what a leg costs — hands the marginal price to `trader._ordered_legs`, `_v2_limit_price`, `_rollback_floor_cents` and the prod trade log with no changes of their own.

Four things are load-bearing. (1) **It is a SEARCH, not a descent from the top,** because the gates bite hardest at the top: a pair can have a comfortably positive Kelly fraction at a realistic size and a NEGATIVE one at full book depth (the "a wide book drives Kelly negative and the pair is skipped" case), so evaluating only the seed and aborting throws away exactly the pairs this mechanism exists to rescue. (2) **Safety rests on verification, not on monotonicity.** The supported set is NOT downward-closed, and this is measured rather than suspected. The chance of profit `p` reads the mid spread and is the same at every size (DR-78), so `f*` alone only falls as `n` rises (`b` falls as the fills rise) — it moved both ways while `p` was re-priced at each size's YES fill, before DR-78 — but once the reachability gate is included the set can have an outright HOLE — on a NO ladder of 0.30@1000 then 0.33@600 it is `[1, 1000]` and `[1501, 1600]` with `[1001, 1500]` unsupported between them. The guarantee is instead that a count is only ever returned after being DIRECTLY verified, so a search that lands low under-sizes rather than mis-prices. One consequence is worth knowing before anyone re-tunes the search: a bisection converges to the top of the LOWEST island and cannot land above a hole, so it UNDER-sizes on such a book rather than mis-pricing — which is also why `compute_trade`'s post-shrink reachability snap has never been observed to fire (118,726 randomized two-level ladders x balances found no case). Keep the snap: that behaviour is an accident of bisection, not a guarantee, and the invariant is what the money depends on. `SIZE_SOLVE_MAX_ITERATIONS` (64) is pure paranoia: a bisection of `[1, max_contracts]` closes in ~log2 passes (~20 for a million-contract book), and on exhaustion the already-verified best is returned with a WARNING. (3) **`depth_levels` is read by TYPE (`strategy._depth_levels`), never truthiness** — a `MagicMock` standing in for a pair answers with a truthy auto-attribute that iterates into nonsense, and a pair whose book we cannot read must be priced on its scalars rather than a guess; same fail-safe rule `leg_sides` applies to an unknown `pair_type`. A pair with no book takes the single-shot path **unchanged**, which is what keeps `tests/test_backtester.py::TestKellyShrinkParity` (which builds a bare pair on purpose) meaningful. (4) **`max_contracts == 0` now means only "not enriched".** Enrichment never writes 0 — a book holding under one contract, or a budget (or a per-trade bound of 0) that cannot afford one, drops the pair with its own INFO line instead, closing the sub-one-contract hole that overloaded sentinel used to have.

`main._compute_trade_specs` still keys `{id(candidate_pair): spec}`, but `display_specs` must be built FROM that dict (its keys, each mapped to the spec `select_portfolio` selected for that candidate — `main._display_specs`), never as `{id(s.pair): s for s in portfolio}` — `compute_trade` returns a re-priced COPY of the pair, so `id(spec.pair)` matches no candidate and every selected row silently renders `—` in the pairs table while the trade itself executes normally. `select_portfolio` returns a spec it shrank to fit the cash as a NEW object, so the selected specs are matched back to their candidates by the pair's two tickers (`main._display_specs`; unique among the candidates, since each finder keeps one best pair per group and a market sits in one group, and `_dedup_pairs` drops a time-series pair whose tickers a same-title pair already has; unique within a portfolio, since `select_portfolio` never reuses a ticker), never by identity: a filter on `id(s) in chosen` over the specs `compute_trade` returned would miss the shrunk spec and render its row `—` while the trade executed at its shrunk size (pinned by `tests/test_main.py::TestSizesOnPortfolioValue::test_a_shrunk_spec_shows_in_the_pairs_table`). `print_pairs_table` reads its price columns from `spec.pair` when a spec exists, so the prices and the trade on a row describe the same thing. **Measured on live sandbox books (2026-09-13, same command before and after): 12 of 37 pairs priced cheaper, 25 identical, 0 dearer, improvements from 0.03c to 1.99c per pair.**


**The sized count must be depth the FoK can REACH, not depth that merely clears the gap (TS-08).** A V2 taker order is ONE fill-or-kill limit per leg, so it buys only the depth resting at or below its own limit — and that limit is a PER-CONTRACT cap derived from the leg price (`scanner.v2_limit_price`), while the leg price is the quantity-weighted average of a MULTI-LEVEL prefix. An average over a ladder sits BELOW that ladder's top level, so the top of the very prefix being priced can rest above the cap it produces and the order is killed, reported as `status="failed"` / "NO leg FoK not filled" — indistinguishable from a genuine price move. Measured post-#51 on a same-title pair with NO asks 0.32@300 / 0.37@300 against a flat YES 0.30@600: the sizer returned n=600 at a NO leg price of 0.3450, whose cap is 0.3600, which reaches only 300. Put the ladder on the YES leg instead and it is worse than a skipped trade — the NO leg is submitted first and FILLS, then the YES leg is killed and the pair unwinds.

`strategy._reachable_contracts(pair, levels, price_a, price_b)` is the single definition of "how many contract pairs this order can actually buy": it derives each leg's cap through `scanner.v2_effective_cap` — the SAME arithmetic the order body carries, including the NO leg's complement round-trip, which can TIGHTEN the effective NO cap on a sub-cent grid — and sums the depth at or below both. **Never re-derive the cap anywhere else**; the whole finding is that the size and the cap disagreed, and a second copy of the formula guarantees a second disagreement. That is also why the arithmetic LIVES in `scanner.py` (`ceil_to_tick`, `v2_limit_price`, `v2_effective_cap`) and is only re-exported by `trader.py` under its historical private names: `trader` imports both `scanner` and `strategy`, so neither may import `trader`.

It is applied in three places. (1) `strategy._evaluate_size` rejects any `n` whose own price cannot reach `n` contracts, which makes `_solve_marginal_size`'s existing bisection converge on the largest self-consistent size — no separate fixed-point loop, and the empty case needs no `tradeable=False` writeback because an unreachable `n` is simply never selected. (2) `compute_trade`'s post-shrink snap is a BACKSTOP: the fee shrink decrements `n` outside `_evaluate_size`, and reachability is genuinely not downward-closed (on NO 0.30@1000 / 0.33@600 the supported set is [1,1000] and [1501,1600] with a hole between). It has never been observed to fire, because `_solve_marginal_size` bisects and therefore converges to the top of the LOWER island and cannot land above the hole — but that is an accident of the current solver, not a guarantee, so the invariant is enforced rather than assumed. Do not delete it as dead code. The fee shrink runs again after it, at the snapped count's own prices (see Budget fitting above). (3) `validate_pair_price` counts reachable depth against `spec.x` using the caps derived from `leg_prices(spec.pair)` — the price the trader is ABOUT TO SUBMIT, not a freshly recomputed average — because the question that matters pre-execution is whether the order we are about to send will fill. Its WARNING says "reachable at the FoK limit", so the first live reachability rejection is not misdiagnosed as thin depth (the line is pinned word for word by `tests/test_scanner.py::TestValidatePairPriceReachableDepth::test_the_rejection_line_is_pinned_word_for_word`).

**Enrichment is NOT involved.** `max_contracts` keeps the meaning #51 gave it — the affordability-bounded count the written prices are valid for, and the upper bound of the search — and nothing overwrites it with a reachability count.
